# Appendix A. Proofs {-}

**Proof of Theorem 1.** Condition on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$, which fixes the score $s$ and the strata. Let $E_k=\{Y_{n+1}\in B_k\}$ and $J=\{i\le n+1: Y_i\in B_k\}$, so that $|J|=n_k+1$ on $E_k$. Membership of a point in $B_k$ is a function of that point alone, so any permutation of $\{1,\ldots,n+1\}$ that maps $J$ onto itself leaves the event $\{J=j,\ n+1\in J\}$ unchanged; by exchangeability, conditionally on $J=j\ni n+1$ and on the multiset $\{(X_i,Y_i):i\in j\}$, the index $n+1$ is uniformly distributed over $j$. Let $R$ be the rank of $S_{n+1}=s(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})$ among $\{S_i\}_{i\in J}$, with ties broken uniformly at random; conditionally as above, $R$ is uniform on $\{1,\ldots,n_k+1\}$. By (3)–(4), $Y_{n+1}\in\hat C(X_{n+1})$ iff $S_{n+1}\le\hat q_k$, which holds whenever $R\le r_k=\lceil(n_k+1)(1-\alpha_k)\rceil$ (if $r_k>n_k$, then $\hat q_k=+\infty$ and coverage is one). Hence the conditional coverage is at least $r_k/(n_k+1)\ge 1-\alpha_k$. Averaging over $J$ and the multiset gives (5); since the argument conditions on $J$, the level $\alpha_k$ may depend on $n_k$. If scores are almost surely distinct, coverage is exactly $\min\{r_k,n_k+1\}/(n_k+1)<1-\alpha_k+1/(n_k+1)$ given $n_k$. The marginal statement follows from the law of total probability, $\sum_kp_k(1-\alpha_k)=1-\sum_kp_k\alpha_k$. Finally, the conditioning on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ allows any score and strata that are measurable functions of $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ alone. $\square$

**Proof of Corollary 1.** Given $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$ the set $A=\{(x,y):y\in\hat C(x)\}$ is fixed. Because the test point is independent of the calibration data, $Q\{Y\in\hat C(X)\mid Y\in B_k\}=\mathbb{E}\,Q_k(A)$ and the left-hand side of (5) equals $\mathbb{E}\,P_k(A)$, where $\mathbb{E}$ averages over $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}\sim P^n$ (i.i.d. data are exchangeable, so (5) applies). (i) If $Q_k=P_k$ the two expectations coincide, and the law of total probability gives the marginal statement. (ii) $|Q_k(A)-P_k(A)|\le d_{\mathrm{TV}}(Q_k,P_k)$ for every fixed $A$; average and use (5). (iii) Under label shift the likelihood ratio of $Q_k$ to $P_k$ is $\lambda_k(y)=w(y)/\bar w_k$ with $\bar w_k=\pi_k/p_k=\mathbb{E}_{P_k}w(Y)\ge m_k$. Hence $Q_k(A^c)=\mathbb{E}_{P_k}\{\lambda_k(Y)\mathbf{1}_{A^c}(X,Y)\}\le(M_k/\bar w_k)P_k(A^c)\le(M_k/m_k)P_k(A^c)$; averaging over $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$ and using (5) gives the multiplicative bound. For the total-variation bound, $\lambda_k\in[a,b]$ with $a=m_k/\bar w_k\le1\le b=M_k/\bar w_k$ and $\mathbb{E}_{P_k}\lambda_k=1$; since $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(Q_k,P_k)=\mathbb{E}_{P_k}(\lambda_k-1)_+$ and, by convexity, $(\lambda-1)_+\le(b-1)(\lambda-a)/(b-a)$ on $[a,b]$, we obtain $d_{\mathrm{TV}}\le(b-1)(1-a)/(b-a)=(M_k-\bar w_k)(\bar w_k-m_k)/\{\bar w_k(M_k-m_k)\}$. The right-hand side is maximised at $\bar w_k=\sqrt{m_kM_k}$, where it equals $(\sqrt{M_k}-\sqrt{m_k})/(\sqrt{M_k}+\sqrt{m_k})=\tanh\{\log(M_k/m_k)/4\}$. $\square$

**Proof of Proposition 2.** Existence: $G_k(t)=P\{f(Y\mid X)\ge t\mid Y\in B_k\}$ is continuous and non-increasing with $G_k(0^+)=1$ and $G_k(t)\to0$ as $t\to\infty$, so the intermediate value theorem gives $t_k>0$ with $G_k(t_k)=1-\alpha_k$. Optimality: write $A=\{(x,y):y\in C(x)\}$, $A^\star$ likewise, $A_k=A\cap(\mathcal{X}\times B_k)$, $A_k^\star=A^\star\cap(\mathcal{X}\times B_k)$ and $|A|_P=\iint\mathbf{1}_A(x,y)\,dy\,P_X(dx)$, so that $\mathbb{E}|C(X)\cap B_k|=|A_k|_P$ and $p_k\mathrm{Cov}_k(C)=\iint\mathbf{1}_{A_k}f(y\mid x)\,dy\,P_X(dx)$. Since $|\{y:f(y\mid x)\ge t_k\}|\le 1/t_k$, $|A_k^\star|_P<\infty$; if $|A_k|_P=\infty$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise the integrand of
$$\Delta_k=\iint\left(\mathbf{1}_{A_k^\star}-\mathbf{1}_{A_k}\right)\left(f(y\mid x)-t_k\right)dy\,P_X(dx)$$
is non-negative pointwise: on $A_k^\star\setminus A_k$ we have $f\ge t_k$, and on $A_k\setminus A_k^\star$, where $y\in B_k$, we have $f<t_k$. Hence $\Delta_k\ge 0$, i.e. $t_k(|A_k^\star|_P-|A_k|_P)\le p_k\{\mathrm{Cov}_k(C^\star)-\mathrm{Cov}_k(C)\}\le 0$, and $t_k>0$ gives $|A_k^\star|_P\le|A_k|_P$. Summing over $k$ gives the statement for $\mathbb{E}|C(X)|$. $\square$

**Proof of Proposition 3.** Condition on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ (hence on $\hat f$) and write $V_i=f(Y_i\mid X_i)$, $\hat V_i=\hat f(Y_i\mid X_i)$, so that $|\hat V_i-V_i|\le\varepsilon_N$. *Thresholds.* By (3), $-\hat q_k$ is the $(n_k+1-r_k)$-th smallest of $\{\hat V_i:i\in I_k\}$, and $(n_k+1-r_k)/n_k\to\alpha_k$ because $n_k\to\infty$ almost surely ($n_k\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,p_k)$ given $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$). Order statistics are 1-Lipschitz in the sup-norm, so $-\hat q_k$ differs by at most $\varepsilon_N$ from the same order statistic of $\{V_i:i\in I_k\}$. The $V_i$, $i\in I_k$, are i.i.d. from the law of $f(Y\mid X)$ given $Y\in B_k$, independent of $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$, whose $\alpha_k$-quantile $t_k$ is unique by assumption; hence that order statistic converges to $t_k$ almost surely [@serfling1980approximation, Sec. 2.3], and $\delta_k:=|\hat q_k+t_k|\to0$ in probability. *Sets.* Fix $x$ and $y\in B_k$. If $y$ lies in exactly one of $\hat C(x)$ and $C^\star(x)$, then $\hat f(y\mid x)+\hat q_k$ and $f(y\mid x)-t_k$ have opposite signs (one is $\ge0$, the other $<0$), while they differ by at most $\varepsilon_N+\delta_k$; hence $|f(y\mid x)-t_k|\le\eta:=\varepsilon_N+\max_k\delta_k$. Therefore
$$\mathbb{E}_X|\hat C(X)\triangle C^\star(X)|\le\sum_{k=1}^K h_k(\eta),\qquad h_k(\eta)=\mathbb{E}\,\big|\{y\in B_k:|f(y\mid X)-t_k|\le\eta\}\big|.$$
For $\eta<t_k/2$ the set inside $h_k$ lies in $\{y:f(y\mid X)\ge t_k/2\}$, whose measure is at most $2/t_k$, and it decreases to $\{y\in B_k:f(y\mid X)=t_k\}$ as $\eta\downarrow0$. By dominated convergence $h_k(\eta)\to\mathbb{E}|\{y\in B_k:f(y\mid X)=t_k\}|$, which is zero: continuity of the law of $f(Y\mid X)$ given $Y\in B_k$ gives $0=p_k^{-1}\iint_{B_k}\mathbf{1}\{f(y\mid x)=t_k\}f(y\mid x)\,dy\,P_X(dx)=p_k^{-1}t_k\,\mathbb{E}|\{y\in B_k:f(y\mid X)=t_k\}|$. Since $\eta\to0$ in probability, (7) follows, and $|\,|\hat C|-|C^\star|\,|\le|\hat C\triangle C^\star|$ gives the statement on expected size. $\square$

**Remark (within-stratum coverage profile).** Under label shift, let $\kappa(y)=P\{y\in\hat C(X_{n+1})\mid Y_{n+1}=y\}$, which is the same under $P$ and $Q$. Then the change in stratum-wise coverage equals the within-stratum covariance $\mathbb{E}_{P_k}[\{\kappa(Y)-c\}\{\lambda_k(Y)-1\}]$ for any constant $c$, so coverage is lost only if the shift moves mass within $B_k$ towards values that are covered less often than the stratum average. For the oracle sets $C^\star$ in the Gaussian model of Proposition 1, $C^\star(x)\cap B_k=\{y\in B_k:|y-x|\le r_k\}$ for a stratum-specific radius $r_k$, so the argument of Proposition 1 (with $a=r_k$) shows that $\kappa$ decreases in $|y|$ within every stratum, that is, towards the outer end of a tail stratum; for the estimated sets the same pattern is observed empirically (Section [-@sec:results]). This is why a tilt towards more extreme outcomes can lower tail-stratum coverage even after stratified calibration.
