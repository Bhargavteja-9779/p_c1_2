## (a) Contributions (Introduction)

*This replaces line 9 of `01_intro.md` ("This paper studies target-conditional coverage for regression and makes the following contributions.") and the numbered list that follows it.*

Calibrating conformal thresholds separately within bins of the observed outcome is not new. @randahl2026bin proposed it as bin-conditional conformal prediction (BCCP) for a heavily skewed outcome. It is also the binning step of the binned fair quantile regression (BFQR) of @wang2023equal, which aims at equal coverage across groups conditionally on the outcome. Its finite-sample bin-wise validity is an instance of Mondrian (label-conditional) validity [@vovk2005alrw; @vovk2013conditional; @toccaceli2019combination]. We take this calibration rule as given and contribute four things: an explanation of why it is needed, the score it should be combined with, a precise account of what it guarantees under target shift, and a large-scale evaluation:

1. **Why covariate adaptivity is not enough.** We study stratum-wise coverage, $P\{Y\in C(X)\mid Y\in B_k\}\ge 1-\alpha_k$, for target strata $B_k$ such as the lower and upper tails. This is the regression analogue of class-conditional coverage and the property that bin-conditional calibration targets. We prove that covariate-conditional validity does not imply it, even for the oracle: in a Gaussian model, the shortest interval with coverage $1-\alpha$ at every $x$ covers only {{prop1.upper10_r05}} of the top-decile outcomes when the predictor explains half of the variance, and its coverage at $y$ tends to zero as $|y|$ grows (Proposition 1). Tail under-coverage of standard conformal sets has been reported empirically [@randahl2026bin; @boruah2026distribution], and exact covariate-conditional coverage cannot be guaranteed without assumptions [@barber2021limits]; Proposition 1 adds that the failure persists with a perfect model, so it is not an artefact of estimation.
2. **Which score to stratify: size-optimal sets.** Stratified calibration is valid for any score, so the score determines only how large the sets are. We prove that, among all prediction sets with coverage at least $1-\alpha_k$ in every stratum, expected Lebesgue measure is minimised by conditional-density level sets with stratum-specific thresholds (Theorem 2). The optimal score is therefore the conditional density itself, and the stratum constraints enter only through the thresholds. This extends the class-specific Neyman–Pearson solution for least-ambiguous set-valued classifiers [@sadinle2019least; see also @lei2014classification] from counting measure on finitely many labels to Lebesgue measure on strata of a continuous outcome; for $K=1$ it reduces to the known optimality of density level sets under marginal coverage [@lei2013distribution; @izbicki2022cdsplit].
3. **SDLC and a precise account of its guarantees.** SDLC (stratified density-level conformal prediction) combines the calibration rule of BCCP with an estimate of the optimal score of Theorem 2, a semiparametric location–scale conditional density with a kernel estimate of the standardised-noise density; its floor variant, the union of the marginal set at level $\alpha$ and the stratified set at level $\beta\ge\alpha$, guarantees marginal coverage $1-\alpha$ and stratum-wise coverage $1-\beta$ simultaneously in finite samples. We present the stratum-wise validity of these procedures (Theorem 1) as an instance of Mondrian validity rather than as a new guarantee. We also make the claim of robustness to target shift precise (Corollary 1): stratum-wise coverage is preserved exactly when the shift re-weights whole strata, $Q(\cdot\mid Y\in B_k)=P(\cdot\mid Y\in B_k)$, and otherwise it can drop by at most the total-variation distance between these within-stratum laws, which under label shift we bound by how much the density ratio varies within each stratum. Unlike re-weighting approaches [@podkopaev2021distribution; @yi2025distribution; @lee2026split], this needs no estimate of the shift, but it is exact only for shifts that act on the stratum probabilities.
4. **A pre-registered benchmark against bin-conditional and covariate-adaptive methods.** On all {{n_datasets}} eligible datasets of the OpenML-CTR23 suite [@fischer2023openml], each with {{n_seeds}} predefined random splits, we compare {{n_methods_main}} conformal procedures that share identical base models and test the hypotheses with dataset-level non-parametric statistics. The procedures include BCCP in its union and hull forms [@randahl2026bin], SCP, NCP, CQR, Mondrian conformal regression on bins of the point prediction [cf. @bostrom2020mondrian], DCP and HPD-split, and we also run a BFQR-style binned CQR, with CQR scores calibrated within equal-mass target deciles [@wang2023equal]. Besides tail coverage, we measure the price of stratum-wise validity in set size, test whether the size advantage that Theorem 2 predicts for the density-level score over residual and quantile scores survives estimation of the density, and analyse behaviour under target shift, sensitivity to design choices and computational cost. Comparators added after pre-registration are reported as documented deviations (Section [-@sec:protocol]), and code, configuration and all raw results are released.

## (b) Replacement for the sentence that introduces Equation (4)

*This replaces, in `02_theory.md` (Section "Stratified calibration and its finite-sample guarantee", line 31), the sentence "Equation (4) is a Mondrian conformal predictor whose taxonomy depends on the label [...]; for regression it yields sets that may be unions of up to $K$ intervals." The preceding sentence ("Note that the stratum used for a candidate value ...") stays.*

Equation (4) is an inductive Mondrian conformal predictor whose taxonomy assigns each example to the stratum of its label [@vovk2005alrw; @vovk2013conditional; @toccaceli2019combination], and it is not new for regression: with a residual score it is the bin-conditional conformal prediction (BCCP) of @randahl2026bin, who report either the union (4) or its convex hull ("contiguized" BCCP), and @wang2023equal calibrate within equal-mass bins of $Y$ to obtain equal coverage across groups conditionally on the outcome. What this paper adds to (3)–(4) is the choice of score, derived from Theorem 2 below, and a precise statement of what the stratified guarantee implies under target shift (Corollary 1). For scores whose sub-level sets in $y$ are intervals, such as the absolute residual or the CQR score, $\hat C(x)$ is a union of at most $K$ intervals; density-based scores can produce more pieces.

## (c) Framing of Theorem 1

*Insert this paragraph immediately before the statement of Theorem 1. The statement and the proof of Theorem 1 stay unchanged. Suggested new header: **Theorem 1 (stratum-wise validity of Mondrian calibration).***

Theorem 1 is not a new type of guarantee. Inductive Mondrian conformal predictors are valid conditionally on each category of their taxonomy, and the taxonomy may depend on the label. Label-conditional validity is the classical special case [@vovk2005alrw; @vovk2013conditional; @toccaceli2019combination], and Theorem 1 is this property for the taxonomy that maps an example to the target stratum of its label. @randahl2026bin state the same bin-wise guarantee for BCCP, and Kandinsky conformal prediction [@bairaktari2025kandinsky] extends group-conditional guarantees to overlapping and fractional groups defined jointly on covariates and labels. We state the result for an arbitrary score fitted on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$, with the threshold chosen by the stratum of the candidate value, and keep the short proof for completeness. Its conditioning step shows that, for independent data and given $n_k$, coverage in stratum $k$ depends on $P$ only through the within-stratum law $P(\cdot\mid Y\in B_k)$, and Corollary 1 relies on exactly this property. The upper bound and the marginal consequence follow from the usual split-conformal argument [@lei2018distribution].

## (d) Corollary 1 (target shift), restated

*This replaces Corollary 1 and the paragraph after it in `02_theory.md` (lines 39–41, from "**Corollary 1 (robustness to target shift).**" to "... requires no estimate of the shifted stratum probabilities."). Notation that is new to the section ($\pi_k$, $\lambda_k$, $w$, $\kappa$) does not clash with existing symbols.*

**Corollary 1 (target shift: exact and approximate stratum-wise validity).** *Condition on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$, let the calibration points be i.i.d. from $P$, and let the test point $(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})$ be drawn from $Q$, independently of them. Write $P_k=P(\cdot\mid Y\in B_k)$ and $Q_k=Q(\cdot\mid Y\in B_k)$ for the within-stratum laws of $(X,Y)$, and $\pi_k=Q(Y\in B_k)$. Let $\mathrm{Cov}_k^Q=Q\{Y_{n+1}\in\hat C(X_{n+1})\mid Y_{n+1}\in B_k\}$, and let $\mathrm{Cov}_k^P$ denote the left-hand side of (5), that is, the same probability when the test point is drawn from $P$.*

*(i) Exact case. Suppose $Q_k=P_k$ for every $k$ with $\pi_k>0$, that is, $Q$ re-weights whole strata ($dQ/dP=\pi_k/p_k$ on $\mathcal{X}\times B_k$). Then $\mathrm{Cov}_k^Q=\mathrm{Cov}_k^P\ge 1-\alpha_k$ for every such $k$, and the upper bound of Theorem 1 carries over. Moreover, $Q\{Y_{n+1}\in\hat C(X_{n+1})\}\ge 1-\sum_k\pi_k\alpha_k\ge 1-\max_k\alpha_k$, whatever the stratum probabilities $\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_K$.*

*(ii) Arbitrary shift. For every $k$ with $\pi_k>0$, $|\mathrm{Cov}_k^Q-\mathrm{Cov}_k^P|\le d_{\mathrm{TV}}(Q_k,P_k)=\sup_A|Q_k(A)-P_k(A)|$, so that $\mathrm{Cov}_k^Q\ge 1-\alpha_k-d_{\mathrm{TV}}(Q_k,P_k)$.*

*(iii) Label shift. Suppose $Q(\cdot\mid Y=y)=P(\cdot\mid Y=y)$ for $P$-almost every $y$, and let $m_k$ and $M_k$ be the essential infimum and supremum on $B_k$ of the density ratio $w=dQ_Y/dP_Y$. If $0<m_k\le M_k<\infty$, then $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(Q_k,P_k)\le\tanh(\delta_k/4)\le\delta_k/4$, where $\delta_k=\log(M_k/m_k)$ is the oscillation of $\log w$ on $B_k$.*

*Proof.* Given $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$, the set $A=\{(x,y):y\in\hat C(x)\}$ is fixed. Because the test point is independent of $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$, $\mathrm{Cov}_k^Q=\mathbb{E}\,Q_k(A)$ and $\mathrm{Cov}_k^P=\mathbb{E}\,P_k(A)$, where $\mathbb{E}$ averages over $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}\sim P^n$. (i) If $Q_k=P_k$, the two expectations coincide, and Theorem 1 and the law of total probability give the claims. (ii) $|Q_k(A)-P_k(A)|\le d_{\mathrm{TV}}(Q_k,P_k)$ for every fixed $A$; average over $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$ and use (5). (iii) Under label shift, $\lambda_k=dQ_k/dP_k=w(y)/\bar w_k$ with $\bar w_k=\pi_k/p_k=\mathbb{E}_{P_k}w(Y)$. Hence $\mathbb{E}_{P_k}\lambda_k=1$ and $\lambda_k\in[a,b]$ with $a=m_k/\bar w_k\le 1\le b=M_k/\bar w_k$ (the case $a=b$ is trivial). Since $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(Q_k,P_k)=\mathbb{E}_{P_k}(\lambda_k-1)_+$, and by convexity $(\lambda-1)_+\le(b-1)(\lambda-a)/(b-a)$ on $[a,b]$, we get $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(Q_k,P_k)\le(b-1)(1-a)/(b-a)=(M_k-\bar w_k)(\bar w_k-m_k)/\{\bar w_k(M_k-m_k)\}$. The right-hand side is maximised at $\bar w_k=\sqrt{m_kM_k}$, where it equals $(\sqrt{M_k}-\sqrt{m_k})/(\sqrt{M_k}+\sqrt{m_k})=\tanh(\delta_k/4)$, and $\tanh u\le u$ for $u\ge 0$. $\square$

By contrast, under a shift of type (i) a marginally calibrated set $C$ has $Q\{Y\in C(X)\}=\sum_k\pi_k\mathrm{Cov}_k^P(C)$. This can be as low as $\min_k\mathrm{Cov}_k^P(C)$, and hence far below $1-\alpha$. Part (i) is the stratified analogue of the elementary fact that class-conditional coverage survives label shift in classification, where every class is a stratum and a label shift necessarily re-weights whole strata [cf. @podkopaev2021distribution]. With a continuous outcome, a label shift generally also changes the distribution of $Y$ *within* each stratum. Part (i) therefore covers exactly those deployment shifts that change how often each stratum occurs but not what the data look like inside it. An example is a season in which upper-tail outcomes become more frequent while the joint distribution of an upper-tail outcome and its covariates stays the same. A stable covariate distribution within each stratum is not sufficient for (i): the within-stratum law of $(X,Y)$ must be stable.

Parts (ii) and (iii) describe what happens when the density ratio varies within strata. Under label shift, let $\kappa(y)=P\{y\in\hat C(X_{n+1})\mid Y_{n+1}=y\}$ be the target-conditional coverage at $y$, which is the same under $P$ and $Q$. Then $\mathrm{Cov}_k^P=\mathbb{E}_{P_k}\kappa(Y)$ and $\mathrm{Cov}_k^Q=\mathbb{E}_{P_k}\{\kappa(Y)\lambda_k(Y)\}$, so that
$$\mathrm{Cov}_k^Q-\mathrm{Cov}_k^P=\mathbb{E}_{P_k}\big[\{\kappa(Y)-c\}\{\lambda_k(Y)-1\}\big]\quad\text{for every constant }c.$$
This is the within-stratum covariance between the coverage profile $\kappa$ and the density ratio. Coverage is therefore lost only if the shift moves mass inside $B_k$ towards values of $y$ that are covered less often than the stratum average. Choosing $c$ as the midpoint of the range of $\kappa$ on $B_k$ sharpens (ii)–(iii) to $|\mathrm{Cov}_k^Q-\mathrm{Cov}_k^P|\le\omega_k\,d_{\mathrm{TV}}(Q_k,P_k)\le\omega_k\tanh(\delta_k/4)$, where $\omega_k$ is the oscillation of $\kappa$ on $B_k$. In the model of Proposition 1 with the residual score $|y-x|$, $\kappa$ on a stratum is (2) with $z\sigma$ replaced by $\hat q_k$, averaged over $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$. It therefore decreases towards the outer end of each tail stratum, so a shift towards more extreme outcomes lowers tail-stratum coverage, possibly below $1-\alpha_k$, even after stratified calibration. The deviation is small when $w$ varies little across each stratum, which favours narrower strata, at the price of fewer calibration points per stratum. For an open tail stratum under an unbounded tilt, such as exponential tilting of $Y$, $M_k=\infty$ and the bound is vacuous. Robustness to such shifts is then an empirical question, which Experiment 4 addresses.

Re-weighting methods take the complementary route. They estimate the density ratio and use it to re-weight the calibration data or the conditional density, which targets coverage under general label shift at the cost of estimating $w$. See @podkopaev2021distribution for classification, @yi2025distribution and @lee2026split for regression, and @tibshirani2019conformal for weighting under covariate shift. Stratified calibration needs no such estimate. It is exact when the shift acts only on stratum probabilities, and otherwise it degrades by no more than (ii)–(iii) allow. SDLC-hull contains $\hat C$, and SDLC-floor contains the stratified set at level $\beta$, so the lower bounds in (i)–(iii) also hold for these variants, with $\alpha_k$ replaced by $\beta$ for SDLC-floor. The marginal level $1-\alpha$ of SDLC-floor comes from a marginally calibrated set and is not protected under shift.
