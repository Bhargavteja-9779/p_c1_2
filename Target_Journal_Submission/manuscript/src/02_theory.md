# Preliminaries and problem formulation {#sec:problem}

## Target-conditional coverage

Let $(X,Y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}$ have joint law $P$, with marginal law $P_X$ of the covariates. A *prediction set* is a map $C$ from $\mathcal{X}$ to subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ whose graph $\{(x,y):y\in C(x)\}$ is jointly measurable. Split conformal prediction [@papadopoulos2002inductive; @lei2018distribution] guarantees *marginal* coverage $P\{Y\in C(X)\}\ge 1-\alpha$, and much recent work targets approximate *covariate-conditional* coverage $P\{Y\in C(X)\mid X=x\}\approx 1-\alpha$ [@romano2019conformalized; @izbicki2022cdsplit; @gibbs2025conformal], which cannot be guaranteed in finite samples without further assumptions [@barber2021limits].

In imbalanced regression the outcomes of interest are rare target values. Let $e_1<\dots<e_{K-1}$ be cut points and $B_1=(-\infty,e_1)$, $B_k=[e_{k-1},e_k)$ for $1<k<K$, $B_K=[e_{K-1},\infty)$ the resulting *target strata*, for instance the lower tail, the bulk and the upper tail, with $p_k=P(Y\in B_k)>0$. We call
$$\mathrm{Cov}_k(C)=P\{Y\in C(X)\mid Y\in B_k\},\qquad k=1,\ldots,K, \tag{1}$$
the *stratum-wise* (target-conditional) coverage of $C$, the regression analogue of class-conditional coverage [@vovk2013conditional; @sadinle2019least] and the notion behind BCCP [@randahl2026bin] and equal opportunity of coverage [@wang2023equal]. Because $\sum_k p_k\,\mathrm{Cov}_k(C)=P\{Y\in C(X)\}$, marginal coverage only constrains a $p$-weighted average of the $\mathrm{Cov}_k$. In particular, a set with *exact* coverage $1-\alpha$, marginally or at every $x$, has $\min_k\mathrm{Cov}_k(C)\le 1-\alpha$, with equality only if all strata are covered at exactly the same rate; any over-coverage of the bulk is paid for in some other stratum.

## Covariate-conditional validity does not imply target-conditional validity

How large can the shortfall be for the best covariate-conditional set? The simplest model gives an answer.

**Proposition 1 (Gaussian model).** *Let $X\sim N(0,\tau^2)$ and $Y\mid X\sim N(X,\sigma^2)$ with $\tau^2+\sigma^2=1$ and $0<\tau^2<1$, so that $R^2=\tau^2$ is the fraction of variance explained by the optimal predictor. Let $z=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)$ and $C^{\mathrm{cov}}(x)=[x-z\sigma,\,x+z\sigma]$, which is, up to Lebesgue-null sets, the unique set of minimal length with $P\{Y\in C(x)\mid X=x\}\ge 1-\alpha$ for every $x$. Then*
$$P\{Y\in C^{\mathrm{cov}}(X)\mid Y=y\}=\Phi\!\left(\frac{z\sigma-(1-R^2)y}{s}\right)-\Phi\!\left(\frac{-z\sigma-(1-R^2)y}{s}\right),\qquad s=\sigma\sqrt{R^2}, \tag{2}$$
*an even function of $y$ that is strictly decreasing in $|y|$ and tends to $0$ as $|y|\to\infty$.*

*Proof.* $(X,Y)$ is bivariate normal with $\mathrm{Var}(Y)=1$ and $\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)=\tau^2$, hence $X\mid Y=y\sim N(\tau^2y,\tau^2\sigma^2)$ and $Y-X\mid Y=y\sim N((1-\tau^2)y,\tau^2\sigma^2)$. Since $Y\in C^{\mathrm{cov}}(X)$ iff $|Y-X|\le z\sigma$, (2) follows. Write $a=z\sigma>0$ and $m=(1-R^2)y$. The function $h(m)=\Phi((a-m)/s)-\Phi((-a-m)/s)$ is even because $\Phi(-u)=1-\Phi(u)$, and for $m>0$, $h'(m)=-\{\phi((a-m)/s)-\phi((a+m)/s)\}/s<0$ because $|a-m|<a+m$. As $1-R^2>0$, (2) is strictly decreasing in $|y|$ and vanishes as $|y|\to\infty$. Minimality and uniqueness of $C^{\mathrm{cov}}(x)$ follow because the $N(x,\sigma^2)$ density is symmetric and strictly unimodal. $\square$

Integrating (2) over strata of $Y$ (Table [-@tbl:prop1]): for a predictor that explains half of the variance, the oracle covariate-conditional interval covers {{prop1.upper10_r05}} of the top-10% outcomes and {{prop1.upper1_r05}} of the top-1% outcomes, against {{prop1.central_r05}} of the central 80%; with $R^2=0.9$ it covers {{prop1.upper10_r09}} of the top decile. The shape of this profile is specific to the Gaussian model, so Table [-@tbl:prop1] also reports Monte Carlo values for heavy-tailed signals and skewed noise. With a Student-$t_3$ signal the top decile is still under-covered at $R^2=0.5$ ({{J:prop1_extension.json|0/top10|f3}}), but the top 1% is covered at {{J:prop1_extension.json|0/top1|f3}}, because extreme outcomes then mostly come from extreme signals, which a covariate-conditional set follows. With right-skewed Gamma noise the oracle *over*-covers the lower decile ({{J:prop1_extension.json|3/bottom10|f3}}) and covers only {{J:prop1_extension.json|3/top10|f3}} of the top decile and {{J:prop1_extension.json|3/top1|f3}} of the top 1%. In every case at least one tail stratum is covered well below $1-\alpha$. Tail under-coverage of standard conformal sets has been reported empirically [@wang2023equal; @randahl2026bin; @boruah2026distribution]; it persists for the oracle, so better covariate adaptivity cannot remove it. Coverage in rare strata requires calibration against the law of $(X,Y)$ *given* $Y\in B_k$, which is what stratified calibration does.

# Proposed method: stratified density-level conformal prediction {#sec:method}

## Stratified (Mondrian) calibration

Data are split into a proper training set $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and a calibration set $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}=\{(X_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$. On $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ we fit a nonconformity score $s:\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}$ (small values = conforming) and choose the cut points, e.g. at quantiles of the training targets. Let $k(y)$ be the stratum containing $y$, $I_k=\{i:Y_i\in B_k\}$, $n_k=|I_k|$, and, for levels $\alpha_k\in(0,1)$,
$$\hat q_k=\text{the }r_k\text{-th smallest value of }\{s(X_i,Y_i):i\in I_k\},\qquad r_k=\lceil (n_k+1)(1-\alpha_k)\rceil, \tag{3}$$
with $\hat q_k=+\infty$ if $r_k>n_k$, which happens exactly when $n_k<1/\alpha_k-1$ (i.e. $n_k\le 8$ for $\alpha_k=0.1$). The stratified prediction set is
$$\hat C(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}: s(x,y)\le \hat q_{k(y)}\}=\bigcup_{k=1}^K\{y\in B_k: s(x,y)\le\hat q_k\}. \tag{4}$$
The threshold applied to a candidate value $y$ is that of the stratum *of that candidate*, not of a prediction; this makes the guarantee target-conditional. Equation (4) is an inductive Mondrian conformal predictor whose taxonomy assigns each example to the stratum of its label [@vovk2005alrw; @vovk2013conditional]; with the absolute-residual score it is BCCP [@randahl2026bin]. When every sub-level set $\{y:s(x,y)\le q\}$ is an interval, as for residual or quantile scores, $\hat C(x)$ is a union of at most $K$ intervals; density scores with a multimodal estimate can produce more pieces.

The following guarantee is the standard label-conditional Mondrian property [@vovk2013conditional], stated for outcome bins by @wang2023equal [Thm. 4.1] and @randahl2026bin. We restate it for an arbitrary score, with the edge cases used later (proof in Appendix A).

**Theorem 1 (stratum-wise validity of Mondrian calibration).** *Suppose that, conditionally on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$, the points $(X_1,Y_1),\ldots,(X_n,Y_n),(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})$ are exchangeable, and let $p_k=P(Y_{n+1}\in B_k\mid\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}})$. Then for every $k$ with $p_k>0$,*
$$P\{Y_{n+1}\in\hat C(X_{n+1})\mid Y_{n+1}\in B_k,\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}\}\ \ge\ 1-\alpha_k, \tag{5}$$
*and consequently $P\{Y_{n+1}\in\hat C(X_{n+1})\mid\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}\}\ge 1-\sum_kp_k\alpha_k\ge 1-\max_k\alpha_k$. If the scores within stratum $k$ are almost surely distinct and finite, then also $P\{Y_{n+1}\in\hat C(X_{n+1})\mid Y_{n+1}\in B_k,n_k,\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}\}\le 1-\alpha_k+1/(n_k+1)$. The levels $\alpha_k$ may depend on $n_k$, and the score and strata may be any functions of $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$.*

The guarantee holds for *any* score. For target shift between calibration and deployment, known facts specialise as follows.

**Corollary 1 (target shift).** *Let $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$ be i.i.d. from $P$ and let the test point be drawn from $Q$, independently of $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$ and $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$. Write $P_k=P(\cdot\mid Y\in B_k)$ and $Q_k=Q(\cdot\mid Y\in B_k)$ for the within-stratum laws of the pair $(X,Y)$ and $\pi_k=Q(Y\in B_k)$; assume $p_k>0$ whenever $\pi_k>0$.*
*(i) If $Q_k=P_k$ for every $k$ with $\pi_k>0$ (the shift re-weights whole strata), then $Q\{Y\in\hat C(X)\mid Y\in B_k\}\ge 1-\alpha_k$ for every such $k$, and $Q\{Y\in\hat C(X)\}\ge 1-\sum_k\pi_k\alpha_k$ whatever the $\pi_k$.*
*(ii) For an arbitrary shift, $Q\{Y\in\hat C(X)\mid Y\in B_k\}\ge 1-\alpha_k-d_{\mathrm{TV}}(Q_k,P_k)$.*
*(iii) Under label shift, $Q(\cdot\mid Y=y)=P(\cdot\mid Y=y)$ with density ratio $w=dQ_Y/dP_Y$; let $m_k$ and $M_k$ be the essential infimum and supremum of $w$ on $B_k$, $0<m_k\le M_k<\infty$. Then $Q\{Y\notin\hat C(X)\mid Y\in B_k\}\le(M_k/m_k)\,\alpha_k$ and $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(Q_k,P_k)\le\tanh\{\log(M_k/m_k)/4\}$.*

The proof is in Appendix A. Part (i) is the continuous-outcome form of the label-shift robustness of label-conditional coverage [@podkopaev2021distribution; @angelopoulos2024theoretical]. For a continuous outcome, a label shift generally also changes the law of $Y$ *within* a stratum, so exactness requires an unchanged joint law of $(X,Y)$ inside each stratum. Part (ii) applies the coverage-gap bounds of @barber2023beyond [Sec. 4.5] within a stratum, and part (iii) follows from a bounded likelihood ratio. Both bounds are loose for the tilts of Experiment 4, whose density ratios are unbounded on the open tail strata; they explain the direction of the effect, not its size. Marginally calibrated sets have no analogue of (i): their coverage under $Q$ is $\sum_k\pi_k\mathrm{Cov}_k$, which can be as low as $\min_k\mathrm{Cov}_k$. Stratified calibration needs no estimate of the shift, but it is exact only for shifts that act on the stratum probabilities.

## The smallest sets with stratum-wise coverage

Theorem 1 leaves the score free; the score determines how large the sets are. For a prediction set $C$ let $|C(x)|$ be its Lebesgue measure and $\mathbb{E}|C(X)|=\int|C(x)|\,P_X(dx)$ its expected size. By the Neyman–Pearson argument behind conformal density level sets [@lei2013distribution], least-ambiguous classifiers [@sadinle2019least] and covariate-indexed length-optimal sets [@kiyani2024length], size-optimal sets are density level sets. The constraints in (1) are indexed by the *label*, so the problem separates by stratum and the thresholds are constant within each stratum.

**Proposition 2 (size-optimal stratum-wise sets).** *Suppose the conditional law of $Y$ given $X$ has a Lebesgue density $f(y\mid x)$, that $f(Y\mid X)>0$ almost surely, and that for each $k$ the random variable $f(Y\mid X)$ has a continuous distribution conditionally on $Y\in B_k$. Fix $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_K\in(0,1)$. Then there are thresholds $t_k>0$ with $P\{f(Y\mid X)\ge t_k\mid Y\in B_k\}=1-\alpha_k$, and the set*
$$C^\star(x)=\bigcup_{k=1}^K\{y\in B_k: f(y\mid x)\ge t_k\} \tag{6}$$
*satisfies $\mathrm{Cov}_k(C^\star)=1-\alpha_k$ for all $k$. Every prediction set $C$ with $\mathrm{Cov}_k(C)\ge 1-\alpha_k$ for all $k$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}|C(X)\cap B_k|\ge\mathbb{E}|C^\star(X)\cap B_k|$ for every $k$, and hence $\mathbb{E}|C(X)|\ge\mathbb{E}|C^\star(X)|$.*

The proof (Appendix A) is the per-stratum Neyman–Pearson argument; it also covers data-dependent (e.g. conformal) sets whose stratum-wise coverage, averaged over the data, is at least $1-\alpha_k$. With $K=1$ it reduces to a single-threshold density level set, whereas HPD-split [@izbicki2022cdsplit] uses an $x$-specific threshold. Proposition 2 is an oracle statement. The next result shows that stratified calibration of a consistent density estimate attains it asymptotically (with fixed strata); its proof follows the plug-in arguments of @lei2013distribution and @izbicki2022cdsplit.

**Proposition 3 (asymptotic size-optimality of the plug-in procedure).** *Let the assumptions of Proposition 2 hold, and suppose in addition that the distribution function of $f(Y\mid X)$ given $Y\in B_k$ is strictly increasing at $t_k$ for every $k$. Let $\hat f$ be estimated from $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ of size $N$, let $\varepsilon_N=\sup_{x,y}|\hat f(y\mid x)-f(y\mid x)|\to0$ in probability, and let $\hat C$ be the stratified set (3)–(4) with score $s=-\hat f$, fixed strata $B_1,\ldots,B_K$ and $n\to\infty$ calibration points. Then $-\hat q_k\to t_k$ in probability for every $k$, and*
$$\mathbb{E}_X\big|\hat C(X)\,\triangle\,C^\star(X)\big|\ \to\ 0\quad\text{in probability,} \tag{7}$$
*where $\mathbb{E}_X$ averages over a new $X\sim P_X$ given the data. In particular $\mathbb{E}_X|\hat C(X)|\to\mathbb{E}|C^\star(X)|$ while (5) holds for every $n$.*

Four consequences guide the method.

*The optimal score* is the conditional density, $s(x,y)=-f(y\mid x)$, with stratum-specific thresholds; a single threshold is optimal only for marginal coverage.

*The price of stratum-wise validity.* Every set that meets the stratum constraints has marginal coverage at least $1-\bar\alpha$, $\bar\alpha=\sum_kp_k\alpha_k$, so $\mathbb{E}|C^\star(X)|$ is at least the size of the optimal marginal set at that level, a single-threshold density level set. The two coincide if and only if $t_1=\dots=t_K$. With a common level $\alpha_k=\alpha$ the difference is the price, in size, of stratum-wise validity.

*Coverage is reallocated across inputs.* In a location–scale model $f(y\mid x)=g\{(y-\mu(x))/\sigma(x)\}/\sigma(x)$ the optimal set is, in standardised units $u=(y-\mu(x))/\sigma(x)$, $\{u: g(u)\ge t_k\sigma(x)\}$ within stratum $k$. Its standardised width *shrinks* as $\sigma(x)$ grows, and it is empty when $t_k\sigma(x)>\max g$: the oracle spends coverage where it is cheap. The stratified normalised residual (S-NCP), whose standardised width does not depend on $x$, is optimal only for constant $\sigma$. The density score's size advantage over S-NCP thus comes partly from the factor $1/\sigma(x)$ and is paid for by lower coverage, and occasionally empty sets, for the noisiest inputs. Users who need comparable coverage across noise levels should prefer S-NCP.

*Shape and scope.* Because thresholds differ across strata, $C^\star(x)$ need not be an interval even for unimodal $f(\cdot\mid x)$. Optimality refers to size under the calibration covariate law; under a shifted $Q$, $C^\star$ stays valid in the sense of Corollary 1(i) but is no longer size-optimal. For targets with atoms (ties, zero inflation) Theorem 1 is unaffected, but a smooth density score is then valid without being size-optimal.

## The SDLC procedure

Figure [-@fig:schematic] summarises the workflow.

{{FIG:schematic|F0_method_schematic.png|Workflow of stratified density-level conformal prediction (SDLC). Upper row: the location–scale density model $\hat f(y\mid x)$ and the target strata $B_1,\dots,B_K$ are fitted on the proper training set. Lower row: calibration scores are grouped by the stratum of the observed target and a conformal quantile $\hat q_k$ is computed in each stratum. The prediction set is the union over strata of the density level sets at the stratum's threshold, which guarantees coverage within every stratum in finite samples (Theorem 1).}}

SDLC instantiates (3)–(4) with the plug-in score $s(x,y)=-\hat f(y\mid x)$, where $\hat f$ is estimated on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$; (5) holds for any $\hat f$. We use the semiparametric location–scale estimator
$$\hat f(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{\hat\sigma(x)}\,\hat g\!\left(\frac{y-\hat\mu(x)}{\hat\sigma(x)}\right). \tag{8}$$
Here $\hat\mu$ is a gradient-boosted regression of $Y$ on $X$, and $\hat\sigma(x)=\max\{\tilde\sigma(x),\varsigma\}$, where $\tilde\sigma$ is a gradient-boosted regression of the absolute *out-of-fold* residuals $|Y-\hat\mu_{-j}(X)|$ (three folds), the spread estimate of locally weighted conformal prediction [@lei2018distribution]. The floor $\varsigma=\max\{10^{-3}\mathrm{sd}(Y_{\mathrm{tr}}),\,0.05\,\mathrm{median}|Y-\hat\mu_{-j}(X)|\}$ is shared by every method that uses $\hat\sigma$. The noise density is a defensive mixture
$$\hat g=(1-\epsilon)\,\hat g_{\mathrm{KDE}}+\epsilon\,t_2(\cdot/\hat s)/\hat s,\qquad \epsilon=0.01, \tag{9}$$
of a Gaussian kernel density estimate (Silverman bandwidth) and a Student-$t_2$ density with robust scale $\hat s=\mathrm{IQR}/1.349$. The mixture is fitted to *cross-fitted* standardised residuals: the out-of-fold residuals of fold $j$ are divided by a scale model trained on the other two folds, so that neither $\hat\mu$ nor $\hat\sigma$ has seen the residual it standardises (an in-sample scale fit shrinks the residuals, in our data by a median factor of {{J:metrics_v3.json|z_sd_ratio_median|f2}} in standard deviation). This choice affects efficiency only, not validity, and was not tuned. The defensive component gives $\hat g$ polynomial tails, so that far-out targets still receive a smooth, strictly decreasing density. The nonparametric $\hat g$ captures skewness and heavy tails of the errors, and heteroscedasticity enters through $\hat\sigma$. If $Y=\mu(X)+\sigma(X)\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon$ independent of $X$, and $\epsilon$ and $\varsigma$ shrink to zero as $N$ grows, (8) can be uniformly consistent and Proposition 3 applies. Our implementation keeps $\epsilon=0.01$ and the floor fixed and truncates to the domain $D$, so Proposition 3 describes the limit of a sequence of such estimators, not the fixed-constant implementation itself. Otherwise SDLC targets the optimal sets of the best location–scale approximation, which affects size but never validity.

Three alternatives serve as ablations. SDLC-N replaces $\hat g$ by the standard normal density (with a unit-scale defensive $t_2$ component), so that its score differs from the normalised residual mainly through the factor $1/\hat\sigma(x)$; because $\hat\sigma$ estimates the mean absolute residual, this isolation is only approximate. An exploratory *two-piece* variant uses side-specific scales below and above $\hat\mu(x)$. A histogram density comes from a multiclass gradient-boosted classifier over 40 equal-mass target bins [cf. @sesia2021conformal; @guha2024conformal].

## Implementation {#sec:implementation}

*Strata.* The primary stratification uses the training-target quantiles $0.10, 0.25, 0.75, 0.90$ ($K=5$: lower tail, lower shoulder, bulk, upper shoulder, upper tail). With tied targets, coinciding cut points are merged, so realised strata can differ from the nominal ones; the sensitivity analyses repeat the main comparisons on datasets whose realised tail strata are close to nominal.

*Exact scores and the domain.* Calibration scores are computed exactly at the observed target, $S_i=-\hat f(Y_i\mid X_i)$. Sets are restricted to a bounded domain $D=[\min y-0.05r,\max y+0.05r]$ of the training targets ($r$ the training range), intersected with $[0,\infty)$ when all training targets are non-negative. A point with $Y\notin D$ receives the score $+\infty$, stays in its stratum, and counts as not covered. Membership of a test target is decided by the same exact rule, $Y_{n+1}\in\hat C(X_{n+1})$ iff $s(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})\le\hat q_{k(Y_{n+1})}$, so the lower bound of Theorem 1 applies to the reported coverage, except that a test target outside $D$ always counts as a miss. When $\hat q_k=+\infty$ the reported set is $B_k\cap D$. We report the fraction of test targets outside $D$ and how often $\hat q_k=+\infty$ occurs.

*Set geometry.* Only the size, number of pieces, hull and tail alerts of a set are computed numerically. For a test point, $\hat C(x)\cap B_k=\{\hat\mu(x)+\hat\sigma(x)u:\hat g(u)\ge-\hat q_k\hat\sigma(x)\}\cap B_k$. We tabulate $\hat g$ on a non-uniform standardised grid with analytic $t$ tails, interpolate linearly, and split every cell exactly at the stratum boundaries and the bounds of $D$. A four times finer grid changes mean set sizes by at most {{J:metrics_v3.json|grid_max_rel_change|pct}} and individual set sizes by at most {{J:metrics_v3.json|grid_max_rel_change_point|pct}} (Supplementary Section S3, Table S4). The same construction is used for every location–scale baseline (DLS, DCP, HPD-split, S-HPD, SDLC-N).

**Algorithm 1.** Stratified density-level conformal prediction (SDLC).

> *Input:* proper training set $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$; calibration set $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$; stratum levels $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_K$; stratum probabilities (e.g. $0.10,0.25,0.75,0.90$).
> *Output:* prediction set $\hat C(x)$ for any test input $x$.
>
> 1. Fit the mean model $\hat\mu$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and compute three-fold out-of-fold residuals $Y-\hat\mu_{-j}(X)$.
> 2. Fit the scale model $\tilde\sigma$ on all absolute out-of-fold residuals and three cross-fitted scale models; set $\hat\sigma=\max\{\tilde\sigma,\varsigma\}$.
> 3. Standardise the out-of-fold residuals with the cross-fitted scales and fit the noise density $\hat g$ by Eq. (9); form $\hat f(y\mid x)$ by Eq. (8).
> 4. Compute the cut points from the training targets and define the strata $B_1,\dots,B_K$ and the domain $D$.
> 5. **for** each $(X_i,Y_i)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$ **do** $S_i\leftarrow-\hat f(Y_i\mid X_i)$ if $Y_i\in D$, else $S_i\leftarrow+\infty$; assign $i$ to stratum $k(Y_i)$.
> 6. **for** $k=1,\dots,K$ **do** $\hat q_k\leftarrow$ the $\lceil (n_k+1)(1-\alpha_k)\rceil$-th smallest score in stratum $k$ (Eq. 3; $+\infty$ if this rank exceeds $n_k$).
> 7. **return** $\hat C(x)=\bigcup_{k}\{y\in B_k\cap D:\hat f(y\mid x)\ge-\hat q_k\}$, computed stratum-exactly on the standardised grid.

## Computational complexity {#sec:complexity}

Fitting (steps 1–3) requires eight gradient-boosted regressions: one mean model, three out-of-fold mean models, one scale model and three cross-fitted scale models. The kernel density estimate costs $O(N+G_g\log G_g)$ by linear binning and FFT convolution on $G_g$ grid points. Calibration (steps 5–6) costs $O(n\log n)$. Prediction (step 7) inverts the density level on each of the $R$ monotone runs of the piecewise-linear $\hat g$ (typically a handful) within each of the $K$ strata, at $O(KR\log G)$ per test point, so the prediction cost is linear in the number of test points, and one fit serves every $\alpha$. Wall-clock times are reported in Section [-@sec:robustness].

## Two practical variants

*Hull variant (SDLC-hull).* When an interval is required, the convex hull of $\hat C(x)$ is reported. It inherits (5), but not the upper bound of Theorem 1 or the optimality of Proposition 2.

*Floor variant (SDLC-floor).* A user may want the efficiency of a single marginal threshold while protecting rare strata at a lower level $1-\beta$. The set $\hat C_{\mathrm{floor}}(x)=\{y: s(x,y)\le\max(\hat q^{\mathrm{marg}}_\alpha,\hat q_{k(y)}(\beta))\}$, with $\hat q^{\mathrm{marg}}_\alpha$ the ordinary split-conformal quantile at level $\alpha$ and $\hat q_k(\beta)$ the stratified quantile (3) at level $\beta$, contains both the marginal set and the stratified set at level $\beta$, so it has marginal coverage $\ge 1-\alpha$ and stratum-wise coverage $\ge 1-\beta$ in finite samples; we use $\beta=2\alpha$. This simple, conservative rule is not a contribution; level-optimising schemes [@wang2023equal; @kiyani2024length] are principled alternatives.

## Relevance-weighted coverage without strata: RW-DLC {#sec:rw}

Stratified calibration protects each stratum, but its protection ends at the resolution of the strata: outcomes in the top 1% lie inside the upper-tail stratum and are covered only on average with the rest of that stratum, and refining the strata quickly leaves too few calibration points per stratum. The imbalanced-regression literature describes the importance of outcomes not by strata but by a *relevance function* $\phi:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)$, for example the inverse of the target density [@torgo2007utility; @steininger2021denseweight]. We therefore also consider *relevance-weighted coverage*
$$\mathrm{Cov}_\phi(C)=\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[\phi(Y)\,\mathbf{1}\{Y\in C(X)\}\big]}{\mathbb{E}[\phi(Y)]}, \tag{10}$$
which, for $\phi=1/p_Y$, is coverage averaged uniformly over the range of the target, the continuous analogue of the macro-coverage that @bhattacharyya2026macro study for long-tailed classification. A stratum-wise guarantee for every stratum implies (10) for every $\phi$ that is constant on strata; (10) instead gives one averaged guarantee for a continuous relevance, without choosing strata.

Let $\phi$ be fixed given $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$, let $S_i=s(X_i,Y_i)$ be the calibration scores with weights $\phi_i=\phi(Y_i)$ and $W=\sum_i\phi_i$, and define for a candidate $y$
$$q(y)=\inf\Big\{s:\ \sum_{i=1}^n\phi_i\,\mathbf{1}\{S_i\le s\}\ \ge\ (1-\alpha)\big(W+\phi(y)\big)\Big\},\qquad \hat C_\phi(x)=\{y:\ s(x,y)\le q(y)\}, \tag{11}$$
with $q(y)=+\infty$ when no such $s$ exists. The candidate's own weight sits at $+\infty$, as in weighted conformal prediction under covariate shift [@tibshirani2019conformal] and label shift [@podkopaev2021distribution].

**Theorem 2 (relevance-weighted validity).** *If $(X_1,Y_1),\ldots,(X_n,Y_n),(X,Y)$ are i.i.d. given $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and $0<\mathbb{E}\phi(Y)<\infty$, then $\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Cov}_\phi(\hat C_\phi)\big]\ge 1-\alpha$, the expectation being over $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$. Equivalently, $\hat C_\phi$ has coverage at least $1-\alpha$ for a test point drawn from the target-shifted law $Q$ with $dQ/dP=\phi(y)/\mathbb{E}\phi(Y)$.*

The proof (Appendix A) applies the weighted-exchangeability argument of @tibshirani2019conformal with the likelihood ratio $\phi(y)/\mathbb{E}\phi(Y)$, which depends only on the target. Two consequences matter in practice. First, (11) gives $q(y)=+\infty$ exactly when $\phi(y)>\alpha W/(1-\alpha)$: a candidate whose relevance exceeds the fraction $\alpha/(1-\alpha)$ of the total calibration relevance is always included. Rare targets are protected without strata, but only to the extent that the calibration set contains comparable relevance mass, which the Kish effective sample size $W^2/\sum_i\phi_i^2$ summarises. Second, Theorem 2 is also an exact guarantee under a target shift whose density ratio is proportional to $\phi$.

**Proposition 4 (size-optimal relevance-weighted sets).** *Under the density assumption of Proposition 2, among all set-valued functions with $\mathrm{Cov}_\phi(C)\ge1-\alpha$, the expected Lebesgue size $\mathbb{E}|C(X)|$ is minimised by $C^\star_\phi(x)=\{y:\ \phi(y)f(y\mid x)\ge t\}$, with $t$ chosen so that the constraint holds with equality (assuming the distribution of $\phi(Y)f(Y\mid X)$ has no atom at $t$).*

The proof is the Neyman–Pearson argument of Proposition 2 with the measure $\phi(y)f(y\mid x)\,dy\,dP_X$ in place of the stratum measure; its discrete counterpart for class-weighted coverage is given by @bhattacharyya2026macro. The proposition yields the score of *relevance-weighted density-level conformal prediction* (RW-DLC), $s(x,y)=-\phi(y)\hat f(y\mid x)$, calibrated by (11) and using the same density estimate (8) as SDLC. Relevance thus enters twice: it tilts the score towards relevant outcomes, which shapes the sets, and it weights the calibration, which delivers the guarantee. In the experiments $\phi$ is fitted on the training targets only, $\phi_U(y)=1/\max\{\hat p_Y(y),\tau\}$ ("uniform over the target range") or $\phi_H=\phi_U^{1/2}$, with $\hat p_Y$ a Gaussian kernel density estimate and $\tau=0.01\max_i\hat p_Y(Y_i)$ (Section [-@sec:setup]).
