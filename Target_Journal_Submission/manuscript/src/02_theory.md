# Problem formulation {#sec:problem}

## Target-conditional coverage

Let $(X,Y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}$ have joint law $P$, with marginal law $P_X$ of the covariates. A *prediction set* is a map $C$ from $\mathcal{X}$ to subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ whose graph $\{(x,y):y\in C(x)\}$ is jointly measurable. Split conformal prediction [@papadopoulos2002inductive; @lei2018distribution] guarantees *marginal* coverage $P\{Y\in C(X)\}\ge 1-\alpha$, and much recent work targets approximate *covariate-conditional* coverage $P\{Y\in C(X)\mid X=x\}\approx 1-\alpha$ [@romano2019conformalized; @izbicki2022cdsplit; @gibbs2025conformal], which cannot be guaranteed in finite samples without further assumptions [@barber2021limits].

In imbalanced regression the outcomes of interest are rare target values. Let $e_1<\dots<e_{K-1}$ be cut points and $B_1=(-\infty,e_1)$, $B_k=[e_{k-1},e_k)$ for $1<k<K$, $B_K=[e_{K-1},\infty)$ the resulting *target strata*, for instance the lower tail, the bulk and the upper tail, with $p_k=P(Y\in B_k)>0$. We call
$$\mathrm{Cov}_k(C)=P\{Y\in C(X)\mid Y\in B_k\},\qquad k=1,\ldots,K, \tag{1}$$
the *stratum-wise* (target-conditional) coverage of $C$. It answers the question that matters when extremes are costly: *when the outcome is extreme, how often does the prediction set contain it?* It is the regression analogue of class-conditional coverage in classification [@vovk2005alrw; @vovk2013conditional; @sadinle2019least]. Because $\sum_k p_k\,\mathrm{Cov}_k(C)=P\{Y\in C(X)\}$, marginal coverage only constrains a $p$-weighted average of the $\mathrm{Cov}_k$: rare strata can be under-covered while the bulk is over-covered. The next proposition shows that this is not merely a defect of estimation that better covariate-conditional methods would remove.

## Covariate-conditional validity does not imply target-conditional validity

**Proposition 1.** *Let $X\sim N(0,\tau^2)$ and $Y\mid X\sim N(X,\sigma^2)$ with $\tau^2+\sigma^2=1$ and $0<\tau^2<1$, so that $R^2=\tau^2$ is the fraction of variance explained by the optimal predictor. Let $z=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)$ and $C^{\mathrm{cov}}(x)=[x-z\sigma,\,x+z\sigma]$, which is, up to Lebesgue-null sets, the unique set of minimal length with $P\{Y\in C(x)\mid X=x\}\ge 1-\alpha$ for every $x$. Then*
$$P\{Y\in C^{\mathrm{cov}}(X)\mid Y=y\}=\Phi\!\left(\frac{z\sigma-(1-R^2)y}{s}\right)-\Phi\!\left(\frac{-z\sigma-(1-R^2)y}{s}\right),\qquad s=\sigma\sqrt{R^2}, \tag{2}$$
*an even function of $y$ that is strictly decreasing in $|y|$ and tends to $0$ as $|y|\to\infty$.*

*Proof.* $(X,Y)$ is bivariate normal with $\mathrm{Var}(Y)=1$ and $\mathrm{Cov}(X,Y)=\tau^2$, hence $X\mid Y=y\sim N(\tau^2y,\tau^2\sigma^2)$ and $Y-X\mid Y=y\sim N((1-\tau^2)y,\tau^2\sigma^2)$. Since $Y\in C^{\mathrm{cov}}(X)$ iff $|Y-X|\le z\sigma$, (2) follows. Write $a=z\sigma>0$ and $m=(1-R^2)y$. The function $h(m)=\Phi((a-m)/s)-\Phi((-a-m)/s)$ is even because $\Phi(-u)=1-\Phi(u)$, and for $m>0$, $h'(m)=-\{\phi((a-m)/s)-\phi((a+m)/s)\}/s<0$ because $|a-m|<a+m$. As $1-R^2>0$, (2) is strictly decreasing in $|y|$ and vanishes as $|y|\to\infty$. Minimality and uniqueness of $C^{\mathrm{cov}}(x)$ follow because the $N(x,\sigma^2)$ density is symmetric and strictly unimodal. $\square$

Table [-@tbl:prop1] integrates (2) over strata of $Y$. For a predictor that explains half of the variance, the oracle covariate-conditional interval covers only {{prop1.upper10_r05}} of the top-10% outcomes and {{prop1.upper1_r05}} of the top-1% outcomes, while it covers {{prop1.central_r05}} of the central 80%; even with $R^2=0.9$ it covers {{prop1.upper10_r09}} of the top decile. The mechanism is general: an extreme outcome is typically the sum of an extreme signal and an extreme noise realisation, and the shortest covariate-conditionally valid set spends its coverage in the bulk of each conditional distribution, where such outcomes rarely fall. Covariate-conditional validity does not *rule out* stratum-wise validity (in the model of Proposition 1 a convexity argument shows that sets with both properties exist), but the two criteria select different sets, and to guarantee coverage in rare strata a set must be calibrated against the distribution of $(X,Y)$ *given* $Y\in B_k$. This is what stratified calibration does.

# Stratified calibration and the optimal score {#sec:method}

## Stratified (Mondrian) calibration

Data are split into a proper training set $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and a calibration set $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}=\{(X_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$. Using $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ only, we fit a nonconformity score $s:\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}$ (small values = conforming) and choose the cut points, e.g. at quantiles of the training targets. Let $k(y)$ be the stratum containing $y$, $I_k=\{i:Y_i\in B_k\}$, $n_k=|I_k|$, and, for levels $\alpha_k\in(0,1)$,
$$\hat q_k=\text{the }\lceil (n_k+1)(1-\alpha_k)\rceil\text{-th smallest value of }\{s(X_i,Y_i):i\in I_k\}, \tag{3}$$
with $\hat q_k=+\infty$ if that rank exceeds $n_k$, which happens exactly when $n_k<1/\alpha_k-1$ (i.e. $n_k\le 8$ for $\alpha_k=0.1$ and $n_k\le 18$ for $\alpha_k=0.05$). The stratified prediction set is
$$\hat C(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}: s(x,y)\le \hat q_{k(y)}\}=\bigcup_{k=1}^K\{y\in B_k: s(x,y)\le\hat q_k\}. \tag{4}$$
The threshold applied to a candidate value $y$ is that of the stratum *of that candidate*, not of a prediction; this is what makes the guarantee target-conditional. Equation (4) is an inductive Mondrian conformal predictor whose taxonomy assigns each example to the stratum of its label [@vovk2005alrw; @vovk2013conditional; @toccaceli2019combination], and it is not new for regression: with the absolute-residual score it is the bin-conditional conformal prediction (BCCP) of @randahl2026bin, who report either the union (4) or its convex hull, and @wang2023equal calibrate within equal-mass bins of $Y$ to equalise coverage across groups. What this paper adds to (3)–(4) is the choice of score (Theorem 2), the floor variant, and a precise account of the guarantee under target shift (Corollary 1). When every sub-level set $\{y:s(x,y)\le q\}$ is an interval, as for residual or quantile scores, $\hat C(x)$ is a union of at most $K$ intervals; density scores with a multimodal estimate can produce more pieces.

The following guarantee is the standard label-conditional Mondrian property, restated for an arbitrary score with the edge cases used later; its proof is given in Appendix A for completeness.

**Theorem 1 (stratum-wise validity of Mondrian calibration).** *Suppose that, conditionally on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$, the points $(X_1,Y_1),\ldots,(X_n,Y_n),(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})$ are exchangeable, and let $p_k=P(Y_{n+1}\in B_k\mid\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}})$. Then for every $k$ with $p_k>0$,*
$$P\{Y_{n+1}\in\hat C(X_{n+1})\mid Y_{n+1}\in B_k,\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}\}\ \ge\ 1-\alpha_k, \tag{5}$$
*and consequently $P\{Y_{n+1}\in\hat C(X_{n+1})\mid\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}\}\ge 1-\sum_kp_k\alpha_k\ge 1-\max_k\alpha_k$. If the scores within stratum $k$ are almost surely distinct, then also $P\{Y_{n+1}\in\hat C(X_{n+1})\mid Y_{n+1}\in B_k,n_k,\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}\}\le 1-\alpha_k+1/(n_k+1)$. The levels $\alpha_k$ may depend on $n_k$, and the score and strata may be any functions of $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$.*

The guarantee holds for *any* score. A useful consequence concerns shifts of the target distribution between calibration and deployment.

**Corollary 1 (target shift).** *Let $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$ be i.i.d. from $P$ and let the test point be drawn from $Q$, independently of $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$ and $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$. Write $P_k=P(\cdot\mid Y\in B_k)$ and $Q_k=Q(\cdot\mid Y\in B_k)$ for the within-stratum laws of the pair $(X,Y)$ and $\pi_k=Q(Y\in B_k)$; assume $p_k>0$ whenever $\pi_k>0$.*
*(i) If $Q_k=P_k$ for every $k$ with $\pi_k>0$ (the shift re-weights whole strata), then $Q\{Y\in\hat C(X)\mid Y\in B_k\}\ge 1-\alpha_k$ for every such $k$, and $Q\{Y\in\hat C(X)\}\ge 1-\sum_k\pi_k\alpha_k$ whatever the $\pi_k$.*
*(ii) For an arbitrary shift, $Q\{Y\in\hat C(X)\mid Y\in B_k\}\ge 1-\alpha_k-d_{\mathrm{TV}}(Q_k,P_k)$.*
*(iii) Under label shift, $Q(\cdot\mid Y=y)=P(\cdot\mid Y=y)$ with density ratio $w=dQ_Y/dP_Y$; let $m_k$ and $M_k$ be the essential infimum and supremum of $w$ on $B_k$, $0<m_k\le M_k<\infty$. Then $Q\{Y\notin\hat C(X)\mid Y\in B_k\}\le(M_k/m_k)\,\alpha_k$ and $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(Q_k,P_k)\le\tanh\{\log(M_k/m_k)/4\}$.*

The proof is in Appendix A. Part (i) is the stratified analogue of the fact that class-conditional coverage survives label shift in classification [cf. @podkopaev2021distribution]. With a continuous outcome, however, a label shift generally also changes the distribution of $Y$ *within* a stratum, and exactness then requires that the joint law of $(X,Y)$ inside each stratum be unchanged; a stable covariate distribution alone is not sufficient. Parts (ii)–(iii) quantify the degradation otherwise: it is small when the density ratio varies little across each stratum, which favours narrow strata at the price of fewer calibration points per stratum, and the bound in (iii) becomes vacuous for an open tail stratum under an unbounded tilt. Marginally calibrated sets enjoy no analogue of (i): their coverage under $Q$ is $\sum_k\pi_k\mathrm{Cov}_k$, which can be as low as $\min_k\mathrm{Cov}_k$. Unlike re-weighting approaches [@podkopaev2021distribution; @tibshirani2019conformal; @yi2025distribution; @lee2026split], stratified calibration needs no estimate of the shift, but it is exact only for shifts that act on the stratum probabilities. Experiment 4 examines continuous tilts, which also re-weight within strata.

## The smallest sets with stratum-wise coverage

Theorem 1 leaves the score free, so validity is never the issue; the score determines how large the sets are. For a prediction set $C$ let $|C(x)|$ be its Lebesgue measure and $\mathbb{E}|C(X)|=\int|C(x)|\,P_X(dx)$ its expected size.

**Theorem 2 (size-optimal stratum-wise sets).** *Suppose the conditional law of $Y$ given $X$ has a Lebesgue density $f(y\mid x)$, that $f(Y\mid X)>0$ almost surely, and that for each $k$ the random variable $f(Y\mid X)$ has a continuous distribution conditionally on $Y\in B_k$. Fix $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_K\in(0,1)$. Then there are thresholds $t_k>0$ with $P\{f(Y\mid X)\ge t_k\mid Y\in B_k\}=1-\alpha_k$, and the set*
$$C^\star(x)=\bigcup_{k=1}^K\{y\in B_k: f(y\mid x)\ge t_k\} \tag{6}$$
*satisfies $\mathrm{Cov}_k(C^\star)=1-\alpha_k$ for all $k$. Every prediction set $C$ with $\mathrm{Cov}_k(C)\ge 1-\alpha_k$ for all $k$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}|C(X)\cap B_k|\ge\mathbb{E}|C^\star(X)\cap B_k|$ for every $k$, and hence $\mathbb{E}|C(X)|\ge\mathbb{E}|C^\star(X)|$.*

*Proof.* Existence: $G_k(t)=P\{f(Y\mid X)\ge t\mid Y\in B_k\}$ is continuous and non-increasing with $G_k(0^+)=1$ and $G_k(t)\to0$ as $t\to\infty$, so the intermediate value theorem gives $t_k>0$ with $G_k(t_k)=1-\alpha_k$. Optimality: write $A=\{(x,y):y\in C(x)\}$, $A^\star$ likewise, $A_k=A\cap(\mathcal{X}\times B_k)$, $A_k^\star=A^\star\cap(\mathcal{X}\times B_k)$ and $|A|_P=\iint\mathbf{1}_A(x,y)\,dy\,P_X(dx)$, so that $\mathbb{E}|C(X)\cap B_k|=|A_k|_P$ and $p_k\mathrm{Cov}_k(C)=\iint\mathbf{1}_{A_k}f(y\mid x)\,dy\,P_X(dx)$. Since $|\{y:f(y\mid x)\ge t_k\}|\le 1/t_k$, $|A_k^\star|_P<\infty$; if $|A_k|_P=\infty$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise the integrand of
$$\Delta_k=\iint\left(\mathbf{1}_{A_k^\star}-\mathbf{1}_{A_k}\right)\left(f(y\mid x)-t_k\right)dy\,P_X(dx)$$
is non-negative pointwise: on $A_k^\star\setminus A_k$ we have $f\ge t_k$, and on $A_k\setminus A_k^\star$, where $y\in B_k$, we have $f<t_k$. Hence $\Delta_k\ge 0$, i.e. $t_k(|A_k^\star|_P-|A_k|_P)\le p_k\{\mathrm{Cov}_k(C^\star)-\mathrm{Cov}_k(C)\}\le 0$, and $t_k>0$ gives $|A_k^\star|_P\le|A_k|_P$. $\square$

Theorem 2 is the regression counterpart of the Neyman–Pearson argument behind least-ambiguous set-valued classifiers with class-specific error levels [@sadinle2019least; @lei2014classification]; with $K=1$ it gives the optimality of single-threshold conditional-density level sets under marginal coverage that underlies HPD-split [@izbicki2022cdsplit] and, without covariates, conformal density level sets [@lei2013distribution]. Since the inequality $\Delta_k\ge0$ holds for every realisation of a set built from data independent of the test point, $C^\star$ also minimises expected size among data-dependent (e.g. conformal) sets whose stratum-wise coverage, averaged over the data, is at least $1-\alpha_k$. Four consequences guide the method.

*The optimal score.* The size-optimal score is the conditional density, $s(x,y)=-f(y\mid x)$, combined with stratum-specific thresholds; a single threshold is optimal only for marginal coverage.

*The price of stratum-wise validity.* Every set that satisfies the stratum constraints has marginal coverage at least $1-\bar\alpha$, $\bar\alpha=\sum_kp_k\alpha_k$. Hence $\mathbb{E}|C^\star(X)|$ is at least the expected size of the size-optimal marginal set at level $1-\bar\alpha$, a single-threshold density level set; under the assumptions of Theorem 2 the two coincide if and only if $t_1=\dots=t_K$, i.e. if and only if that marginal set already has coverage $1-\alpha_k$ in every stratum. With a common level $\alpha_k=\alpha$ this difference is the price, in size, of stratum-wise validity.

*Shape.* $C^\star(x)$ need not be an interval even when $f(\cdot\mid x)$ is unimodal: thresholds differ across strata (typically $t_k$ is smaller in a rare tail stratum), so $C^\star(x)$ can contain a piece of a tail stratum while excluding part of an adjacent stratum.

*Scope.* The optimality refers to size averaged over the covariate law $P_X$ of the calibration population; under a shifted $Q$, $C^\star$ remains valid in the sense of Corollary 1(i) but is no longer size-optimal. For targets with atoms (ties, zero inflation) Theorem 1 is unaffected, whereas Lebesgue size is degenerate at an atom and the size-optimal set contains every atom; on such data a smooth density score is valid but not size-optimal.

## The proposed method: stratified density-level conformal prediction (SDLC)

SDLC instantiates (3)–(4) with the plug-in score $s(x,y)=-\hat f(y\mid x)$, where $\hat f$ is estimated on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$. By Theorem 1 the guarantee (5) holds for any $\hat f$; Theorem 2 motivates the score. With $\hat f=f$ and growing strata, $-\hat q_k\to t_k$ and $\hat C$ approaches $C^\star$; we expect, but do not prove, the same for a consistent $\hat f$ (a result in the spirit of @lei2013distribution and @izbicki2022cdsplit would require continuity of the law of $f(Y\mid X)$ at each $t_k$). We use the semiparametric location–scale estimator
$$\hat f(y\mid x)=\frac{1}{\hat\sigma(x)}\,\hat g\!\left(\frac{y-\hat\mu(x)}{\hat\sigma(x)}\right), \tag{7}$$
where $\hat\mu$ is a gradient-boosted regression of $Y$ on $X$, $\hat\sigma(x)=\max\{\tilde\sigma(x),10^{-3}\,\mathrm{sd}(Y_{\mathrm{tr}})\}$ with $\tilde\sigma$ a gradient-boosted regression of the absolute *out-of-fold* residuals $|Y-\hat\mu_{-j}(X)|$ (three folds, as in locally weighted conformal prediction [@lei2018distribution]), and $\hat g$ is a Gaussian kernel density estimate (Silverman bandwidth) of the standardised out-of-fold residuals. The noise density $\hat g$ is nonparametric, so (7) captures skewness and heavy tails of the error distribution, while heteroscedasticity enters through $\hat\sigma$. If $Y=\mu(X)+\sigma(X)\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon$ independent of $X$, (7) can be consistent; otherwise SDLC targets the optimal sets of the best location–scale approximation, which affects size but never validity. Two alternative estimators are evaluated as secondary results: an exploratory *two-piece* variant that replaces $\hat\sigma$ by $\hat\sigma_-(x)$ below $\hat\mu(x)$ and $\hat\sigma_+(x)$ above it (separate scale models for negative and positive out-of-fold residuals, with $\hat g$ the kernel estimate of the side-wise standardised residuals; this is a normalised density that captures covariate-dependent asymmetry of scale), and a fully nonparametric histogram density from a multiclass gradient-boosted classifier over 40 equal-mass target bins (a "regression-as-classification" density, cf. @sesia2021conformal and @guha2024conformal).

## Implementation {#sec:implementation}

*Strata.* The primary stratification uses the training-target quantiles $0.10, 0.25, 0.75, 0.90$ ($K=5$: lower tail, lower shoulder, bulk, upper shoulder, upper tail). With tied targets coinciding cut points are merged and empty strata removed, so the realised number of strata and their masses can differ from the nominal values; realised values are reported per dataset.

*Exact scores.* Calibration scores are computed exactly at the observed target, $S_i=-\hat g(\hat Z_i)/\hat\sigma(X_i)$ with $\hat Z_i=(Y_i-\hat\mu(X_i))/\hat\sigma(X_i)$. Sets are restricted to a bounded domain $D=[\min y-0.05r,\max y+0.05r]$ of the training targets ($r$ the training range): a point with $Y\notin D$ receives the score $+\infty$, stays in its stratum, and counts as not covered. Membership of a test target is decided by the same exact rule, $Y_{n+1}\in\hat C(X_{n+1})$ iff $s(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})\le\hat q_{k(Y_{n+1})}$, so Theorem 1 applies to the implemented procedure, with one exception: when $\hat q_k=+\infty$ the reported set is $B_k\cap D$ rather than $B_k$. We report the fraction of test targets outside $D$ and how often $\hat q_k=+\infty$ occurs.

*Set geometry.* Only the size, the number of pieces and the hull of a set are computed numerically. Because $\hat C(x)\cap B_k=\{\hat\mu(x)+\hat\sigma(x)u:\hat g(u)\ge-\hat q_k\hat\sigma(x)\}\cap B_k$, the level sets are evaluated on a per-$x$ grid $y=\hat\mu(x)+\hat\sigma(x)u_j$ of 2048 standardised points, i.e. at a resolution relative to the conditional scale, with the regions beyond the tabulated range of $\hat g$ treated analytically. The same exact construction is used for every location–scale-family baseline (DLS, DCP, HPD-split, S-HPD). Histogram-density methods are evaluated on the grid of the histogram itself. (The pre-registered implementation evaluated all density-based sets on a fixed grid of 200 equal-mass cells of the *marginal* target distribution; Section [-@sec:protocol] explains why it was replaced.)

> **Algorithm 1 (SDLC).** *Input:* $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$, $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$, levels $\alpha_k$, strata probabilities. (i) Fit $\hat\mu$, out-of-fold residuals, $\hat\sigma$ and $\hat g$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tr}}$; build the strata from training targets. (ii) For each calibration point compute $S_i=-\hat f(Y_i\mid X_i)$ (or $+\infty$ outside $D$) and its stratum $k(Y_i)$. (iii) Compute $\hat q_k$ by (3) within each stratum. (iv) For a test $x$, return $\{y\in D:\hat f(y\mid x)\ge-\hat q_{k(y)}\}$. *Cost:* fitting is that of three gradient-boosted regressions; calibration is $O(n\log n)$; prediction is $O(G)$ per test point for $G$ grid points.

## Two practical variants

*Floor variant (SDLC-floor).* Practitioners may want the efficiency of a single marginal threshold while protecting rare strata at a lower level $1-\beta$. Define $\hat C_{\mathrm{floor}}(x)=\{y: s(x,y)\le\max(\hat q^{\mathrm{marg}}_\alpha,\hat q_{k(y)}(\beta))\}$, where $\hat q^{\mathrm{marg}}_\alpha$ is the ordinary split-conformal quantile at level $\alpha$ and $\hat q_k(\beta)$ the stratified quantile (3) at level $\beta$. Since $\hat C_{\mathrm{floor}}$ contains both the marginal set and the stratified set at level $\beta$, it has marginal coverage $\ge 1-\alpha$ and stratum-wise coverage $\ge 1-\beta$ simultaneously, in finite samples and for any $\beta\in(0,1)$; we use $\beta=2\alpha$. The rule is conservative: the size-optimal set under both constraints lowers the stratum thresholds only where a floor binds and would raise the common threshold to restore exact marginal coverage.

*Hull variant (SDLC-hull).* When an interval is required, the convex hull of $\hat C(x)$ is reported. It contains $\hat C(x)$ and therefore inherits (5); the upper bound of Theorem 1 and the optimality of Theorem 2 do not carry over, and when some $\hat q_k=+\infty$ the hull spans most of $D$.
