**S8.3 Secondary and post hoc analyses of the held-out replication.**

Post hoc checks of the held-out collection (main text, Datasets; Table S17b) leave every C-H3 decision unchanged. With the duplicate pair merged (16 independent targets), SDLC was smaller than S-NCP on {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.merged/S-NCP/wins|int}} of 16 datasets (ratio {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.merged/S-NCP/geo_ratio|f3}}, Holm $p$ = {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.merged/S-NCP/p_holm|f3}}) and than BCCP on {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.merged/BCCP/wins|int}} (ratio {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.merged/BCCP/geo_ratio|f3}}). On the {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.n_continuous|int}} held-out datasets with continuous targets (at least 100 distinct values, no atom above 5%) it was smaller than S-NCP and S-HPD on all of them (ratios {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.continuous/S-NCP/geo_ratio|f3}} and {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.continuous/S-HPD/geo_ratio|f3}}), and the same held for the {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.n_nominal_tails|int}} datasets whose tail strata had their nominal mass. The small advantage over S-NCP is therefore a result for continuous targets; on discretised or atom-heavy targets the two scores were indistinguishable.

The secondary results repeated the trade-off seen on CTR23. SDLC covered {{cs.SDLC-LS.top1}} of the top 1%, against {{cs.S-NCP.top1}} for S-NCP (range over datasets {{J:statistics_review05.json|held-out.top1_SDLC/min|f2}}–{{J:statistics_review05.json|held-out.top1_SDLC/max|f2}}; top 1% defined as $y\ge q_{0.99}$ of the training targets, as everywhere in the main pipeline), and its worst coverage over deciles of $\hat\sigma(x)$ was {{cs.SDLC-LS.sigma_decile_worst}}, against {{cs.S-NCP.sigma_decile_worst}}. The price of stratification relative to DLS was {{conf.price}}. The pre-specified secondary analyses (Supplementary Table S16) were less favourable for the interval forms: SDLC-hull was smaller than BCCP-hull (ratio {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.hull/SDLC-hull vs BCCP-hull/geo_ratio|f3}}, Holm $p$ = {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.hull/SDLC-hull vs BCCP-hull/p_holm|f3}}) but not than S-NCP-hull (ratio {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.hull/SDLC-hull vs S-NCP-hull/geo_ratio|f3}}, {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.hull/SDLC-hull vs S-NCP-hull/wins|int}}/{{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.hull/SDLC-hull vs S-NCP-hull/losses|int}} datasets, $p$ = {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.hull/SDLC-hull vs S-NCP-hull/p_holm|f3}}). Post hoc, SDLC was equivalent to S-HPD within ±5% (TOST $p$ = {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.tost5/S-HPD/p_tost|f3}}), equivalence with S-NCP narrowly failed ($p$ = {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.tost5/S-NCP/p_tost|f3}}), and SDLC was reliably smaller than S-NCP on {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.reliability/S-NCP/reliably_smaller|int}} of {{conf.n_eval}} datasets and reliably larger on {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.reliability/S-NCP/reliably_larger|int}}. On the {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.n_skewed|int}} held-out targets with $|$skewness$|>1$, the advantage over S-NCP and S-HPD disappeared (ratios {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.skew_groups/skewed/S-NCP/geo_ratio|f3}} and {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.skew_groups/skewed/S-HPD/geo_ratio|f3}}), while that over BCCP and S-CQR remained (Supplementary Table S17). Among scores that use $\hat\sigma$, the choice matters little for size; the clear gains are over BCCP and stratified CQR.

**S8.4 Coverage under target shift.**

{{TABLE:shift|Coverage under target shift ($\alpha=0.1$; means over datasets). *Positive control*: the upper stratum is up-weighted by 5 and the lower stratum down-weighted by 0.5, without changing the within-stratum laws. *Tilts*: importance weights $\omega_\gamma(y)=\exp(\gamma\zeta_y)$ with $\zeta_y$ the normal score of $y$ under the training distribution; the coverage of the upper calibration stratum is shown for all datasets and for the {{shift_n_ess_ge_50}} datasets whose effective sample size is at least 50.}}

Under the positive control, which re-weights whole strata, the stratum-wise coverage of every stratified method was unchanged, as Corollary 1(i) states (Supplementary Table S22, the shift figure of the main text). Their marginal coverage stayed at the nominal level ({{T:T8_shift|method=SDLC|pc_marginal|f3}} for SDLC), whereas that of the marginal methods fell to {{T:T8_shift|method=SCP|pc_marginal|f3}}–{{T:T8_shift|method=Mondrian-ŷ|pc_marginal|f3}}. Continuous tilts towards the upper tail also re-weight targets *within* strata, so Corollary 1(i) does not apply. Under a moderate tilt ($\gamma=1$) the stratified methods kept {{T:T8_shift|method=BCCP|up1_upper|f3}}–{{T:T8_shift|method=S-NCP|up1_upper|f3}} coverage of the upper stratum, against {{T:T8_shift|method=SCP|up1_upper|f3}}–{{T:T8_shift|method=DCP|up1_upper|f3}} for the marginal methods. Under a strong tilt ($\gamma=2$) every method lost coverage. The stratified methods lost less ({{T:T8_shift|method=S-CQR|up2_upper|f3}}–{{T:T8_shift|method=S-NCP|up2_upper|f3}}, against {{T:T8_shift|method=SCP|up2_upper|f3}}–{{T:T8_shift|method=DCP|up2_upper|f3}}). Among them, SDLC ({{T:T8_shift|method=SDLC|up2_upper|f3}}) was below S-NCP and S-HPD ({{T:T8_shift|method=S-NCP|up2_upper|f3}}), which is the same reallocation away from the most extreme outcomes seen above; Remark A.1 (Appendix A) shows why coverage is lowest near the outer end of a tail stratum, where the tilts move mass. The effective sample size of the $\gamma=2$ weights was small (median {{ess_up2_median}}, minimum {{ess_up2_min}}), but restricting the analysis to the {{shift_n_ess_ge_50}} datasets where it was at least 50 left the ordering unchanged.

**S8.5 Upper-tail alerts.**

{{TABLE:alerts|Upper-tail alerts ($\alpha=0.1$; means over datasets): recall, alert rate and precision, the number of datasets with recall at least 0.9, and the expected cost per unit when a missed extreme outcome costs $c$ times a false alert. Set-based alerts (SCP to SDLC-floor) versus dedicated rules built from the same models: conformal tail classifiers (TC-LS, TC-H), conformal selection with FDR level $q_{\mathrm{FDR}}$ (CS-BH-$q_{\mathrm{FDR}}$) and the point-prediction rule (Point).}}

The preferable rule depends on the cost of a miss relative to a false alert. With equal costs, the rules that raise few alerts were cheapest: the point rule and conformal selection (expected cost {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=Point|cost_r1|f3}} and {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=CS-BH-0.2|cost_r1|f3}} per unit, against {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=SDLC|cost_r1|f3}} for SDLC). When a miss costs ten times a false alert, SDLC's expected cost did not differ significantly from that of TC-LS (SDLC lower on {{al.hi.cost.TC-LS.wins}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets, $p$ = {{al.hi.cost.TC-LS.praw}}) and was lower than that of conformal selection on {{al.hi.cost.CS-BH-0.2.wins}} datasets. At a cost ratio of 20, SDLC was cheaper than TC-LS on {{al.hi.cost20.TC-LS.wins}} datasets ($p$ = {{al.hi.cost20.TC-LS.praw}}), because its recall exceeds the guaranteed 0.9 while TC-LS attains it almost exactly. Among the stratified scores, however, S-NCP gave the cheapest alerts: SDLC's expected cost was higher than S-NCP's on {{T:T13b_alert_tests|side=hi&comparison=cost r=10: SDLC vs S-NCP|losses|int}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets at $c=10$ and on {{T:T13b_alert_tests|side=hi&comparison=cost r=20: SDLC vs S-NCP|losses|int}} at $c=20$, because S-NCP raised fewer upper-tail alerts. These $p$-values are unadjusted and descriptive. Alerts read off *marginal* sets (SCP, CQR, HPD-split) were the cheapest on average at high cost ratios ({{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=HPD-split|cost_r20|f3}} for HPD-split at $c=20$). They had no recall guarantee, however, and their recall fell below 0.9 on {{alert.marg.below09.min}}–{{alert.marg.below09.max}} datasets. the cost figure of the main text shows cost curves for three valuation datasets.

**S8.6 Sensitivity analyses.**

*Miscoverage level and strata.* The conclusions held at $\alpha=0.05$ and $\alpha=0.2$ (SDLC worst stratum {{T:T5_alpha_sensitivity|alpha=0.05&method=SDLC|worst_stratum|f3}} and {{T:T5_alpha_sensitivity|alpha=0.2&method=SDLC|worst_stratum|f3}}; Supplementary Table S6). With decile strata the worst decile coverage of SDLC rose to {{T:T6_strata_schemes|scheme=S10&method=SDLC|worst_decile|f3}}, from {{T:T6_strata_schemes|scheme=S5&method=SDLC|worst_decile|f3}} with five strata, at a size increase from {{T:T6_strata_schemes|scheme=S5&method=SDLC|size_geo|f3}} to {{T:T6_strata_schemes|scheme=S10&method=SDLC|size_geo|f3}}. The floor variant (SDLC-floor, $\beta=0.2$) reached its weaker guarantee with smaller sets (worst stratum {{T:T5_alpha_sensitivity|alpha=0.1&method=SDLC-floor|worst_stratum|f3}}, size {{T:T5_alpha_sensitivity|alpha=0.1&method=SDLC-floor|size_geo|f3}}).

*Calibration-set size.* With a quarter of the calibration split (on average {{T:T7_calibration_size|cal_frac=0.25&method=SDLC|mean_n_cal|int}} points) stratum-wise validity was retained, but SDLC's sets grew from {{T:T7_calibration_size|cal_frac=1.0&method=SDLC|size_geo|f3}} to {{T:T7_calibration_size|cal_frac=0.25&method=SDLC|size_geo|f3}}, mainly because tail strata with fewer than $1/\alpha-1$ points have an infinite threshold ({{T:T7_calibration_size|cal_frac=0.25&method=SDLC|runs_with_inf|int}} runs, against {{T:T7_calibration_size|cal_frac=1.0&method=SDLC|runs_with_inf|int}} with the full split; Supplementary Table S8).

*Base learner.* With a two-layer perceptron ({{mlp.n_seeds}} seeds), marginal methods reached a worst-stratum coverage of {{T:T9_mlp_summary|method=SCP|worst_stratum|f3}}–{{T:T9_mlp_summary|method=DCP|worst_stratum|f3}} and stratified methods {{T:T9_mlp_summary|method=BCCP|worst_stratum|f3}}–{{T:T9_mlp_summary|method=SDLC|worst_stratum|f3}}. SDLC again gave the smallest stratified sets: smaller than BCCP on {{mlp.BCCP.wins}} of {{mlp.n_datasets}} datasets (ratio {{mlp.BCCP.geo}}), than S-NCP on {{mlp.S-NCP.wins}} (ratio {{mlp.S-NCP.geo}}, Holm $p$ {{mlp.S-NCP.p}}) and than S-HPD on {{mlp.S-HPD.wins}} (ratio {{mlp.S-HPD.geo}}).

*Imbalance-aware training.* With DenseWeight sample weights (Steininger et al. 2021), SCP's worst-stratum coverage rose from {{T:T9_dw_vs_lgb|method=SCP|worst_lgb|f3}} to {{T:T9_dw_vs_lgb|method=SCP|worst_dw|f3}}, but the shortfall remained large and its sets grew by a factor of {{T:T9_dw_vs_lgb|method=SCP|size_ratio_dw_vs_lgb|f2}}. Stratified calibration remained valid ({{T:T9_dw_summary|method=SDLC|worst_stratum|f3}} for SDLC), and SDLC again gave the smallest stratified sets (Holm $p$ {{dw.BCCP.p}}, {{dw.S-NCP.p}}, {{dw.S-CQR.p}} and {{dw.S-HPD.p}} against BCCP, S-NCP, S-CQR and S-HPD). Imbalance-aware learning and stratified calibration are complementary; the former does not deliver the guarantee.

*Grouped splits.* On three datasets with repeated units (sarcos, superconductivity, video_transcoding) we repeated the main comparison with splits that keep each unit in one part (5 seeds; Supplementary Table S15). On sarcos and superconductivity the results were close to those of random splits (SDLC/S-NCP size ratios {{J:metrics_v3.json|grouped/sarcos/sdlc_vs_sncp_grouped|f3}} and {{J:metrics_v3.json|grouped/superconductivity/sdlc_vs_sncp_grouped|f3}}). On video_transcoding, splitting by video increased every method's sets several-fold (SDLC: {{T:T16_grouped_splits|dataset=video_transcoding&method=SDLC|size_random|f3}} to {{T:T16_grouped_splits|dataset=video_transcoding&method=SDLC|size_grouped|f3}}), so random splits of this dataset measure interpolation between runs of known videos. Stratified methods still covered their worst stratum at {{J:metrics_v3.json|grouped/video_transcoding/strat_worst_grouped_min|f3}}–{{T:T16_grouped_splits|dataset=video_transcoding&method=S-NCP|worst_grouped|f3}}, against at most {{J:metrics_v3.json|grouped/video_transcoding/marg_worst_grouped_max|f3}} for marginal methods, but SDLC's sets were larger than BCCP's (ratio {{J:metrics_v3.json|grouped/video_transcoding/sdlc_vs_bccp_grouped|f3}}), and BCCP and S-CQR covered less than nominal marginally ({{T:T16_grouped_splits|dataset=video_transcoding&method=BCCP|coverage_grouped|f3}} and {{T:T16_grouped_splits|dataset=video_transcoding&method=S-CQR|coverage_grouped|f3}}), as expected when exchangeability holds between groups rather than rows. SDLC's size advantage thus depends on the density model transferring to unseen units, and grouped validation is advisable for such data. Post hoc, we also split two datasets by time (Supplementary Table S15). With whole months held out (Bike_Sharing_Demand; the curated data contain no year or day), stratified methods kept a worst-stratum coverage of {{T:T24_temporal_splits|dataset=Bike_Sharing_Demand&split=blocked&method=SDLC|worst|f2}} (SDLC) against at most {{T:T24_temporal_splits|dataset=Bike_Sharing_Demand&split=blocked&method=HPD-split|worst|f2}} for marginal methods, sets grew by about a quarter, and SDLC remained smaller than BCCP and S-NCP. With Moneyball split forward in time (earliest seasons for training, then calibration, latest for test), stratified methods kept close to nominal coverage ({{T:T24_temporal_splits|dataset=Moneyball&split=forward&method=SDLC|coverage|f3}} for SDLC) but their sets became three to four times larger than under random splits, SDLC's most of all ({{T:T24_temporal_splits|dataset=Moneyball&split=forward&method=SDLC|size|f2}} against {{T:T24_temporal_splits|dataset=Moneyball&split=forward&method=BCCP|size|f2}} for BCCP). Under a genuine shift over time, the density score loses its advantage.

**S8.7 Error analysis.**

The price of stratum-wise validity (SDLC relative to DLS) rose with the skewness of the target (Spearman $\rho$ = {{ea.spearman_price_abs_skew.rho}}, $p$ {{ea.spearman_price_abs_skew.p}}) and fell with the calibration-set size ($\rho$ = {{ea.spearman_price_log_ncal.rho}}). It was largest for forest fires ({{T:T10_error_analysis|dataset=forest_fires|price|f2}}), whose atom at zero merges the two lower strata. Its upper-stratum coverage also includes automatic misses: whether the largest fire falls into the training split decides the domain $\mathcal{Y}_D$, and in three of ten seeds one to three test targets lay above it. Across all datasets, out-of-domain test targets were rare (at most {{J:statistics_review05.json|ctr23.ood_max|pct}} of a run's test points on CTR23 and {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.ood_max|pct}} on the held-out data; Supplementary Tables S1–S2). SDLC was larger than S-NCP on {{h3.S-NCP.losses}} datasets (socmob, cars, forest fires and QSAR fish toxicity; ratios up to {{T:T10_error_analysis|dataset=socmob|vs_sncp|f2}}), all small or with many ties, so that the scale and noise models rest on few distinct values. The density score helps most when the location model is accurate and the noise is heteroscedastic ($\rho$ = {{ea.spearman_vs_sncp_rmse.rho}} with the relative RMSE).
