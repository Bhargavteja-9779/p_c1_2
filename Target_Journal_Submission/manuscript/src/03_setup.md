# Experimental design {#sec:setup}

The study answers seven research questions (RQ1–RQ7), reported in this order in Section [-@sec:results]. The datasets, seeds, splits, miscoverage levels, strata, base learners, methods and analyses of the main benchmark were fixed in a version-controlled configuration before it was run (Section [-@sec:protocol]).

* **RQ1 (tail coverage).** Do marginal and covariate-adaptive methods under-cover rare target strata, and does stratified calibration restore stratum-wise coverage?
* **RQ2 (price).** How much larger are the sets under stratum-wise validity, compared with marginal methods at the same and at a stricter level?
* **RQ3 (score).** Does the density score of Proposition 2 give smaller stratified sets than other scores, and which component is responsible? We answer it with an ablation on real data, a synthetic study and a confirmatory replication.
* **RQ4 (relevance-weighted coverage).** Does RW-DLC attain relevance-weighted coverage and cover the most extreme outcomes better than SDLC?
* **RQ5 (shift).** How robust is stratum-wise coverage under target shift?
* **RQ6 (decisions).** Are prediction sets useful for flagging extreme outcomes, compared with dedicated conformal tail classifiers and conformal selection?
* **RQ7 (sensitivity).** How sensitive are the conclusions to the miscoverage level, strata, calibration-set size, density estimator, base learner, imbalance-aware training and grouped splits?

## Datasets {#sec:data}

The main benchmark uses the OpenML-CTR23 suite of 35 curated tabular regression datasets [@fischer2023openml]; using a complete external suite avoids selecting datasets that favour the proposed method. By a pre-specified rule, we excluded the four datasets whose target takes fewer than 20 distinct values, because Lebesgue set size does not measure informativeness for such ordinal targets. The rule is a threshold on the number of distinct values only; count targets (e.g. abalone rings) and heavily discretised targets were retained. Targets with heavy ties or atoms are retained, such as the burned area of forest fires, which is zero for {{J:metrics_v3.json|forest_zero_frac|pct}} of the fires. The remaining {{n_datasets}} datasets (Supplementary Table S1) have {{ds_n_min}} to {{ds_n_max}} observations and {{ds_p_min}} to {{ds_p_max}} features after one-hot encoding; {{n_skewed}} have a target skewness above 1 in absolute value.

*Confirmatory datasets.* For the replication, we took the regression datasets of the tabular benchmark of @grinsztajn2022tree (OpenML suites 335 and 336), removed every dataset sharing source data with CTR23 and duplicate versions, and applied the same exclusion rule. This left {{conf.n_datasets}} held-out datasets (Supplementary Table S2). As pre-specified, datasets with more than 50,000 rows were reduced to one fixed random subsample of 50,000 rows shared by all seeds; the CTR23 suite, whose largest dataset has 72,000 rows, was used as curated. Checks made after the analysis (Supplementary Tables S2, S3 and S17b) found that two held-out datasets (yprop_4_1, topo_2_1) contain the same observations and target, so the 17 datasets represent 16 independent targets; that six targets were log-transformed by the curators; that two targets are codes rather than quantities (visualizing_soil, seattlecrime6, the latter with 87% duplicate rows); and that the collection has at least 4,209 rows and no extremely skewed target ($|$skewness$|\le$ {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.absskew_max|f1}}, against up to {{J:statistics_review05.json|ctr23.absskew_max|f1}} in CTR23).

## Splitting, leakage control and repetitions

Each of the {{n_seeds}} predefined seeds defines a random partition into proper training (50%), calibration (25%) and test (25%) sets; the replication uses five seeds. Every model, stratum boundary and density estimate uses the proper training set only. Several datasets contain repeated units, for example exact duplicate rows ({{J:metrics_v3.json|dup_sarcos|pct}} of sarcos), repeated videos (video transcoding) and repeat sales (King County). The guarantees therefore refer to a new row from the same pool, not to a new unit (Supplementary Section S2 and Supplementary Table S3). Ten datasets were used during development or to diagnose the implementation (Section [-@sec:protocol]); the main comparisons are repeated without them.

## Base learners

All methods share the same LightGBM models [@ke2017lightgbm] with one fixed configuration (learning rate 0.05, 31 leaves, up to 2000 rounds with early stopping on a 10% internal validation split): a mean model, quantile models (for CQR), the scale models of Algorithm 1, and a multiclass model over 40 target bins (histogram density). No method receives specific tuning. The sensitivity analyses (RQ7) add a two-layer perceptron with the same pipeline and LightGBM trained with DenseWeight sample weights, which up-weight rare targets [@steininger2021denseweight].

## Compared methods

*Marginal or covariate-adaptive calibration.* Split conformal prediction with absolute residuals (SCP) [@papadopoulos2002inductive]; normalised conformal prediction (NCP) [@lei2018distribution]; CQR [@romano2019conformalized]; Mondrian conformal regression with five bins of the point prediction $\hat y=\hat\mu(x)$ (Mondrian-ŷ) [cf. @bostrom2020mondrian; @boruah2026distribution]; DCP [@chernozhukov2021distributional]; HPD-split [@izbicki2022cdsplit]; and density level sets with a single threshold (DLS) [cf. @lei2013distribution]. DCP, HPD-split and DLS use the same location–scale density as SDLC, so the comparison isolates the calibration rule.

*Stratified calibration.* BCCP [@randahl2026bin], the absolute-residual score with stratum-wise thresholds, in union and hull form (BCCP-hull); the normalised residual with stratum-wise thresholds (S-NCP), in union and hull form; stratified CQR (S-CQR); the HPD score with stratum-wise thresholds (S-HPD); and SDLC with its hull and floor ($\beta=2\alpha$) variants. S-CQR omits the level optimisation of @wang2023equal and is not an implementation of BFQR. S-NCP is the information-matched comparator of SDLC: both use the same $\hat\mu$ and $\hat\sigma$.

*Ablations.* The chain BCCP → S-NCP → SDLC-N → SDLC adds, in turn, the scale $\hat\sigma(x)$, the density weighting $1/\hat\sigma(x)$ and the estimated noise shape. The histogram and two-piece densities are secondary results.

*Tail-alert rules (RQ6).* A set raises an *upper-tail alert* when it intersects the upper stratum $B_K$; for every method with stratum-wise coverage $1-\alpha$ the alert recall is at least $1-\alpha$. We compare such alerts with a class-conditional conformal tail classifier that thresholds $\hat P(Y\in B_K\mid x)$ from the location–scale model (TC-LS) or the histogram model (TC-H) to guarantee recall $1-\alpha$; conformal selection with Benjamini–Hochberg control of the false discovery rate at $q_{\mathrm{FDR}}\in\{0.1,0.2\}$ (CS-BH-0.1, CS-BH-0.2) [@jin2023selection]; and the point-prediction rule (Point), "alert if $\hat\mu(x)$ is in $B_K$". All methods are evaluated from the same cached model outputs with the exact rules of Section [-@sec:implementation].

## Evaluation metrics

For each test set we report:

* marginal coverage and stratum-wise coverage (1) in each calibration stratum;
* coverage in strata *not* used for calibration (training-target deciles, the top and bottom 5% and 1%) and in deciles of $\hat\sigma(x)$, a diagnostic of covariate-conditional coverage;
* mean set size divided by the training-target standard deviation (*normalised size*, also written size/sd($y$)), the share of empty sets and the number of disjoint pieces;
* tail-alert recall, alert rate, precision, and the expected cost per unit when a missed extreme outcome costs $c$ times a false alert;
* coverage under shift, by importance weighting of the test set: tilts $\omega_\gamma(y)=\exp(\gamma\zeta_y)$ ($\gamma\in\{1,2\}$), with $\zeta_y=\Phi^{-1}\{\hat F_Y(y)\}$ the normal score of $y$ under the training distribution $\hat F_Y$, and a *positive control* that multiplies the upper stratum by 5 and the lower by 0.5 without changing the within-stratum laws, so that Corollary 1(i) applies exactly. We report the Kish effective sample size of the weights.

Stratum coverages are averaged over seeds before taking the minimum across strata ("worst-stratum coverage"). Because the expected minimum of noisy estimates is at most the minimum of their expectations (Jensen's inequality), worst-stratum coverage is biased downwards [cf. @boruah2026null], we also report a *null reference*: the worst-stratum coverage expected under exact validity given the realised stratum sizes (Monte Carlo over the Beta–binomial law of split-conformal coverage).

## Statistical analysis

Following @demsar2006statistical, the unit of analysis is the dataset: metrics are averaged over seeds and methods are compared across datasets. Set sizes are compared on the log scale by the geometric-mean ratio with a 95% bootstrap confidence interval, the Hodges–Lehmann estimate, wins and losses, and the sign test. Paired comparisons use the Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Holm's correction within each family [@holm1979simple]. One-sided tests are used only for the hypothesis that stratified calibration improves worst-stratum and tail coverage over marginal methods; comparisons among stratified methods are two-sided. Equivalence of worst-stratum coverage is assessed by two one-sided Wilcoxon tests (TOST) with a margin of 0.01. Friedman mean ranks are descriptive only [@benavoli2016should].

## Protocol, deviations and confirmatory replication {#sec:protocol}

*What was pre-specified.* We call an element of the analysis *pre-specified* if it was committed to version control before the corresponding evaluation was run; none was registered with a third party. The configuration of the main benchmark, the primary method and directional expectations for the research questions of the main benchmark (all except RQ4) were pre-specified in this sense.

*What changed afterwards.* (1) A loader bug that parsed string-typed features as missing was corrected and the affected datasets recomputed. (2) After the first run we learned of BCCP and BFQR; BCCP's hull form, decile strata and tail-alert metrics were added. (3) The first implementation evaluated density-based sets on a coarse grid of the *marginal* target distribution, which inflated them; under it the size comparison with BCCP was not significant (Supplementary Section S5). (4) A review of the second run led to corrections applied to every method: cross-fitted standardisation, a defensive $t$ component instead of an ad hoc floor, half-open stratum pieces, a truncated domain and stratum-exact geometry. All reported results use the final implementation.

*Consequences.* Because the implementation changed after results had been seen, every size comparison on CTR23 (RQ3) is exploratory; the coverage results are unaffected in direction. To test the size comparisons, we committed a confirmatory analysis plan (hypotheses, tests, decision rules and script; Supplementary Section S6) to version control *before* evaluating any conformal method on the {{conf.n_datasets}} held-out datasets; its commit identifier (`ccba99b`), commit time and the SHA-256 hash of the committed plan are listed in the anonymised code package (file PLAN_TIMESTAMPS.md); the version history itself will be made public upon acceptance. The time stamp is that of our repository, not of a third-party registry, so we call the plan pre-specified rather than pre-registered; base-model test metrics had already been computed on these datasets. Section [-@sec:confirmatory] reports the outcome.

*Relevance-weighted study (RQ4).* RW-DLC was developed after the main benchmark had been run. Its analysis plan (relevance functions, comparators, metrics, hypotheses RW-1 to RW-4, tests and decision rules; Supplementary Section S7) was committed (identifier `a9f31d2`, listed in the same file) before RW-DLC was evaluated on any dataset, but after the SDLC results on the same held-out datasets, including its under-coverage of the top 1%, were known. RW-1 and RW-2 therefore test consequences of Theorem 2 and RW-3 an anticipated effect; only RW-4 could fail for empirical reasons, and it did (Section [-@sec:confirmatory]). RW-DLC, its ablations RW-DLS (score $-\hat f$) and RW-NCP (normalised residual), both with relevance-weighted calibration, and the comparators SCP, NCP, CQR, DLS (labelled DLS-M in the analysis plan), S-NCP and SDLC were evaluated with coverage decided exactly at the observed target and set sizes computed on a grid of 4,000 target values over $\mathcal{Y}_D$. Additional metrics are the relevance-weighted coverage (9), the mean and minimum coverage over training-target deciles, and coverage of the top and bottom 1%.

## Use of a large language model {#sec:llm}

In line with the journal's policy, we document that a large language model (Claude, Anthropic) was used as a research assistant in this study: to search and verify the literature, to write and test code, to orchestrate the computational experiments, to draft and edit the text, and to produce simulated adversarial reviews of the drafts. The model did not generate any data or result. Every number, table and figure in the paper is produced by the code provided as supplementary material from public data (Section [-@sec:protocol]), and every reference was checked against its publisher or index record. The authors checked the analyses and the text and take full responsibility for the content.
