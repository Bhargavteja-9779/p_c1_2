# Related work {#sec:related}

## Conformal regression and covariate-conditional coverage {#sec:rel-conformal}

Split (inductive) conformal prediction calibrates a nonconformity score of any fitted regressor on held-out data and guarantees marginal coverage in finite samples under exchangeability [@papadopoulos2002inductive; @vovk2005alrw; @lei2018distribution]. Most later work changes the score so that the sets adapt to the covariates: normalised residuals [@lei2018distribution], conformalised quantile regression (CQR) [@romano2019conformalized; @sesia2020comparison], distributional conformal prediction (DCP) [@chernozhukov2021distributional], conformal predictive distributions [@vovk2019nonparametric] and jackknife+/CV+ [@barber2021jackknife]. A family of methods builds sets from density estimates: conformal density level sets, which are asymptotically optimal without covariates [@lei2013distribution], local conformal bands [@lei2014bands], Dist-split, CD-split and HPD-split [@izbicki2020flexible; @izbicki2022cdsplit], conditional histograms [@sesia2021conformal], regression-as-classification [@guha2024conformal] and conformal thresholded intervals [@luo2025cti]. Under coverage constraints indexed by covariate shifts, length-optimal sets are conditional-density level sets with a covariate-dependent level [@kiyani2024length]. SDLC uses a score from this family but calibrates it with stratum-specific thresholds.

Exact covariate-conditional coverage cannot be guaranteed in finite samples [@barber2021limits], so the literature pursues relaxations: conditional calibration over classes of covariate groups or shifts [@gibbs2025conformal], localised calibration [@guan2023localized; @hore2025local], calibration within covariate partitions learned by trees [@cabezas2025regression; @santos2026loboost], and learned scores or adjusted regressors [@xie2024boosted; @gao2024adjusting]. Expert-system applications use conformal prediction as an uncertainty layer, for example for option prices [@bastos2024conformal], cyclone tracks [@meng2024uncertainty] and concrete carbonation [@hajibabaee2025carbonation]. All of these condition, at most, on functions of $X$, and Proposition 1 shows that even exact covariate-conditional coverage leaves extreme outcomes under-covered. Covariate adaptivity therefore complements stratified calibration but cannot replace it.

## Category-, label- and bin-conditional calibration {#sec:rel-mondrian}

Mondrian conformal predictors [@vovk2005alrw; @vovk2022alrw] calibrate separately within categories defined by a taxonomy of object and label, which yields finite-sample coverage conditional on the category [@angelopoulos2024theoretical]; label-conditional validity is the special case studied by @vovk2013conditional and @toccaceli2019combination. In classification, sparse classes are pooled by clustered conformal prediction [@ding2023class], BCOPS builds label-conditional sets from densities [@guan2022prediction], and Mondrian prediction has been applied in this journal to product verification [@oucheikh2022product]. Closest to our optimality result is @sadinle2019least [see also @lei2014classification]: the least ambiguous classifier under class-specific coverage thresholds $p(y\mid x)$ with class-specific cut-offs. Proposition 2 is the corresponding statement for a continuous outcome. Group-conditional coverage also underlies equalized coverage in fair prediction [@romano2020malice], and Kandinsky conformal prediction [@bairaktari2025kandinsky] covers overlapping or fractional groups defined jointly on covariates and labels, with a high-probability guarantee; disjoint target strata are its simplest case, for which Mondrian calibration is exact.

In regression, Mondrian categories have mostly been functions of the covariates or the model: binned difficulty estimates [@bostrom2020mondrian], regression trees [@johansson2018interpretable], covariate clusters [@sousa2024improving], predictive distributions [@bostrom2021mondrian] and quartiles of out-of-fold predictions [@boruah2026distribution], where split conformal intervals for steel fatigue strength under-covered the strongest quartile; worst-bin statistics need a null correction [@boruah2026null]. Such categories do not give coverage conditional on $Y$.

Two methods calibrate within bins of the outcome itself. @wang2023equal require coverage to be equal across sensitive groups conditionally on the outcome. Their binned fair quantile regression (BFQR) calibrates CQR scores within each group and equal-mass outcome bin and chooses the bin levels $\beta_m$ to minimise the average width subject to $\sum_m\beta_m/M=1-\alpha$, so coverage is equalised across groups but traded across bins. Bin-conditional conformal prediction (BCCP) [@randahl2026bin], motivated by the skewed distribution of conflict fatalities, runs split conformal prediction with a residual score within user-defined outcome bins and returns the union of the per-bin sets or its hull; it guarantees coverage within each bin, and the authors note that this holds for any score. Our stratified calibration step (Eqs. 3–4) coincides with BCCP for the absolute residual, and its validity (Theorem 1) is not claimed as new. What BCCP leaves open, and this paper addresses, is why such calibration is needed even under exact covariate-conditional coverage, which score makes bin-valid sets smallest and why, how the guarantee behaves under label shift, and how the alternatives compare on a large benchmark.

## Imbalanced regression and its uncertainty {#sec:rel-imbalanced}

Imbalanced regression concerns targets whose most important values are rare [@branco2016survey; @ribeiro2020imbalanced]. Utility-based regression [@torgo2007utility], precision and recall for regression [@torgo2009precision] and SERA [@ribeiro2020imbalanced] evaluate point predictions on the relevant range. Data-level methods synthesise rare examples, for instance SMOTER [@torgo2013smote] and SMOGN [@branco2017smogn] [benchmarked by @avelino2024resampling]; contributions in this journal include oversampling with autoencoders [@belhaouari2024oversampling], GAN refinement [@alahyari2026smogan], hybrid balancing [@shahbazi2026hybrid] and soft computing [@arteaga2023enhancing]. Algorithm-level methods re-weight the loss: DenseWeight [@steininger2021denseweight], label and feature distribution smoothing [@yang2021delving] and Balanced MSE [@ren2022balanced]. Uncertainty enters mainly as a means to better point predictions [@wang2023variational; @dolar2025uncertainty], without coverage guarantees; extreme conformal prediction addresses very high confidence levels instead [@pasche2026extreme]. For pure yes/no decisions about extremes, conformal selection controls the false discovery rate [@jin2023selection] and a class-conditional conformal classifier controls the miss rate; both serve as baselines in our decision study.

## Distribution shift {#sec:rel-shift}

Weighted conformal prediction handles covariate shift through likelihood ratios [@tibshirani2019conformal]. Under label (target) shift, the law of $Y$ changes while that of $X$ given $Y$ does not. Re-weighting approaches estimate the shift, in classification [@podkopaev2021distribution] and regression [@yi2025distribution; @lee2026split], whereas label-conditional calibration needs no estimate because the shift re-weights whole classes [@podkopaev2021distribution]. Stratified calibration carries this over to regression exactly when the shift re-weights whole strata (Corollary 1); otherwise the guarantee holds approximately, which our experiments quantify.

For imbalanced regression, this paper thus complements methods that improve accuracy on rare targets with a guarantee on how often prediction sets contain them, evaluated on {{n_datasets}} OpenML-CTR23 datasets [@fischer2023openml] and replicated on {{conf.n_datasets}} held-out datasets.
