# Related work {#sec:related}

Four lines of work meet in this paper: conformal regression with covariate-adaptive coverage, calibration within categories defined by the label, imbalanced regression, and conformal prediction under distribution shift.

## Conformal regression and covariate-conditional coverage {#sec:rel-conformal}

Split (inductive) conformal prediction calibrates a nonconformity score of an arbitrary fitted regressor on held-out data and guarantees marginal coverage in finite samples under exchangeability [@papadopoulos2002inductive; @vovk2005alrw; @lei2018distribution]. Much subsequent work changes the score so that the sets adapt to the covariates. Locally weighted (normalised) conformal prediction divides absolute residuals by an estimate of their spread [@lei2018distribution]; conformalised quantile regression (CQR) calibrates the interval between two estimated conditional quantiles [@romano2019conformalized; @sesia2020comparison]; and distributional conformal prediction (DCP) uses the probability integral transform of an estimated conditional distribution function [@chernozhukov2021distributional]. Conformal predictive distributions [@vovk2019nonparametric] and jackknife+/CV+ intervals, which reuse all data at higher computational cost [@barber2021jackknife], are further alternatives. A family of methods builds sets from density estimates: density level sets, conformalised without covariates and asymptotically optimal [@lei2013distribution] and extended to regression by local conformal bands [@lei2014bands]; Dist-split and CD-split [@izbicki2020flexible]; HPD-split, which conformalises highest-density regions of an estimated conditional density [@izbicki2022cdsplit]; conformal sets from conditional histograms, which adapt to skewness [@sesia2021conformal]; regression-as-classification, which learns a density over a discretised target [@guha2024conformal]; and conformal thresholded intervals, which threshold the estimated conditional quantiles to shorten the sets [@luo2025cti]. Length-optimal conformal sets under conditional or group-conditional constraints are level sets of the conditional density [@kiyani2024length]. SDLC uses a score from this family but calibrates it with stratum-specific thresholds.

These methods target approximate covariate-conditional coverage, $P\{Y\in C(X)\mid X=x\}\approx 1-\alpha$, which cannot be guaranteed in finite samples without further assumptions [@barber2021limits]. The literature has therefore pursued relaxed guarantees and approximations: local conformal bands with asymptotic conditional validity [@lei2014bands]; conditional calibration, which guarantees coverage over a user-specified class of covariate groups or shifts [@gibbs2025conformal]; localised calibration, which weights calibration points by their proximity to the test point in covariate space [@guan2023localized; @hore2025local]; calibration within the cells of covariate partitions learned by regression trees [@cabezas2025regression] or by the leaf paths of gradient-boosted trees [@santos2026loboost]; and learned scores or adjusted regressors that reduce conditional miscoverage [@xie2024boosted; @gao2024adjusting]. In applied expert systems, conformal prediction serves as an uncertainty layer, for example for option prices [@bastos2024conformal], tropical-cyclone track forecasts [@meng2024uncertainty] and the carbonation depth of recycled-aggregate concrete [@hajibabaee2025carbonation]. All of these approaches are marginal or condition, exactly or approximately, on functions of $X$, and Proposition 1 (Section [-@sec:problem]) shows that, in a Gaussian model, even exact covariate-conditional coverage leaves extreme outcomes under-covered. Covariate-adaptive scores are therefore complementary to stratified calibration rather than a substitute for it.

## Category-, label- and bin-conditional calibration {#sec:rel-mondrian}

Mondrian conformal predictors [@vovk2005alrw; @vovk2022alrw] assign each example to a category through a taxonomy that may depend on both the object and its label, and calibrate separately within each category; this yields finite-sample coverage conditional on the category [@angelopoulos2024theoretical]. @vovk2012conditional distinguished object-, label- and training-conditional validity and showed that Mondrian inductive conformal predictors achieve category-conditional, and in particular label-conditional, validity [see also @vovk2013conditional]; @toccaceli2019combination studied label-conditional inductive Mondrian predictors and their combination.

In classification, the corresponding property is class-conditional coverage. When classes are many and calibration examples per class are few, clustered conformal prediction pools classes with similar score distributions [@ding2023class], a device analogous to merging sparse target strata; BCOPS constructs label-conditional sets from density estimates [@guan2022prediction]; and class-conditional Mondrian conformal prediction has been applied in this journal to product verification [@oucheikh2022product]. Closest to our optimality result is work on set-valued classifiers with class-specific error control [@lei2014classification], in particular @sadinle2019least, who showed that the least ambiguous classifier under class-specific coverage thresholds the conditional class probabilities $p(y\mid x)$ with class-specific cut-offs. Proposition 2 is the same characterisation for a continuous outcome, with Lebesgue measure as the size and target strata in place of classes. Group-conditional coverage is also the basis of equalized coverage across protected groups in fair prediction [@romano2020malice]. Kandinsky conformal prediction [@bairaktari2025kandinsky] provides conditional coverage for overlapping or fractional groups defined jointly on covariates and labels, includes regression, and contains covariate-group-conditional, class-conditional and Mondrian conformal prediction as special cases, with a high-probability guarantee whose slack grows with the complexity of the group class. Disjoint target strata are its simplest label-defined groups, for which Mondrian calibration gives an exact finite-sample guarantee; overlapping groups (for example strata and deciles together) and weighted groups (for example tilts of the target distribution) are a natural way to refine the guarantee beyond a single partition.

In regression, Mondrian categories have mostly been defined through the covariates or the fitted model: by binning difficulty estimates [@bostrom2020mondrian], by the leaves of regression trees [@johansson2018interpretable], by clusters of the covariates [@sousa2024improving] and by quartiles of out-of-fold point predictions [@boruah2026distribution]; Mondrian taxonomies have also been applied to conformal predictive distributions [@bostrom2021mondrian]. The last study reports that split conformal intervals for steel fatigue strength, although marginally valid, under-cover the highest-strength quartile; a follow-up shows that worst-bin coverage statistics are biased downwards and proposes a null correction [@boruah2026null]. Because the categories are functions of $X$, the resulting guarantees do not extend to coverage conditional on $Y$.

We are aware of two regression methods that calibrate within bins of the outcome itself. @wang2023equal define equal opportunity of coverage, which requires coverage to be equal across groups of a sensitive attribute conditionally on the true outcome; their binned fair quantile regression (BFQR) splits $Y$ into equal-mass bins and calibrates within each combination of group and bin, with a conformal step that maintains coverage, and they illustrate that standard methods mis-cover the head and tail bins of the outcome. Their aim is fairness, but the binning device is the same, and their analysis gives the bin-conditional finite-sample guarantee for regression. The most closely related work is the bin-conditional conformal prediction (BCCP) of @randahl2026bin, motivated by the heavily skewed distribution of fatalities from armed conflict. BCCP partitions the calibration scores by user-defined bins of the observed outcome, runs split conformal prediction with a residual score within each bin over a grid of candidate outcomes, and returns either the union of the per-bin sets, which may be discontiguous, or its hull ("contiguized" sets). The authors state that BCCP guarantees coverage within each bin under within-bin exchangeability, describe it as a hybrid of label-conditional (Mondrian) and split conformal prediction, and evaluate it on simulations and on conflict fatalities.

The stratified calibration step of the present paper (Section [-@sec:method], Eqs. 3–4) coincides with BCCP: with the absolute-residual score and the strata as bins it is BCCP, and taking the convex hull, as in our hull variant, gives its contiguized form. Its finite-sample validity (Theorem 1) is an instance of Mondrian validity and is not claimed as new; @randahl2026bin note that it holds for any score. BCCP leaves open which score yields the smallest bin-valid sets, how the score's efficiency comes about, and how the guarantee behaves under label shift. The present paper addresses these questions: the oracle analysis of covariate-conditional sets (Proposition 1), the size-optimal score under per-stratum coverage and the asymptotic optimality of its plug-in version (Propositions 2 and 3), the SDLC procedure built on them, the label-shift analysis (Corollary 1), and large-scale evidence in which BCCP, stratified versions of the normalised-residual, quantile and HPD scores, and marginal and covariate-adaptive methods serve as baselines.

## Imbalanced regression and its uncertainty {#sec:rel-imbalanced}

Imbalanced regression concerns problems in which the target values that matter most are rare in the training data [@branco2016survey; @ribeiro2020imbalanced]. Utility-based regression encodes the non-uniform importance of targets through relevance and utility functions [@torgo2007utility], and precision and recall for regression [@torgo2009precision] and the SERA metric [@ribeiro2020imbalanced] evaluate point predictions on the relevant, typically extreme, part of the target range. Data-level methods re-balance the training set: SMOTER [@torgo2013smote] and SMOGN, which adds Gaussian noise [@branco2017smogn], synthesise examples with rare targets, and @avelino2024resampling survey and benchmark such re-sampling strategies. Recent contributions in this journal include oversampling with nearest neighbours and autoencoders [@belhaouari2024oversampling], the refinement of SMOGN samples by a generative adversarial network [@alahyari2026smogan], a hybrid of data-level and algorithm-level balancing [@shahbazi2026hybrid] and soft-computing techniques that actively address imbalance [@arteaga2023enhancing]. Algorithm-level methods re-weight the loss: DenseWeight weights training examples inversely to the estimated density of their target [@steininger2021denseweight], label and feature distribution smoothing exploit the similarity of nearby targets in deep models [@yang2021delving], and Balanced MSE corrects the regression loss for the imbalance of the training label distribution [@ren2022balanced].

Uncertainty has entered this literature mainly as a means to better point predictions or as a by-product of probabilistic modelling. Variational imbalanced regression obtains uncertainty estimates through probabilistic smoothing [@wang2023variational], and UQDIR identifies rare samples through epistemic uncertainty and re-samples them with a weight function to improve accuracy [@dolar2025uncertainty]; neither provides a coverage guarantee. Extreme conformal prediction addresses a different difficulty, very high confidence levels, by combining extreme-value theory with conformal prediction [@pasche2026extreme]. Guarantees on how often prediction sets contain the rare targets themselves, the uncertainty counterpart of the relevance-based evaluation above, have so far come from conflict forecasting and fair regression [@randahl2026bin; @wang2023equal] rather than from the imbalanced-regression literature (Section [-@sec:rel-mondrian]). Where the decision is only whether a unit's outcome is extreme, dedicated procedures exist: conformal selection flags units whose outcome exceeds a threshold with control of the false discovery rate [@jin2023selection], and a class-conditional conformal classifier on the event "outcome in the tail" controls the miss rate. We use both as baselines for tail alerts (Section [-@sec:results]).

## Distribution shift {#sec:rel-shift}

Weighted conformal prediction restores validity under covariate shift by re-weighting calibration scores with the likelihood ratio of the covariate distributions [@tibshirani2019conformal]. Under label shift, also called target shift, the marginal distribution of $Y$ changes while the conditional distribution of $X$ given $Y$ does not. For classification, @podkopaev2021distribution study conformal prediction under label shift, using re-weighted and label-conditional calibration. For regression, @yi2025distribution identify the likelihood ratio under target shift and construct highest-density sets from a weight-adjusted conditional density, and @lee2026split combine a label-shift-adjusted Bayesian score with importance weighting. The re-weighting approaches estimate the shift [@yi2025distribution; @lee2026split]; label-conditional calibration in classification needs no estimate, because a label shift re-weights whole classes [@podkopaev2021distribution].

Stratified calibration carries this property over to regression: it needs no estimate of the shift, because a guarantee that holds within every stratum is unaffected by changes in the stratum probabilities. This invariance is exact when the shift re-weights whole strata, that is, when the conditional distribution of $(X,Y)$ within each stratum is unchanged (Corollary 1). If the density ratio varies inside a stratum, the within-stratum distribution changes and the guarantee holds only approximately, which our experiments quantify under target tilting (Section [-@sec:setup]).

**Positioning of this work.** Calibration within outcome strata [@randahl2026bin; @wang2023equal] and its per-stratum validity [@vovk2005alrw; @vovk2013conditional; @randahl2026bin] are established, and the present paper takes them as given. It asks instead why such calibration is needed even when covariate-conditional coverage is exact (Proposition 1), which score makes stratum-valid sets smallest and where the gain comes from (Propositions 2 and 3, SDLC), and when the guarantee survives label shift (Corollary 1). It then examines these questions empirically on {{n_datasets}} OpenML-CTR23 datasets [@fischer2023openml] with {{n_seeds}} repetitions each, under a protocol fixed in advance, against BCCP, stratified normalised-residual, quantile and HPD scores and marginal and covariate-adaptive conformal methods, and replicates the main comparisons on {{conf.n_datasets}} held-out datasets under a pre-registered analysis plan. For imbalanced regression, this complements methods that improve accuracy on rare targets with a guarantee on how often prediction sets contain them.
