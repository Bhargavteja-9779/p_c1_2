abstract: |
  Prediction intervals for regression are usually calibrated to hold on average over all outcomes, so they can systematically miss the rare, extreme outcomes that matter most in imbalanced regression. We study stratum-wise coverage, the probability that a prediction set contains the outcome given that the outcome lies in a target stratum such as the upper tail. Even the oracle interval with exact covariate-conditional coverage under-covers the tails: in a Gaussian model with half of the variance explained it covers {{prop1.upper10_r05}} of the top decile. Calibrating conformal thresholds within target strata guarantees stratum-wise coverage for any score, so the score determines only efficiency. We show that conditional-density level sets with stratum-specific thresholds are the smallest stratum-valid sets, that their plug-in conformal version is asymptotically optimal, and that the gain over a normalised residual comes from reallocating coverage across inputs. The resulting method, stratified density-level conformal prediction (SDLC), was compared with {{n_methods_main}} procedures on {{n_datasets}} OpenML-CTR23 datasets. Marginal and covariate-adaptive methods covered {{marg.worst_stratum.min}}–{{marg.worst_stratum.max}} of their worst-covered stratum at a nominal 90%, whereas stratified methods were consistent with exact validity. SDLC gave the smallest stratum-valid sets, smaller than bin-conditional conformal prediction on {{h3.BCCP.wins}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets, and a pre-registered replication on {{conf.n_eval}} held-out datasets confirmed the ordering (size ratios between {{C-H3.S-CQR.geo}} and {{C-H3.S-HPD.geo}}). Gains over a stratified normalised residual were small and came with lower coverage of the noisiest inputs. A decision study shows when dedicated conformal tail classifiers are preferable for alerts. Code and raw results are released.
