abstract: |
  Conformal prediction intervals for regression are usually calibrated on average over all outcomes, so they can systematically miss the rare, extreme outcomes that matter most in imbalanced regression. Even an ideal interval with exact coverage at every input covers only {{prop1.upper10_r05}} of the top decile in a Gaussian model with half of the variance explained. We study how to guarantee coverage of rare outcomes and what it costs. On {{n_datasets}} datasets of the OpenML-CTR23 benchmark suite, seven standard conformal methods covered only {{marg.worst_stratum.min}}–{{marg.worst_stratum.max}} of their worst-covered target stratum at a nominal 90%. Calibrating within target strata restored coverage in every stratum and enlarged sets by a factor of about {{price.SDLC.geo}}, which is cheaper than lowering the miscoverage level for all outcomes. We show that conditional-density level sets with stratum-specific thresholds are the smallest stratum-valid sets. Their plug-in method, stratified density-level conformal prediction (SDLC), gave smaller sets than stratified absolute-residual and quantile methods, as a pre-specified analysis confirmed on {{conf.n_eval}} held-out datasets, but within a few percent of a stratified normalised residual, which covered the most extreme outcomes better. For coverage weighted by a relevance function, the size-optimal relevance-weighted method (RW-DLC) covered {{J:statistics_review05.json|rw3_sensitivity/unified (y >= q99, >= 10 test points)/rw|pct}} of the top 1% of held-out outcomes, against {{J:statistics_review05.json|rw3_sensitivity/unified (y >= q99, >= 10 test points)/sdlc|pct}} for SDLC with five strata and {{J:statistics_review08.json|S7.top1_mean/SDLC-S7|pct}} for SDLC with dedicated 1% strata. Both methods work with any regressor and cost microseconds per prediction. For a known extreme range a dedicated stratum suffices; relevance weighting suits coverage across the whole target range without chosen strata.
