title: "Covering rare outcomes in imbalanced regression: stratified and relevance-weighted conformal prediction with density scores"

abstract: |
  Prediction intervals for regression are usually calibrated to hold on average over all outcomes, so they can systematically miss the rare, extreme outcomes that matter most in imbalanced regression. Even the oracle interval with exact covariate-conditional coverage covers only {{prop1.upper10_r05}} of the top decile in a Gaussian model with half of the variance explained. We study two guarantees for rare outcomes and the prediction sets that meet them most efficiently. For coverage within every stratum of the target, calibration within strata is an established Mondrian device; we show that conditional-density level sets with stratum-specific thresholds are the smallest such sets and evaluate the resulting method, stratified density-level conformal prediction (SDLC). For coverage weighted by an imbalanced-regression relevance function, we propose relevance-weighted density-level conformal prediction (RW-DLC), which needs no strata, has a finite-sample guarantee and uses the size-optimal score. On {{n_datasets}} OpenML-CTR23 datasets, marginal and covariate-adaptive methods covered only {{marg.worst_stratum.min}}–{{marg.worst_stratum.max}} of their worst-covered stratum at a nominal 90%, whereas stratified methods were consistent with exact validity. SDLC gave smaller sets than bin-conditional conformal prediction and stratified quantile regression, which a pre-specified replication on {{conf.n_eval}} held-out datasets confirmed, but stayed within a few per cent of a stratified normalised residual. In a second pre-specified study on the held-out datasets, RW-DLC met its relevance-weighted target and covered {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/rw|pct}} of the top 1% of outcomes, against {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/sdlc|pct}} for SDLC, at a cost in coverage of the bulk. Code and raw results are released.
