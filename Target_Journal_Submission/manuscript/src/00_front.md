abstract: |
  Conformal prediction intervals are usually calibrated on average over all outcomes, so they can miss the rare, extreme outcomes that matter most in imbalanced regression. Even an ideal interval with exact coverage at every input covers only {{prop1.upper10_r05}} of the top decile in a Gaussian model with half of the variance explained. We study how to guarantee coverage of rare outcomes and what it costs. On {{n_datasets}} datasets of the OpenML-CTR23 benchmark suite, standard conformal methods covered only {{marg.worst_stratum.min}}–{{marg.worst_stratum.max}} of their worst-covered target stratum at a nominal 90%. Calibrating within target strata restored coverage in every stratum and enlarged sets by a factor of about {{price.SDLC.geo}}, less than lowering the overall miscoverage level would. We show that conditional-density level sets with stratum-specific thresholds are the smallest stratum-valid sets. Their plug-in method, stratified density-level conformal prediction (SDLC), gave smaller sets than stratified absolute-residual and quantile methods, as a pre-specified analysis confirmed on {{conf.n_eval}} held-out datasets, but within a few percent of a stratified normalised residual, which covered the most extreme outcomes better. For coverage weighted by a relevance function, the size-optimal method RW-DLC covered {{J:statistics_review05.json|rw3_sensitivity/unified (y >= q99, >= 10 test points)/rw|pct}} of the top 1% of held-out outcomes against {{J:statistics_review05.json|rw3_sensitivity/unified (y >= q99, >= 10 test points)/sdlc|pct}} for SDLC with five strata; dedicated 1% strata matched this coverage but, where sparse, gave sets about a third larger. Both work with any regressor and cost microseconds per prediction in batches. A dedicated stratum suffices for a well-populated extreme range; relevance weighting suits sparse tails and coverage across the whole target range.
