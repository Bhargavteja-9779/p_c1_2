abstract: |
  Prediction intervals for regression are usually calibrated to hold on average over all outcomes, so they can systematically miss the rare, extreme outcomes that matter most in imbalanced regression. We study stratum-wise coverage, the probability that a prediction set contains the outcome given that the outcome lies in a target stratum such as the upper tail. We prove that even the oracle interval with exact covariate-conditional coverage under-covers extreme outcomes: in a Gaussian model with half of the variance explained it covers only {{prop1.upper10_r05}} of the top decile. Calibrating conformal thresholds within target strata, as in bin-conditional conformal prediction, guarantees stratum-wise coverage for any score; we show that the score then determines only the set size and that conditional-density level sets with stratum-specific thresholds are size-optimal. This yields stratified density-level conformal prediction (SDLC), a floor variant with simultaneous marginal and stratum-wise guarantees, and precise conditions for robustness to target shift. In a pre-registered benchmark on {{n_datasets}} OpenML-CTR23 datasets with {{n_seeds}} random splits each, marginal and covariate-adaptive conformal methods covered only {{marg.upper_min}}–{{marg.upper_max}} of upper-tail outcomes at a nominal 90%, whereas stratified methods covered {{strat.upper_min}}–{{strat.upper_max}}, consistent with exact validity, at a median size increase of {{price.S-NCP.med}}–{{price.SDLC (proposed).med}}-fold. SDLC produced smaller stratum-valid sets than bin-conditional conformal prediction (geometric-mean ratio {{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-Res/geo|f2}}) and binned quantile regression ({{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-CQR/geo|f2}}), replicated with a neural base learner, and more targeted alerts for extreme outcomes. Code and all raw results are released.
