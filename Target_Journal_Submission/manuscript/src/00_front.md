abstract: |
  Expert systems that value, insure or inspect are judged by rare, extreme outcomes, yet their prediction intervals hold only on average. In a house-valuation benchmark, standard 90% conformal intervals covered only {{J:case_study_dollars.json|kings_county/SCP/top_coverage_10seeds_mean|pct}} of the most expensive tenth of sales. We study stratum-wise coverage: the probability that a set contains the outcome given that the outcome lies in a target stratum, such as the upper tail. Even the oracle interval with exact covariate-conditional coverage under-covers the tails: in a Gaussian model with half of the variance explained it covers {{prop1.upper10_r05}} of the top decile. Calibrating conformal thresholds within target strata guarantees it for any score, so the score determines only efficiency. We show that conditional-density level sets with stratum-specific thresholds are the smallest stratum-valid sets, that their plug-in conformal version is asymptotically optimal, and that the gain over a normalised residual comes from reallocating coverage across inputs. The resulting stratified density-level conformal prediction (SDLC) was evaluated among {{n_methods_main}} procedures on {{n_datasets}} OpenML-CTR23 datasets. At a nominal 90%, marginal and covariate-adaptive methods covered only {{marg.worst_stratum.min}}–{{marg.worst_stratum.max}} of their worst-covered stratum, whereas stratified methods were consistent with exact validity. SDLC gave the smallest stratum-valid sets on average, smaller than bin-conditional conformal prediction on {{h3.BCCP.wins}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets, and a pre-registered replication on {{conf.n_eval}} held-out datasets confirmed the ordering. Gains over the closest competitors were small (ratios up to {{C-H3.S-HPD.geo}}, intervals including one) and cost coverage of the noisiest inputs. A decision study shows when dedicated tail classifiers are preferable for alerts.
