title: "Covering rare outcomes in imbalanced regression: stratified and relevance-weighted conformal prediction with density scores"

abstract: |
  Conformal prediction intervals for regression are usually calibrated to hold on average over all outcomes, so they can systematically miss the rare, extreme outcomes that matter most in imbalanced regression. Even the ideal interval with exact coverage at every input covers only {{prop1.upper10_r05}} of the top decile of the target in a Gaussian model with half of the variance explained. We therefore study two guarantees for rare outcomes. For coverage within every stratum (range) of the target, calibrating separately within each stratum is an established technique; we show that conditional-density level sets with stratum-specific thresholds are the smallest valid sets and evaluate the resulting method, stratified density-level conformal prediction (SDLC). For coverage weighted by a relevance function, as used in imbalanced regression, we propose relevance-weighted density-level conformal prediction (RW-DLC), which needs no strata and has a finite-sample guarantee. On {{n_datasets}} datasets of the OpenML-CTR23 benchmark suite, seven standard conformal methods covered only {{marg.worst_stratum.min}}–{{marg.worst_stratum.max}} of their worst-covered stratum at a nominal 90%, whereas stratified methods stayed within sampling error of the nominal level. SDLC gave smaller sets than stratified methods based on absolute residuals or quantiles, which a pre-specified analysis plan confirmed on {{conf.n_eval}} held-out datasets (4,209–50,000 rows, no extremely skewed targets), but its sets were within a few percent of those of a stratified normalised residual. On the held-out datasets, RW-DLC met its relevance-weighted target and covered {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/rw|pct}} of the top 1% of outcomes, against {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/sdlc|pct}} for SDLC, at the cost of lower coverage of typical outcomes.
