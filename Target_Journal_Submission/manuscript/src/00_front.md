title: "Covering rare outcomes in imbalanced regression: stratified and relevance-weighted conformal prediction with density scores"

abstract: |
  Conformal prediction intervals for regression are usually calibrated to hold on average over all outcomes, so they can systematically miss the rare, extreme outcomes that matter most in imbalanced regression. Even an ideal interval with exact coverage at every input covers only {{prop1.upper10_r05}} of the top decile in a Gaussian model with half of the variance explained. We therefore study two guarantees for rare outcomes. For coverage within every stratum (range) of the target, calibrating within each stratum is established; we show that conditional-density level sets with stratum-specific thresholds are the smallest valid sets and evaluate the resulting method, stratified density-level conformal prediction (SDLC). For coverage weighted by a relevance function, as used in imbalanced regression, we evaluate relevance-weighted density-level conformal prediction (RW-DLC), which needs no strata. On {{n_datasets}} datasets of the OpenML-CTR23 benchmark suite, seven standard conformal methods covered only {{marg.worst_stratum.min}}–{{marg.worst_stratum.max}} of their worst-covered stratum at a nominal 90%, whereas stratified methods stayed within sampling error of the nominal level. SDLC gave smaller sets than stratified methods based on absolute residuals or quantiles, which a pre-specified analysis plan confirmed on {{conf.n_eval}} held-out datasets (4,209–50,000 rows, no extremely skewed targets), but its sets were within a few percent of those of a stratified normalised residual, which covered the most extreme outcomes better. On held-out data, RW-DLC met its relevance-weighted target and covered {{J:statistics_review05.json|rw3_sensitivity/unified (y >= q99, >= 10 test points)/rw|pct}} of the top 1% of outcomes, against {{J:statistics_review05.json|rw3_sensitivity/unified (y >= q99, >= 10 test points)/sdlc|pct}} for SDLC with five strata; SDLC with dedicated 1% tail strata covered {{J:statistics_review08.json|S7.top1_mean/SDLC-S7|pct}} with similar set sizes.
