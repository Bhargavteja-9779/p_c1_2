# Conclusion {#sec:conclusion}

Prediction intervals that are valid on average can fail exactly where imbalanced regression needs them: on the rare outcomes. We showed that this failure is structural, since even the oracle interval with exact covariate-conditional coverage misses most extreme outcomes, and that calibrating conformal thresholds within strata of the target, an established Mondrian device, restores coverage for rare outcomes in finite samples and under shifts of the stratum frequencies. Our main methodological contribution concerns efficiency: among all sets with stratum-wise coverage, conditional-density level sets with stratum-specific thresholds are the smallest, which leads to SDLC. In a pre-registered benchmark on {{n_datasets}} datasets with {{n_seeds}} splits each, stratified calibration raised the worst-stratum coverage from {{marg.worst_min}}–{{marg.worst_max}} to about {{main.SDLC-LS.worst_stratum}} (consistent with exact validity), at a median set-size increase of {{price.S-NCP.med}}–{{price.SDLC (proposed).med}}-fold; SDLC gave smaller stratum-valid sets than bin-conditional conformal prediction with residual scores (geometric-mean ratio {{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-Res/geo|f2}}) and than binned quantile-regression calibration ({{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-CQR/geo|f2}}), and more targeted tail alerts, with gains that replicated with a neural base learner. Its advantage over a stratified normalised residual was smaller and depended on the quality of the scale model. Future work should combine stratified calibration with more flexible conditional density estimators, data-efficient cross-fitted calibration for sparse strata, and dependent data.
