# Conclusion and future work {#sec:conclusion}

Prediction intervals that are valid on average can fail exactly where imbalanced regression needs them: on the rare outcomes. Even the oracle interval with exact covariate-conditional coverage under-covers the tails of the target, and on {{n_datasets}} benchmark datasets marginal and covariate-adaptive conformal methods covered only {{marg.worst_stratum.min}}–{{marg.worst_stratum.max}} of the outcomes in their worst-covered stratum at a nominal 90%. We studied two guarantees that protect rare outcomes and the sets that meet them most efficiently.

Calibrating thresholds within strata of the target, an established Mondrian device, guarantees coverage in every stratum for any score. For this guarantee, conditional-density level sets with stratum-specific thresholds are the smallest sets for the oracle, and SDLC, their plug-in version, gave smaller sets than bin-conditional conformal prediction with residual scores and than stratified quantile regression, on most CTR23 datasets and on {{conf.n_eval}} held-out datasets under a pre-specified analysis. Among scores that use the estimated scale, however, the differences were within a few per cent, and the simpler stratified normalised residual covered noisy inputs and the most extreme outcomes better. Strata also protect only at their own resolution. Relevance-weighted calibration removes that limit: RW-DLC guarantees coverage averaged by an imbalanced-regression relevance function in finite samples, uses the score that is size-optimal for that guarantee, and in a second pre-specified study covered {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/rw|pct}} of the top 1% of outcomes on held-out data, against {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/sdlc|pct}} for SDLC, at a cost in coverage of the bulk. For yes/no decisions about extremes, dedicated conformal tail classifiers attain the same recall guarantee with fewer alerts.

Future work should combine the two guarantees, for example a relevance-weighted guarantee with a floor on every stratum; learn relevance functions or strata that minimise size subject to validity; replace the location–scale density by flexible conditional density estimators, whose gap to the oracle was largest under covariate-dependent multimodality; and extend both guarantees to grouped and dependent data, where our grouped-split analysis showed that efficiency depends on transfer to unseen units.
