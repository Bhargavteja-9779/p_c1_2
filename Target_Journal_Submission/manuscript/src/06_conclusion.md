# Conclusion and future work {#sec:conclusion}

Prediction intervals that are valid on average can fail exactly where imbalanced regression needs them: on the rare outcomes. Even the oracle interval with exact covariate-conditional coverage under-covers the tails of the target, and on {{n_datasets}} benchmark datasets marginal and covariate-adaptive conformal methods covered only {{marg.worst_stratum.min}}–{{marg.worst_stratum.max}} of the outcomes in their worst-covered stratum at a nominal 90%. Calibrating thresholds within strata of the target, an established Mondrian device, restores coverage of rare outcomes in finite samples for any score, holds exactly under shifts that re-weight whole strata, and buys more tail coverage per unit of size than lowering the miscoverage level.

The score then determines efficiency. Conditional-density level sets with stratum-specific thresholds are the smallest stratum-valid sets for the oracle, and their plug-in conformal version is asymptotically optimal. The resulting method, SDLC, gave smaller stratum-valid sets than bin-conditional conformal prediction with residual scores and than stratified quantile and HPD scores on most CTR23 datasets, and the ordering was confirmed on {{conf.n_eval}} held-out datasets under a pre-registered analysis. Its advantage over a stratified normalised residual is small and comes mostly from reallocating coverage from noisy to quiet inputs, at the price of lower coverage of the noisiest inputs and of the most extreme outcomes. For yes/no decisions about extremes, dedicated conformal tail classifiers attain the same recall guarantee with fewer alerts, whereas set-based alerts are cheaper when missed extremes are very costly.

Future work should combine stratified calibration with flexible conditional density estimators, whose gap to the oracle was largest under covariate-dependent multimodality; with data-efficient calibration for sparse strata and overlapping groups; and with dependent data.
