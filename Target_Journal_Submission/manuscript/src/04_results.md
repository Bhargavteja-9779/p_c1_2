## Marginal and covariate-adaptive methods under-cover the tails (RQ1)

All methods attained their nominal marginal coverage, but coverage of the target strata differed sharply. The seven marginal or covariate-adaptive methods covered {{marg.upper_tail_cov.min}}–{{marg.upper_tail_cov.max}} of the targets in the upper stratum $B_K$ and {{marg.top1_cov.min}}–{{marg.top1_cov.max}} of the top 1%, and their worst-stratum coverage was {{marg.worst_stratum.min}}–{{marg.worst_stratum.max}}. On {{marg.below.min}}–{{marg.below.max}} of the {{n_datasets}} datasets, it was below the range expected under exact validity. Marginal methods over-cover the central deciles and pay for it in the outer ones (Fig. [-@fig:deciles]); covariate adaptivity reduces the shortfall but does not remove it. Every stratified method, in contrast, covered {{strat.upper_tail_cov.min}}–{{strat.upper_tail_cov.max}} of the upper stratum, with worst-stratum coverage {{strat.worst_stratum.min}}–{{strat.worst_stratum.max}}, and was below the null range on at most {{strat.below.max}} datasets, as expected under exact validity. SDLC's worst-stratum coverage exceeded that of each marginal method on at least {{h1w.minwins}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets (one-sided Holm-adjusted $p\le$ {{h1w.maxp}}; Hodges–Lehmann differences {{h1w.hlmin}}–{{h1w.hlmax}}). The result is unchanged without the development datasets ($p\le$ {{h1wx.maxp}}) and for coverage of the upper stratum alone ($p\le$ {{h1u.maxp}}). SDLC covered {{main.SDLC-LS.top5_cov.pct}} of the top 5% and {{main.SDLC-LS.top1_cov.pct}} of the top 1%, against at most {{marg.top5_cov.max}} and {{marg.top1_cov.max}} for the marginal methods; the top 1% is not calibrated, and its coverage stays below the nominal level for every method.

## The price of stratum-wise validity (RQ2)

Stratum-wise validity enlarges the sets (Table [-@tbl:price]). Holding the score fixed, stratified calibration increased the geometric-mean size by factors of {{price.S-NCP.geo}} (normalised residual) to {{price.BCCP.geo}} (absolute residual). For SDLC relative to the single-threshold density level set (DLS) the factor was {{price.SDLC.geo}} ({{price.SDLC.lo}}–{{price.SDLC.hi}}), with a median of {{price.SDLC.med}}, for a gain in worst-stratum coverage of {{price.SDLC.gain}} (between {{price.gain.min}} and {{price.gain.max}} across scores). The price exceeded 1.5 on only {{price.SDLC.n15}} datasets for SDLC (Supplementary Section S8.7).

A fairer comparison is with marginal sets that buy tail coverage by lowering $\alpha$ (Table [-@tbl:matched]). At $\alpha=0.05$ every marginal method still had a lower worst-stratum coverage ({{mc.SCP.worst}}–{{mc.DCP.worst}}) than SDLC at $\alpha=0.1$ ({{main.SDLC-LS.worst_stratum}}), and SDLC's sets were smaller than theirs on {{mc.nsmaller.min}}–{{mc.nsmaller.max}} of the {{n_datasets}} datasets. Calibrating within strata thus gives more tail coverage for less size than raising the overall level.

## Density, residual and quantile scores under stratified calibration (RQ3) {#sec:rq3}

Among the stratified methods, which all had equivalent worst-stratum coverage (TOST with margin 0.01: $p\le$ {{tost.S-NCP}} for every comparison with SDLC), SDLC produced the smallest sets (Table [-@tbl:size], Fig. [-@fig:tradeoff]). Its sets were smaller than those of BCCP on {{h3.BCCP.wins}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets (geometric-mean ratio {{h3.BCCP.geo}}, 95% CI {{h3.BCCP.lo}}–{{h3.BCCP.hi}}; Holm-adjusted $p$ {{h3.BCCP.p}}), than those of stratified CQR on {{h3.S-CQR.wins}} (ratio {{h3.S-CQR.geo}}) and than those of S-HPD on {{h3.S-HPD.wins}} (ratio {{h3.S-HPD.geo}}). Against the information-matched S-NCP, SDLC was smaller on {{h3.S-NCP.wins}} datasets (Holm $p$ {{h3.S-NCP.p}}; Hodges–Lehmann ratio {{h3.S-NCP.hlr}}), but the gain was small and its geometric-mean ratio, {{h3.S-NCP.geo}} ({{h3.S-NCP.lo}}–{{h3.S-NCP.hi}}), was pulled towards one by a few datasets with large losses (Supplementary Section S8.7). The Hodges–Lehmann ratio, which describes the typical dataset, is our primary size estimand. Post hoc equivalence tests, seed-level reliability counts and the interval (hull) forms are reported in Supplementary Section S8.1.

SDLC remained smaller than all four stratified comparators without the development datasets, on the {{n_nominal}} datasets with near-nominal tail strata, at other $\alpha$ and with three or ten strata (Holm $p\le$ {{sens.robust.maxp}}; Supplementary Table S5). Two subsets were less clear. On the {{sens.small.S-NCP.n}} datasets with fewer than 1,600 observations no stratified score was consistently better (SDLC vs S-NCP: {{sens.small.S-NCP.wins}}/{{sens.small.S-NCP.losses}}). On the {{sens.skewed.S-NCP.n}} strongly skewed datasets, the advantage over S-NCP ({{sens.skewed.S-NCP.wins}}/{{sens.skewed.S-NCP.losses}}, Holm $p$ = {{sens.skewed.S-NCP.p}}) and S-CQR ($p$ = {{sens.skewed.S-CQR.p}}) was not significant.

### Confirmatory replication on held-out datasets {#sec:confirmatory}

On the {{conf.n_eval}} held-out datasets, analysed exactly as pre-specified (Supplementary Section S6), all four hypotheses were supported (Table [-@tbl:confirmatory]).

* *C-H1 (tail coverage).* SDLC's worst-stratum coverage exceeded that of each of the seven marginal and covariate-adaptive methods (Holm $p$ {{C-H1.maxp}}).
* *C-H2 (validity).* Its worst-stratum coverage fell below the 5% quantile of the exact-validity null reference on {{C-H2.below}} of {{conf.n_eval}} datasets (critical value {{C-H2.crit}}).
* *C-H3 (set size).* SDLC's sets were smaller than those of BCCP (geometric-mean ratio {{C-H3.BCCP.geo}}, {{C-H3.BCCP.wins}}/{{C-H3.BCCP.losses}} datasets, Holm $p$ = {{C-H3.BCCP.p}}), S-CQR ({{C-H3.S-CQR.geo}}, {{C-H3.S-CQR.wins}}/{{C-H3.S-CQR.losses}}, $p$ {{C-H3.S-CQR.p}}), S-NCP ({{C-H3.S-NCP.geo}}, {{C-H3.S-NCP.wins}}/{{C-H3.S-NCP.losses}}, $p$ = {{C-H3.S-NCP.p}}) and S-HPD ({{C-H3.S-HPD.geo}}, {{C-H3.S-HPD.wins}}/{{C-H3.S-HPD.losses}}, $p$ = {{C-H3.S-HPD.p}}). By the pre-specified rule (signed-rank test plus direction of the geometric-mean ratio), every comparison favours SDLC. The gains over S-NCP and S-HPD are small: the bootstrap intervals of their geometric-mean ratios ({{C-H3.S-NCP.lo}}–{{C-H3.S-NCP.hi}} and {{C-H3.S-HPD.lo}}–{{C-H3.S-HPD.hi}}) include one, although the reduction occurs on most datasets.
* *C-H4 (equivalence).* The worst-stratum coverage of SDLC was equivalent to that of S-NCP and BCCP within 0.02 (TOST $p$ {{C-H4.S-NCP}} and {{C-H4.BCCP}}).

C-H1, C-H2 and C-H4 follow from Theorem 1 and check the implementation; only C-H3 could have failed for empirical reasons. The pre-specified secondary analyses were less favourable for the interval forms (SDLC-hull was not smaller than S-NCP-hull), and post hoc checks of the held-out collection (duplicate pair merged, continuous targets only, nominal tail strata) left every C-H3 decision unchanged; the advantage over S-NCP was confined to continuous targets and disappeared for strongly skewed ones (Supplementary Section S8.3, Table S17b).

## Relevance-weighted coverage of the most extreme outcomes (RQ4) {#sec:rw-results}

Stratified calibration left the most extreme outcomes under-covered (top-1% coverage of SDLC {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/sdlc|f3}} on the held-out datasets). RW-DLC was evaluated under its own pre-specified plan (Section [-@sec:protocol]); Table [-@tbl:rw_conf] reports the held-out datasets ({{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/n_datasets|int}} datasets, {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/n_runs|int}} runs); Supplementary Table S18 lists the hypothesis tests. The plan also specified an exploratory arm on the {{n_datasets}} CTR23 datasets (Supplementary Table S19), reported after the held-out results below.

* *RW-1 (validity).* The relevance-weighted coverage of RW-DLC was {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-1/mean|f3}} on average, equivalent to the nominal 0.90 within the pre-specified margin of 0.02 (TOST $p$ {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-1/p_tost|p}}), as Theorem 2 predicts.
* *RW-2 (marginal methods miss the weighted target).* RW-DLC exceeded every marginal method in relevance-weighted coverage, by {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-2/NCP/diff_mean|f3}} (NCP) to {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-2/DLS-M/diff_mean|f3}} (DLS) on average (Holm-adjusted $p\le$ {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-2/NCP/p_holm|p}} for all four comparisons).
* *RW-3 (extreme tail).* RW-DLC covered {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/rw|f3}} of the top 1% of outcomes, against {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/sdlc|f3}} for SDLC (mean difference {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/diff_mean|f3}}, 95% CI {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/ci/0|f3}}–{{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/ci/1|f3}}, higher on {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/wins|int}} of {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/n|int}} datasets with top-1% test points, one-sided $p$ = {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/p|p}}). The definition-sensitivity of RW-3 is reported in Supplementary Section S8.3 (it held with the main-pipeline definition of the top 1%).
* *RW-4 (score).* RW-DLC's sets were smaller than those of the two ablations that use relevance-weighted calibration with an unweighted score, RW-DLS (geometric-mean ratio {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-4/RW-DLS-U/geo_ratio|f3}}) and RW-NCP ({{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-4/RW-NCP-U/geo_ratio|f3}}), but after Holm correction neither difference reached the pre-specified threshold ($p$ = {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-4/RW-DLS-U/p_holm|p}} for both), so RW-4 was not supported.

On the CTR23 datasets all four hypotheses were supported: relevance-weighted coverage {{J:rw_statistics.json|ctr23/RW-1/mean|f3}} (TOST $p$ {{J:rw_statistics.json|ctr23/RW-1/p_tost|p}}), top-1% coverage {{J:rw_statistics.json|ctr23/RW-3/rw|f3}} against {{J:rw_statistics.json|ctr23/RW-3/sdlc|f3}} for SDLC (higher on {{J:rw_statistics.json|ctr23/RW-3/wins|int}} of {{J:rw_statistics.json|ctr23/RW-3/n|int}} datasets), and, unlike on the held-out data, smaller sets than RW-DLS and RW-NCP (ratios {{J:rw_statistics.json|ctr23/RW-4/RW-DLS-U/geo_ratio|f3}} and {{J:rw_statistics.json|ctr23/RW-4/RW-NCP-U/geo_ratio|f3}}, Holm $p$ {{J:rw_statistics.json|ctr23/RW-4/RW-DLS-U/p_holm|p}}).

The guarantee has a price elsewhere. Because RW-DLC averages coverage over the target range, it spends less on the bulk: its marginal coverage was {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/marg_cov_rw_mean|f3}} (minimum over datasets {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/marg_cov_rw_min|f3}}) and its worst coverage over target deciles was lower than that of SDLC (mean difference {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/descriptive/SDLC/worst_diff|f3}}). Its sets were not reliably larger than SDLC's (geometric-mean ratio {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/descriptive/SDLC/size_geo_ratio|f3}}, Hodges–Lehmann {{J:statistics_review08.json|rw_vs_sdlc_size/hl|f3}}, median {{J:statistics_review08.json|rw_vs_sdlc_size/median|f3}}, 95% CI {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/descriptive/SDLC/size_geo_lo|f3}}–{{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/descriptive/SDLC/size_geo_hi|f3}}, descriptive). No test point had an infinite threshold; the smallest effective calibration sample size was {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/ess_U_min|int}} (median {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/ess_U_median|int}}). The milder relevance $\phi_H$ gave intermediate results. RW-DLC and SDLC thus answer different requirements: SDLC guarantees every stratum, and RW-DLC guarantees an average over the whole target range that reaches the outcomes beyond the strata (Fig. [-@fig:rw]).

*Dedicated tail strata (post hoc).* RW-DLC's advantage in RQ4 is over SDLC with the five primary strata, whose highest stratum is the top 10%. Stratified calibration can target the top 1% directly: with stratum edges at the training quantiles 0.01, 0.10, 0.25, 0.75, 0.90 and 0.99, the 1% strata received enough calibration points for a finite threshold on all but {{J:statistics_review08.json|S7.datasets_with_inf_threshold|int}} held-out dataset, where the method returned the whole stratum in some seeds. Evaluated from the cached model outputs (Supplementary Table S17b), SDLC with these seven strata covered {{J:statistics_review08.json|S7.top1_mean/SDLC-S7|f3}} of the top 1% (minimum over datasets {{J:statistics_review08.json|S7.top1_min/SDLC-S7|f2}}), against {{J:statistics_review08.json|vs SDLC-S7/top1_rw|f3}} for RW-DLC, with a marginal coverage of {{J:statistics_review08.json|S7.cov_mean/SDLC-S7|f3}} against {{J:statistics_review08.json|vs SDLC-S7/cov_rw|f3}}; RW-DLC's sets were not reliably smaller (geometric-mean ratio {{J:statistics_review08.json|vs SDLC-S7/size_geo|f3}}, Hodges–Lehmann {{J:statistics_review08.json|vs SDLC-S7/size_hl|f3}}, smaller on {{J:statistics_review08.json|vs SDLC-S7/size_wins|int}} of {{J:statistics_review08.json|n_datasets|int}} datasets). On the smaller CTR23 datasets the picture changed. Dedicated strata again covered the top 1% as well as RW-DLC ({{J:statistics_review08.json|ctr23.S7.top1_mean/SDLC-S7|f3}} against {{J:statistics_review08.json|ctr23.vs SDLC-S7/top1_rw|f3}}), but their 1% strata were often too sparse for a finite threshold ({{J:statistics_review08.json|ctr23.S7.datasets_with_inf_threshold|int}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets), so that the method returned whole tail strata, and RW-DLC's sets were smaller on {{J:statistics_review08.json|ctr23.vs SDLC-S7/size_wins|int}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets (geometric-mean ratio {{J:statistics_review08.json|ctr23.vs SDLC-S7/size_geo|f3}}, Holm $p$ {{J:statistics_review08.json|ctr23.vs SDLC-S7/size_p_holm|p}}), at a lower marginal coverage ({{J:statistics_review08.json|ctr23.vs SDLC-S7/cov_rw|f3}} against {{J:statistics_review08.json|ctr23.vs SDLC-S7/cov_comp|f3}}). When a known extreme range has enough calibration points, a dedicated stratum delivers its coverage as well as relevance weighting; when it does not, RW-DLC reaches the same coverage of the extremes with much smaller sets, and it gives a guarantee averaged over the whole target range without a choice of strata.

## Target shift (RQ5) {#sec:shift}

Under the positive control, which re-weights whole strata, the stratum-wise coverage of every stratified method was unchanged, as Corollary 1(i) states, and their marginal coverage stayed at the nominal level ({{T:T8_shift|method=SDLC|pc_marginal|f3}} for SDLC), whereas that of the marginal methods fell to {{T:T8_shift|method=SCP|pc_marginal|f3}}–{{T:T8_shift|method=Mondrian-ŷ|pc_marginal|f3}} (Fig. [-@fig:shift]). Continuous tilts towards the upper tail also move mass within strata; under a strong tilt every method lost coverage of the upper stratum, the stratified ones less ({{T:T8_shift|method=S-CQR|up2_upper|f3}}–{{T:T8_shift|method=S-NCP|up2_upper|f3}}, against {{T:T8_shift|method=SCP|up2_upper|f3}}–{{T:T8_shift|method=DCP|up2_upper|f3}}), and SDLC less than S-NCP (Supplementary Section S8.4).

## Tail alerts and decisions (RQ6)

Every stratified method guarantees an upper-tail alert recall of at least 0.9; their mean recall was {{alert.strat.recall.min}}–{{alert.strat.recall.max}}, and it was at least 0.9 on all {{n_datasets}} datasets (Supplementary Table S23). The price is a high alert rate. Among the stratified methods SDLC raised fewer alerts than BCCP ({{al.hi.BCCP.wins}}/{{al.hi.BCCP.losses}} datasets) and S-CQR, but more than S-NCP ({{al.hi.S-NCP.wins}}/{{al.hi.S-NCP.losses}}). The dedicated tail classifier TC-LS attains the guaranteed recall almost exactly (mean {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=TC-LS|recall|f3}}) and raised fewer alerts than SDLC on every dataset (alert-rate ratio {{al.hi.TC-LS.geo}}); for a pure yes/no decision it is the more efficient tool. Conformal selection [@jin2023selection] controls the false discovery rate instead and flagged few units, with high precision (mean {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=CS-BH-0.2|precision|f3}} at $q_{\mathrm{FDR}}=0.2$) but a mean recall of only {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=CS-BH-0.2|recall|f3}}; the point-prediction rule (Point) behaved similarly (recall {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=Point|recall|f3}}).

The preferable rule depends on the cost $c$ of a miss relative to a false alert (Fig. [-@fig:costs]; details in Supplementary Section S8.5). With equal costs, rules that raise few alerts (the point rule, conformal selection) were cheapest. At $c=10$ SDLC's expected cost did not differ significantly from that of TC-LS, and at $c=20$ SDLC was cheaper on most datasets, because its recall exceeds the guaranteed 0.9; among the stratified scores, however, S-NCP gave the cheapest alerts.

## Sensitivity analyses (RQ7) {#sec:robustness}

*Robustness.* The conclusions held at $\alpha=0.05$ and $0.2$, with three or ten strata, with a quarter of the calibration set (larger sets, because sparse tail strata then return the whole stratum), with a two-layer perceptron instead of gradient boosting, and with imbalance-aware DenseWeight training, which improved the tail coverage of marginal methods but did not remove the shortfall. Splits that keep repeated units together or hold out whole months changed little, but splitting video transcoding by video and Moneyball forward in time enlarged all sets several-fold and removed SDLC's size advantage (SDLC larger than BCCP). The price of stratification rose with target skewness and fell with calibration-set size; SDLC was larger than S-NCP only on small or heavily tied datasets. Details are in Supplementary Section S8.6–S8.7 and Tables S5–S8 and S15.
*Computational cost.* Measured like-for-like (same inputs, one thread, model predictions excluded; Supplementary Table S13), computing a prediction set took a median of {{J:timing_summary.json|like/SDLC/pred_us_median|f1}} µs per test point for SDLC in batches, against {{J:timing_summary.json|like/S-NCP/pred_us_median|f2}} µs for S-NCP, a median factor of {{J:timing_summary.json|like/ratio_pred_sdlc_sncp/median|int}}; including the model predictions, end-to-end prediction was only {{J:timing_summary.json|like/ratio_e2e_sdlc_sncp/median|f2}} times slower (median over datasets; at most {{J:timing_summary.json|like/ratio_e2e_sdlc_sncp/max|int}} times, on a dataset with a very cheap point model). A single query outside a batch took about 6 ms (median over datasets; Supplementary Table S13), dominated by fixed per-call overhead. Calibration took at most {{J:timing_summary.json|like/SDLC/cal_ms_max|f1}} ms. RW-DLC, computed on a grid of 4,000 target values because its threshold depends on the candidate, cost {{J:timing_summary.json|rw/RW-DLC/us_median|int}} µs per test point, as much as SDLC on the same grid (ratio {{J:timing_summary.json|rw/ratio_rwdlc_sdlc_grid_median|f2}}); fitting its relevance function took at most {{J:timing_summary.json|rw/relevance_fit_s_max|f1}} s, its peak memory was at most {{J:timing_summary.json|rw/peak_MB_G4000_max|int}} MB, and a finer grid changed mean set sizes by less than 0.1% (Supplementary Table S13). Both methods are therefore cheap next to model fitting, but an interval method remains preferable when single-query latency matters.
