## RQ1: Marginal and covariate-adaptive methods under-cover rare targets

All methods attained their nominal marginal coverage (Table [-@tbl:main]). Coverage in the target strata, however, differed sharply. The seven marginal or covariate-adaptive methods covered between {{marg.upper_min}} and {{marg.upper_max}} of the upper-tail targets (the top 10% of the training distribution) and their worst-stratum coverage ranged from {{marg.worst_min}} to {{marg.worst_max}}; split conformal prediction with absolute residuals covered only {{main.SCP.upper_tail_pct}} of the upper tail, and CQR only {{main.CQR.upper_tail_pct}}. Fig. [-@fig:deciles] shows the mechanism: marginal methods over-cover the central deciles and pay for it in the outer ones, exactly the pattern predicted by Proposition 1. Every stratified method, in contrast, covered {{strat.upper_min}}–{{strat.upper_max}} of the upper tail, with worst-stratum coverage between {{strat.worst_min}} and {{strat.worst_max}}.

{{FIG:deciles|F3_decile_coverage_profile.png|Coverage by decile of the training-target distribution, averaged over {{n_datasets}} datasets ($\alpha=0.1$). Marginal methods over-cover the bulk and under-cover both tails; stratified methods stay close to $1-\alpha$ in every decile.}}

The improvement is consistent across datasets (Fig. [-@fig:pertail]). SDLC had a higher worst-stratum coverage than every marginal or covariate-adaptive baseline on at least {{test.H1.worst_stratum.NCP.wins}} datasets (Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon $p$ {{H1.max_p}} for all seven comparisons; median gains {{H1.min_md}}–{{H1.max_md}}; rank-biserial correlation $\ge${{H1.min_rbc}}; Table [-@tbl:tests]), and the same held after removing the three development datasets ({{conf.n}} datasets; all Holm-adjusted $p$ {{conf.max_p}}). For the upper tail alone the gains were also significant for every comparison, with SDLC better than SCP on {{test.H1.upper_tail.SCP.wins}} datasets and better than CQR on {{test.H1.upper_tail.CQR.wins}}.

{{FIG:pertail|F4_upper_tail_per_dataset.png|Coverage of the upper-tail stratum (top 10% of training targets) for each dataset, averaged over splits ($\alpha=0.1$). The vertical line marks $1-\alpha$.}}

Are the stratified methods' worst-stratum values of about {{main.SDLC-LS.worst_stratum}}, slightly below 0.90, a sign of invalidity? No: the minimum over strata of finite-sample coverages is biased downwards even when every stratum is exactly valid. Under exact split-conformal validity, given the realised stratum sizes, the expected worst-stratum coverage is {{T:T2_main_results|code=SDLC-LS|null_worst_mean|f3}} on average. SDLC fell below the 5% quantile of this null distribution on {{T:T2_main_results|code=SDLC-LS|n_ds_below_null|int}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets and BCCP on {{T:T2_main_results|code=S-Res|n_ds_below_null|int}}, close to the $\approx 1.5$ expected by chance, whereas the marginal methods fell below it on {{T:T2_main_results|code=NCP|n_ds_below_null|int}}–{{T:T2_main_results|code=SCP|n_ds_below_null|int}} datasets. Fig. [-@fig:error] (right) shows the deviation from the null mean against calibration size.

Protection extends beyond the calibrated strata only partly. For the top 5% of targets, which lie inside the upper calibration stratum, SDLC covered {{main.SDLC-LS.top5_pct}}, against {{marg.top5_min}}–{{marg.top5_max}} for the marginal methods. At the resolution of deciles, which cut across the calibration strata, SDLC dipped slightly below the nominal level in deciles 7–9 (Fig. [-@fig:deciles]): the guarantee is per calibrated stratum, not per arbitrary sub-range, a point we return to in Section [-@sec:robustness].

## RQ2: The price of stratum-wise validity

Stratum-wise validity is not free. Relative to the same score with a single marginal threshold, stratified calibration increased the set size by a geometric mean of {{price.SDLC (proposed).gm}} {{price.SDLC (proposed).ci}} for the density score and between {{price.S-NCP.gm}} and {{price.BCCP.gm}} for the other scores (Table [-@tbl:price]); the median increase was smaller ({{price.S-NCP.med}}–{{price.SDLC (proposed).med}}), because a few datasets carry most of the cost. In exchange, worst-stratum coverage rose by {{price.S-NCP.gain}}–{{price.BCCP.gain}} on average. Fig. [-@fig:tradeoff] places all methods on this coverage–size plane. ## RQ3: Which score should be stratified? {#sec:rq3}

Theorem 2 predicts that, among stratum-valid sets, density level sets are the smallest. Among the pre-registered stratified procedures, SDLC produced the smallest sets in median (Table [-@tbl:main]): its sets were smaller than those of BCCP on {{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-Res/n_smaller|int}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets (geometric-mean ratio {{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-Res/geo|f3}}, 95% CI [{{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-Res/ci/0|f3}}, {{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-Res/ci/1|f3}}]; Holm-adjusted $p$ = {{test.H3.log_size.BCCP.p}}), smaller than those of the BFQR-style S-CQR on {{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-CQR/n_smaller|int}} (ratio {{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-CQR/geo|f3}}; $p$ = {{test.H3.log_size.S-CQR.p}}) and smaller than those of S-HPD on {{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-HPD-LS/n_smaller|int}} (ratio {{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-HPD-LS/geo|f3}}; $p$ {{test.H3.log_size.S-HPD.p}}); when intervals are required, SDLC-hull was smaller than BCCP-hull on {{J:statistics.json|hull_vs_bccp_hull/losses|int}} datasets (ratio {{J:statistics.json|hull_vs_bccp_hull/geo_ratio|f3}}; $p$ = {{J:statistics.json|hull_vs_bccp_hull/p|p}}). The comparison with the normalised-residual score is more nuanced. SDLC was smaller than S-NCP on {{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-NCP/n_smaller|int}} datasets ($p$ = {{test.H3.log_size.S-NCP.p}}), but the geometric-mean ratio was {{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-NCP/geo|f3}} ({{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-NCP/ci/0|f3}}–{{J:statistics.json|sdlc_size_ratios/S-NCP/ci/1|f3}}), because on a few datasets SDLC was considerably larger (Section [-@sec:error]): SDLC is typically, not uniformly, more efficient than S-NCP. All stratified methods had statistically indistinguishable worst-stratum coverage (all Holm-adjusted $p$ = {{test.H3.worst_stratum.BCCP.p}}), so the size differences are not bought with coverage.

## A decision view: tail alerts

A prediction set that reaches into the upper stratum can be read as an alert that the outcome may be extreme. By Theorem 1, the recall of such alerts, $P\{\hat C(X)\cap B_K\neq\emptyset\mid Y\in B_K\}\ge P\{Y\in\hat C(X)\mid Y\in B_K\}\ge 1-\alpha$, is guaranteed for stratified methods. Table [-@tbl:alerts] and Fig. [-@fig:alerts] show the price in false alarms. BCCP raised an upper-tail alert for {{T:T13_tail_alerts|method=BCCP|alert_hi_rate|pct}} of all test points, with precision {{T:T13_tail_alerts|method=BCCP|alert_hi_precision|f3}}; SDLC alerted for {{T:T13_tail_alerts|method=SDLC (proposed)|alert_hi_rate|pct}} with precision {{T:T13_tail_alerts|method=SDLC (proposed)|alert_hi_precision|f3}}, at a recall of {{T:T13_tail_alerts|method=SDLC (proposed)|alert_hi_recall|f3}} versus {{T:T13_tail_alerts|method=BCCP|alert_hi_recall|f3}}. The density score thus concentrates the extra coverage on the observations for which an extreme outcome is plausible, whereas the residual score of BCCP extends the upper-tail piece to many observations whose point prediction is far from the tail. S-NCP behaved like SDLC (alert rate {{T:T13_tail_alerts|method=S-NCP|alert_hi_rate|pct}}).

## RQ4: Robustness to target shift

Table [-@tbl:shift] and Fig. [-@fig:shift] report coverage when the test distribution is tilted towards the upper tail, towards both tails or towards the lower tail by importance weights $\exp(\gamma z)$, where $z$ is the normal score of the target under the training distribution. These tilts also re-weight targets *within* strata, so Corollary 1(i) does not apply exactly. Under a moderate upward tilt ($\gamma=1$) the stratified methods kept coverage between {{T:T8_label_shift|method=BCCP|up1|pct}} and {{T:T8_label_shift|method=BCCP-hull|up1|pct}}, whereas the marginal methods dropped to {{T:T8_label_shift|method=SCP|up1|pct}}–{{T:T8_label_shift|method=DCP|up1|pct}}. Under a strong upward tilt ($\gamma=2$) all methods lost coverage, but the stratified methods much less: SDLC retained {{T:T8_label_shift|method=SDLC (proposed)|up2|pct}} and S-NCP {{T:T8_label_shift|method=S-NCP|up2|pct}}, against {{T:T8_label_shift|method=SCP|up2|pct}} for SCP and {{T:T8_label_shift|method=CQR|up2|pct}} for CQR (one-sided Holm-adjusted $p$ for SDLC versus each marginal method at most {{shift.up2.max_p}}). The residual loss is the within-stratum effect described after Corollary 1: a strong tilt moves mass towards the outer end of the upper stratum, where every set is less likely to reach. The density score is somewhat more exposed to this effect than the normalised residual, because it spends less coverage on the most extreme part of a tail stratum.

*Miscoverage level.* The conclusions held at $\alpha=0.05$ and $\alpha=0.2$ (Table [-@tbl:alpha]). At $\alpha=0.05$ the marginal methods' worst-stratum coverage ranged from {{T:T5_alpha_sensitivity|alpha=0.05&method=SCP|worst_stratum|f3}} to {{T:T5_alpha_sensitivity|alpha=0.05&method=DCP|worst_stratum|f3}} against {{T:T5_alpha_sensitivity|alpha=0.05&method=SDLC (proposed)|worst_stratum|f3}} for SDLC; the price of stratification increased because rare strata contain few calibration points, and S-NCP was then smaller than SDLC (normalised size {{T:T5_alpha_sensitivity|alpha=0.05&method=S-NCP|nsize_geo|f3}} versus {{T:T5_alpha_sensitivity|alpha=0.05&method=SDLC (proposed)|nsize_geo|f3}}). ## Error analysis {#sec:error}

Where does stratification cost the most, and where does SDLC lose to simpler scores? The price of stratum-wise validity (SDLC relative to DLS) was not associated with the predictability of the target (Spearman $\rho$ = {{ea.rho_price_rmse}} with RMSE/sd($y$), $p$ = {{ea.p_price_rmse}}) and only weakly with its skewness ($\rho$ = {{ea.rho_price_skew}}, $p$ = {{ea.p_price_skew}}); it was concentrated on a few datasets (Fig. [-@fig:error], left). The largest price occurred for forest fires, whose burned area is zero for {{J:metrics.json|forest_zero_frac|pct}} of the fires: the atom at zero merges the nominal lower strata into a single stratum holding about three quarters of the training targets, and covering the heavy upper tail of an almost unpredictable target conditionally on the target requires very long sets. Here SDLC, whose smooth density cannot represent the atom, was also less efficient than BCCP and S-NCP (size ratios {{T:T10_error_analysis_per_dataset|dataset=forest_fires|size_ratio_vs_SNCP|f2}} relative to S-NCP), as anticipated by the scope remark after Theorem 2. The other datasets on which SDLC was clearly larger than S-NCP (socmob, cars, concrete) are among the smallest in the suite ({{T:T1_datasets|dataset=cars|n|int}}–{{T:T1_datasets|dataset=socmob|n|int}} observations), where the scale model that enters the density score through the factor $1/\hat\sigma(x)$ is estimated from few points; on socmob SDLC was nevertheless much smaller than BCCP. On the target with the most ties (weekly working hours in the health-insurance data), coinciding quantiles reduced the realised number of strata to two or three, so the nominal "tails" were not tails; validity was unaffected. Small calibration sets also explain the lowest worst-stratum coverages (Fig. [-@fig:error], right), which remain within the range expected under exact validity.
