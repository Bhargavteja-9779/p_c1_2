## Marginal and covariate-adaptive methods under-cover the tails (RQ1)

All methods attained their nominal marginal coverage, but coverage of the target strata differed sharply. The seven marginal or covariate-adaptive methods covered {{marg.upper_tail_cov.min}}–{{marg.upper_tail_cov.max}} of the targets in the upper stratum $B_K$ and {{marg.top1_cov.min}}–{{marg.top1_cov.max}} of the top 1%, and their worst-stratum coverage was {{marg.worst_stratum.min}}–{{marg.worst_stratum.max}}. On {{marg.below.min}}–{{marg.below.max}} of the {{n_datasets}} datasets, it was below the range expected under exact validity. Marginal methods over-cover the central deciles and pay for it in the outer ones (Fig. [-@fig:deciles]); covariate adaptivity reduces the shortfall but does not remove it. Every stratified method, in contrast, covered {{strat.upper_tail_cov.min}}–{{strat.upper_tail_cov.max}} of the upper stratum, with worst-stratum coverage {{strat.worst_stratum.min}}–{{strat.worst_stratum.max}}, and was below the null range on at most {{strat.below.max}} datasets, as expected under exact validity. SDLC's worst-stratum coverage exceeded that of each marginal method on at least {{h1w.minwins}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets (one-sided Holm-adjusted $p\le$ {{h1w.maxp}}; Hodges–Lehmann differences {{h1w.hlmin}}–{{h1w.hlmax}}). The result is unchanged without the development datasets ($p\le$ {{h1wx.maxp}}) and for coverage of the upper stratum alone ($p\le$ {{h1u.maxp}}). SDLC covered {{main.SDLC-LS.top5_cov.pct}} of the top 5% and {{main.SDLC-LS.top1_cov.pct}} of the top 1%, against at most {{marg.top5_cov.max}} and {{marg.top1_cov.max}} for the marginal methods; the top 1% is not calibrated, and its coverage stays below the nominal level for every method.

Marginal methods (SCP, CQR, HPD-split) over-cover the centre and under-cover the outer deciles; stratified calibration (S-NCP, SDLC) flattens the profile. The residual dip in deciles 2 and 9 lies inside the broad shoulder strata.}}

## The price of stratum-wise validity (RQ2)

Stratum-wise validity enlarges the sets (Table [-@tbl:price]). Holding the score fixed, stratified calibration increased the geometric-mean size by factors of {{price.S-NCP.geo}} (normalised residual) to {{price.BCCP.geo}} (absolute residual). For SDLC relative to the single-threshold density level set (DLS) the factor was {{price.SDLC.geo}} ({{price.SDLC.lo}}–{{price.SDLC.hi}}), with a median of {{price.SDLC.med}}, for a gain in worst-stratum coverage of {{price.SDLC.gain}} (between {{price.gain.min}} and {{price.gain.max}} across scores). The price exceeded 1.5 on only {{price.SDLC.n15}} datasets for SDLC (Section [-@sec:error]).

A fairer comparison is with marginal sets that buy tail coverage by lowering $\alpha$ (Table [-@tbl:matched]). At $\alpha=0.05$ every marginal method still had a lower worst-stratum coverage ({{mc.SCP.worst}}–{{mc.DCP.worst}}) than SDLC at $\alpha=0.1$ ({{main.SDLC-LS.worst_stratum}}), and SDLC's sets were smaller than theirs on {{mc.nsmaller.min}}–{{mc.nsmaller.max}} of the {{n_datasets}} datasets. Calibrating within strata thus gives more tail coverage for less size than raising the overall level.

## Density, residual and quantile scores under stratified calibration (RQ3) {#sec:rq3}

### Benchmark datasets

Among the stratified methods, which all had equivalent worst-stratum coverage (TOST with margin 0.01: $p\le$ {{tost.S-NCP}} for every comparison with SDLC), SDLC produced the smallest sets (Table [-@tbl:size], Fig. [-@fig:tradeoff]). Its sets were smaller than those of BCCP on {{h3.BCCP.wins}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets (geometric-mean ratio {{h3.BCCP.geo}}, 95% CI {{h3.BCCP.lo}}–{{h3.BCCP.hi}}; Holm-adjusted $p$ {{h3.BCCP.p}}), than those of stratified CQR on {{h3.S-CQR.wins}} (ratio {{h3.S-CQR.geo}}) and than those of S-HPD on {{h3.S-HPD.wins}} (ratio {{h3.S-HPD.geo}}). Against the information-matched S-NCP, SDLC was smaller on {{h3.S-NCP.wins}} datasets (Holm $p$ {{h3.S-NCP.p}}; Hodges–Lehmann ratio {{h3.S-NCP.hlr}}), but the gain was small and its geometric-mean ratio, {{h3.S-NCP.geo}} ({{h3.S-NCP.lo}}–{{h3.S-NCP.hi}}), was pulled towards one by a few datasets with large losses (Section [-@sec:error]). The Hodges–Lehmann ratio, which describes the typical dataset, is our primary size estimand. Post hoc, equivalence within ±5% was not established with S-NCP (TOST $p$ = {{J:statistics_secondary.json|ctr23.tost5/S-NCP/p_tost|f3}}) or S-HPD ($p$ = {{J:statistics_secondary.json|ctr23.tost5/S-HPD/p_tost|f3}}), and SDLC's sets were reliably smaller than S-NCP's (seed-level 95% interval below zero) on {{J:statistics_secondary.json|ctr23.reliability/S-NCP/reliably_smaller|int}} datasets and reliably larger on {{J:statistics_secondary.json|ctr23.reliability/S-NCP/reliably_larger|int}}. When an interval is required, SDLC-hull was smaller than BCCP-hull (ratio {{h3h.BCCP-hull.geo}}, {{h3h.BCCP-hull.wins}}/{{h3h.BCCP-hull.losses}}), whereas against S-NCP-hull the median favoured SDLC-hull ({{h3h.S-NCP-hull.wins}}/{{h3h.S-NCP-hull.losses}}, Hodges–Lehmann ratio {{h3h.S-NCP-hull.hlr}}) and the geometric mean did not ({{h3h.S-NCP-hull.geo}}). These comparisons are exploratory (Section [-@sec:protocol]); Section [-@sec:confirmatory] tests them on held-out data.

SDLC remained smaller than all four stratified comparators without the development datasets, on the {{n_nominal}} datasets with near-nominal tail strata, at other $\alpha$ and with three or ten strata (Holm $p\le$ {{sens.robust.maxp}}; Supplementary Table S5). Two subsets were less clear. On the {{sens.small.S-NCP.n}} datasets with fewer than 1,600 observations no stratified score was consistently better (SDLC vs S-NCP: {{sens.small.S-NCP.wins}}/{{sens.small.S-NCP.losses}}). On the {{sens.skewed.S-NCP.n}} strongly skewed datasets, the advantage over S-NCP ({{sens.skewed.S-NCP.wins}}/{{sens.skewed.S-NCP.losses}}, Holm $p$ = {{sens.skewed.S-NCP.p}}) and S-CQR ($p$ = {{sens.skewed.S-CQR.p}}) was not significant.

### Synthetic study with known densities {#sec:synthetic}

Five synthetic designs with known densities (Table [-@tbl:synthetic]; A skewed, B covariate-dependent skewness, C bimodal, D heavy-tailed, E zero-inflated) separate the score from its estimate. With the true density, the oracle SDLC set was smaller than the oracle normalised residual in every design, by {{J:metrics_v3.json|synthetic_oracle_gain_min|pct}} to {{J:metrics_v3.json|synthetic_oracle_gain_max|pct}}. The estimated SDLC was smaller than S-NCP in {{J:metrics_v3.json|synthetic_est_lt_sncp|int}} of 50 design-seed combinations, but under the misspecified design C its sets were {{J:metrics_v3.json|synthetic_gap_C|pct}} larger than the oracle's. Under a genuine label shift (Supplementary Table S10), all stratified methods degraded similarly.

### Confirmatory replication on held-out datasets {#sec:confirmatory}

On the {{conf.n_eval}} held-out datasets, analysed exactly as pre-specified (Supplementary Section S6), all four hypotheses were supported (Table [-@tbl:confirmatory]).

* *C-H1 (tail coverage).* SDLC's worst-stratum coverage exceeded that of each of the seven marginal and covariate-adaptive methods (Holm $p$ {{C-H1.maxp}}).
* *C-H2 (validity).* Its worst-stratum coverage fell below the 5% quantile of the exact-validity null reference on {{C-H2.below}} of {{conf.n_eval}} datasets (critical value {{C-H2.crit}}).
* *C-H3 (set size).* SDLC's sets were smaller than those of BCCP (geometric-mean ratio {{C-H3.BCCP.geo}}, {{C-H3.BCCP.wins}}/{{C-H3.BCCP.losses}} datasets, Holm $p$ = {{C-H3.BCCP.p}}), S-CQR ({{C-H3.S-CQR.geo}}, {{C-H3.S-CQR.wins}}/{{C-H3.S-CQR.losses}}, $p$ {{C-H3.S-CQR.p}}), S-NCP ({{C-H3.S-NCP.geo}}, {{C-H3.S-NCP.wins}}/{{C-H3.S-NCP.losses}}, $p$ = {{C-H3.S-NCP.p}}) and S-HPD ({{C-H3.S-HPD.geo}}, {{C-H3.S-HPD.wins}}/{{C-H3.S-HPD.losses}}, $p$ = {{C-H3.S-HPD.p}}). By the pre-specified rule (signed-rank test plus direction of the geometric-mean ratio), every comparison favours SDLC. The gains over S-NCP and S-HPD are small: the bootstrap intervals of their geometric-mean ratios ({{C-H3.S-NCP.lo}}–{{C-H3.S-NCP.hi}} and {{C-H3.S-HPD.lo}}–{{C-H3.S-HPD.hi}}) include one, although the reduction occurs on most datasets.
* *C-H4 (equivalence).* The worst-stratum coverage of SDLC was equivalent to that of S-NCP and BCCP within 0.02 (TOST $p$ {{C-H4.S-NCP}} and {{C-H4.BCCP}}).

C-H1, C-H2 and C-H4 follow from Theorem 1 and check the implementation; only C-H3 could have failed for empirical reasons. Post hoc checks of the held-out collection (Section [-@sec:data]; Supplementary Table S17b) leave every C-H3 decision unchanged. With the duplicate pair merged (16 independent targets), SDLC was smaller than S-NCP on {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.merged/S-NCP/wins|int}} of 16 datasets (ratio {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.merged/S-NCP/geo_ratio|f3}}, Holm $p$ = {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.merged/S-NCP/p_holm|f3}}) and than BCCP on {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.merged/BCCP/wins|int}} (ratio {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.merged/BCCP/geo_ratio|f3}}). On the {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.n_continuous|int}} held-out datasets with continuous targets (at least 100 distinct values, no atom above 5%) it was smaller than S-NCP and S-HPD on all of them (ratios {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.continuous/S-NCP/geo_ratio|f3}} and {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.continuous/S-HPD/geo_ratio|f3}}), and the same held for the {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.n_nominal_tails|int}} datasets whose tail strata had their nominal mass. The small advantage over S-NCP is therefore a result for continuous targets; on discretised or atom-heavy targets the two scores were indistinguishable.

The secondary results repeated the trade-off seen on CTR23. SDLC covered {{cs.SDLC-LS.top1}} of the top 1%, against {{cs.S-NCP.top1}} for S-NCP (range over datasets {{J:statistics_review05.json|held-out.top1_SDLC/min|f2}}–{{J:statistics_review05.json|held-out.top1_SDLC/max|f2}}; top 1% defined as $y\ge q_{0.99}$ of the training targets, as everywhere in the main pipeline), and its worst coverage over deciles of $\hat\sigma(x)$ was {{cs.SDLC-LS.sigma_decile_worst}}, against {{cs.S-NCP.sigma_decile_worst}}. The price of stratification relative to DLS was {{conf.price}}. The pre-specified secondary analyses (Supplementary Table S16) were less favourable for the interval forms: SDLC-hull was smaller than BCCP-hull (ratio {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.hull/SDLC-hull vs BCCP-hull/geo_ratio|f3}}, Holm $p$ = {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.hull/SDLC-hull vs BCCP-hull/p_holm|f3}}) but not than S-NCP-hull (ratio {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.hull/SDLC-hull vs S-NCP-hull/geo_ratio|f3}}, {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.hull/SDLC-hull vs S-NCP-hull/wins|int}}/{{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.hull/SDLC-hull vs S-NCP-hull/losses|int}} datasets, $p$ = {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.hull/SDLC-hull vs S-NCP-hull/p_holm|f3}}). Post hoc, SDLC was equivalent to S-HPD within ±5% (TOST $p$ = {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.tost5/S-HPD/p_tost|f3}}), equivalence with S-NCP narrowly failed ($p$ = {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.tost5/S-NCP/p_tost|f3}}), and SDLC was reliably smaller than S-NCP on {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.reliability/S-NCP/reliably_smaller|int}} of {{conf.n_eval}} datasets and reliably larger on {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.reliability/S-NCP/reliably_larger|int}}. On the {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.n_skewed|int}} held-out targets with $|$skewness$|>1$, the advantage over S-NCP and S-HPD disappeared (ratios {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.skew_groups/skewed/S-NCP/geo_ratio|f3}} and {{J:statistics_secondary.json|confirmatory.skew_groups/skewed/S-HPD/geo_ratio|f3}}), while that over BCCP and S-CQR remained (Supplementary Table S17). Among scores that use $\hat\sigma$, the choice matters little for size; the clear gains are over BCCP and stratified CQR.

## Relevance-weighted coverage of the most extreme outcomes (RQ4) {#sec:rw-results}

Stratified calibration left the most extreme outcomes under-covered (top-1% coverage of SDLC {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/sdlc|f3}} on the held-out datasets). RW-DLC was evaluated under its own pre-specified plan (Section [-@sec:protocol]); Table [-@tbl:rw_conf] reports the held-out datasets ({{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/n_datasets|int}} datasets, {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/n_runs|int}} runs); Supplementary Table S18 lists the hypothesis tests.

* *RW-1 (validity).* The relevance-weighted coverage of RW-DLC was {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-1/mean|f3}} on average, equivalent to the nominal 0.90 within the pre-specified margin of 0.02 (TOST $p$ {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-1/p_tost|p}}), as Theorem 2 predicts.
* *RW-2 (marginal methods miss the weighted target).* RW-DLC exceeded every marginal method in relevance-weighted coverage, by {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-2/NCP/diff_mean|f3}} (NCP) to {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-2/DLS-M/diff_mean|f3}} (DLS) on average (Holm-adjusted $p\le$ {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-2/NCP/p_holm|p}} for all four comparisons).
* *RW-3 (extreme tail).* RW-DLC covered {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/rw|f3}} of the top 1% of outcomes, against {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/sdlc|f3}} for SDLC (mean difference {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/diff_mean|f3}}, 95% CI {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/ci/0|f3}}–{{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/ci/1|f3}}, higher on {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/wins|int}} of {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/n|int}} datasets with top-1% test points, one-sided $p$ = {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-3/p|p}}). The pre-specified definition ($y>q_{0.99}$) differs from that of the main pipeline ($y\ge q_{0.99}$) and leaves the group empty or nearly empty on targets whose maximum is an atom. With the main-pipeline definition and at least 10 test points per seed, RW-3 still held (RW-DLC {{J:statistics_review05.json|rw3_sensitivity/unified (y >= q99, >= 10 test points)/rw|f3}} against {{J:statistics_review05.json|rw3_sensitivity/unified (y >= q99, >= 10 test points)/sdlc|f3}}, higher on {{J:statistics_review05.json|rw3_sensitivity/unified (y >= q99, >= 10 test points)/wins|int}} of {{J:statistics_review05.json|rw3_sensitivity/unified (y >= q99, >= 10 test points)/n|int}} datasets, one-sided $p$ = {{J:statistics_review05.json|rw3_sensitivity/unified (y >= q99, >= 10 test points)/p|f3}}; post hoc), also with the duplicate pair merged (Supplementary Table S17b). The mean hides a wide range: RW-DLC covered between {{J:statistics_review05.json|rw3_sensitivity/unified (y >= q99, >= 10 test points)/rw_min|f2}} and {{J:statistics_review05.json|rw3_sensitivity/unified (y >= q99, >= 10 test points)/rw_max|f2}} of the top 1% across datasets.
* *RW-4 (score).* RW-DLC's sets were smaller than those of the two ablations that use relevance-weighted calibration with an unweighted score, RW-DLS (geometric-mean ratio {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-4/RW-DLS-U/geo_ratio|f3}}) and RW-NCP ({{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-4/RW-NCP-U/geo_ratio|f3}}), but after Holm correction neither difference reached the pre-specified threshold ($p$ = {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/RW-4/RW-DLS-U/p_holm|p}} for both), so RW-4 was not supported.

The guarantee has a price elsewhere. Because RW-DLC averages coverage over the target range, it spends less on the bulk: its marginal coverage was {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/marg_cov_rw_mean|f3}} (minimum over datasets {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/marg_cov_rw_min|f3}}) and its worst coverage over target deciles was lower than that of SDLC (mean difference {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/descriptive/SDLC/worst_diff|f3}}). Its sets were not reliably larger than SDLC's (geometric-mean ratio {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/descriptive/SDLC/size_geo_ratio|f3}}, 95% CI {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/descriptive/SDLC/size_geo_lo|f3}}–{{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/descriptive/SDLC/size_geo_hi|f3}}, descriptive). No test point had an infinite threshold; the smallest effective calibration sample size was {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/ess_U_min|int}} (median {{J:rw_statistics.json|confirmatory/ess_U_median|int}}). The milder relevance $\phi_H$ gave intermediate results. RW-DLC and SDLC thus answer different requirements: SDLC guarantees every stratum, and RW-DLC guarantees an average over the whole target range that reaches the outcomes beyond the strata (Fig. [-@fig:rw]).

## Target shift (RQ5) {#sec:shift}

Under the positive control, which re-weights whole strata, the stratum-wise coverage of every stratified method was unchanged, as Corollary 1(i) states (Table [-@tbl:shift], Fig. [-@fig:shift]). Their marginal coverage stayed at the nominal level ({{T:T8_shift|method=SDLC|pc_marginal|f3}} for SDLC), whereas that of the marginal methods fell to {{T:T8_shift|method=SCP|pc_marginal|f3}}–{{T:T8_shift|method=Mondrian-ŷ|pc_marginal|f3}}. Continuous tilts towards the upper tail also re-weight targets *within* strata, so Corollary 1(i) does not apply. Under a moderate tilt ($\gamma=1$) the stratified methods kept {{T:T8_shift|method=BCCP|up1_upper|f3}}–{{T:T8_shift|method=S-NCP|up1_upper|f3}} coverage of the upper stratum, against {{T:T8_shift|method=SCP|up1_upper|f3}}–{{T:T8_shift|method=DCP|up1_upper|f3}} for the marginal methods. Under a strong tilt ($\gamma=2$) every method lost coverage. The stratified methods lost less ({{T:T8_shift|method=S-CQR|up2_upper|f3}}–{{T:T8_shift|method=S-NCP|up2_upper|f3}}, against {{T:T8_shift|method=SCP|up2_upper|f3}}–{{T:T8_shift|method=DCP|up2_upper|f3}}). Among them, SDLC ({{T:T8_shift|method=SDLC|up2_upper|f3}}) was below S-NCP and S-HPD ({{T:T8_shift|method=S-NCP|up2_upper|f3}}), which is the same reallocation away from the most extreme outcomes seen above; Remark A.1 (Appendix A) shows why coverage is lowest near the outer end of a tail stratum, where the tilts move mass. The effective sample size of the $\gamma=2$ weights was small (median {{ess_up2_median}}, minimum {{ess_up2_min}}), but restricting the analysis to the {{shift_n_ess_ge_50}} datasets where it was at least 50 left the ordering unchanged.

## Tail alerts and decisions (RQ6)

Every stratified method guarantees an upper-tail alert recall of at least 0.9; their mean recall was {{alert.strat.recall.min}}–{{alert.strat.recall.max}}, and it was at least 0.9 on all {{n_datasets}} datasets (Table [-@tbl:alerts]). The price is a high alert rate. Among the stratified methods SDLC raised fewer alerts than BCCP ({{al.hi.BCCP.wins}}/{{al.hi.BCCP.losses}} datasets) and S-CQR, but more than S-NCP ({{al.hi.S-NCP.wins}}/{{al.hi.S-NCP.losses}}). The dedicated tail classifier TC-LS attains the guaranteed recall almost exactly (mean {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=TC-LS|recall|f3}}) and raised fewer alerts than SDLC on every dataset (alert-rate ratio {{al.hi.TC-LS.geo}}); for a pure yes/no decision it is the more efficient tool. Conformal selection [@jin2023selection] controls the false discovery rate instead and flagged few units, with high precision (mean {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=CS-BH-0.2|precision|f3}} at $q_{\mathrm{FDR}}=0.2$) but a mean recall of only {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=CS-BH-0.2|recall|f3}}; the point-prediction rule (Point) behaved similarly (recall {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=Point|recall|f3}}).

The preferable rule depends on the cost of a miss relative to a false alert. With equal costs, the rules that raise few alerts were cheapest: the point rule and conformal selection (expected cost {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=Point|cost_r1|f3}} and {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=CS-BH-0.2|cost_r1|f3}} per unit, against {{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=SDLC|cost_r1|f3}} for SDLC). When a miss costs ten times a false alert, SDLC's expected cost did not differ significantly from that of TC-LS (SDLC lower on {{al.hi.cost.TC-LS.wins}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets, $p$ = {{al.hi.cost.TC-LS.praw}}) and was lower than that of conformal selection on {{al.hi.cost.CS-BH-0.2.wins}} datasets. At a cost ratio of 20, SDLC was cheaper than TC-LS on {{al.hi.cost20.TC-LS.wins}} datasets ($p$ = {{al.hi.cost20.TC-LS.praw}}), because its recall exceeds the guaranteed 0.9 while TC-LS attains it almost exactly. Among the stratified scores, however, S-NCP gave the cheapest alerts: SDLC's expected cost was higher than S-NCP's on {{T:T13b_alert_tests|side=hi&comparison=cost r=10: SDLC vs S-NCP|losses|int}} of {{n_datasets}} datasets at $c=10$ and on {{T:T13b_alert_tests|side=hi&comparison=cost r=20: SDLC vs S-NCP|losses|int}} at $c=20$, because S-NCP raised fewer upper-tail alerts. These $p$-values are unadjusted and descriptive. Alerts read off *marginal* sets (SCP, CQR, HPD-split) were the cheapest on average at high cost ratios ({{T:T13_tail_alerts|side=hi&rule=HPD-split|cost_r20|f3}} for HPD-split at $c=20$). They had no recall guarantee, however, and their recall fell below 0.9 on {{alert.marg.below09.min}}–{{alert.marg.below09.max}} datasets. Fig. [-@fig:costs] shows cost curves for three valuation datasets.

## Sensitivity analyses (RQ7) {#sec:robustness}

*Miscoverage level and strata.* The conclusions held at $\alpha=0.05$ and $\alpha=0.2$ (SDLC worst stratum {{T:T5_alpha_sensitivity|alpha=0.05&method=SDLC|worst_stratum|f3}} and {{T:T5_alpha_sensitivity|alpha=0.2&method=SDLC|worst_stratum|f3}}; Supplementary Table S6). With decile strata the worst decile coverage of SDLC rose to {{T:T6_strata_schemes|scheme=S10&method=SDLC|worst_decile|f3}}, from {{T:T6_strata_schemes|scheme=S5&method=SDLC|worst_decile|f3}} with five strata, at a size increase from {{T:T6_strata_schemes|scheme=S5&method=SDLC|size_geo|f3}} to {{T:T6_strata_schemes|scheme=S10&method=SDLC|size_geo|f3}}. The floor variant (SDLC-floor, $\beta=0.2$) reached its weaker guarantee with smaller sets (worst stratum {{T:T5_alpha_sensitivity|alpha=0.1&method=SDLC-floor|worst_stratum|f3}}, size {{T:T5_alpha_sensitivity|alpha=0.1&method=SDLC-floor|size_geo|f3}}).

*Calibration-set size.* With a quarter of the calibration split (on average {{T:T7_calibration_size|cal_frac=0.25&method=SDLC|mean_n_cal|int}} points) stratum-wise validity was retained, but SDLC's sets grew from {{T:T7_calibration_size|cal_frac=1.0&method=SDLC|size_geo|f3}} to {{T:T7_calibration_size|cal_frac=0.25&method=SDLC|size_geo|f3}}, mainly because tail strata with fewer than $1/\alpha-1$ points have an infinite threshold ({{T:T7_calibration_size|cal_frac=0.25&method=SDLC|runs_with_inf|int}} runs, against {{T:T7_calibration_size|cal_frac=1.0&method=SDLC|runs_with_inf|int}} with the full split; Supplementary Table S8).

*Base learner.* With a two-layer perceptron ({{mlp.n_seeds}} seeds), marginal methods reached a worst-stratum coverage of {{T:T9_mlp_summary|method=SCP|worst_stratum|f3}}–{{T:T9_mlp_summary|method=DCP|worst_stratum|f3}} and stratified methods {{T:T9_mlp_summary|method=BCCP|worst_stratum|f3}}–{{T:T9_mlp_summary|method=SDLC|worst_stratum|f3}}. SDLC again gave the smallest stratified sets: smaller than BCCP on {{mlp.BCCP.wins}} of {{mlp.n_datasets}} datasets (ratio {{mlp.BCCP.geo}}), than S-NCP on {{mlp.S-NCP.wins}} (ratio {{mlp.S-NCP.geo}}, Holm $p$ {{mlp.S-NCP.p}}) and than S-HPD on {{mlp.S-HPD.wins}} (ratio {{mlp.S-HPD.geo}}).

*Imbalance-aware training.* With DenseWeight sample weights [@steininger2021denseweight], SCP's worst-stratum coverage rose from {{T:T9_dw_vs_lgb|method=SCP|worst_lgb|f3}} to {{T:T9_dw_vs_lgb|method=SCP|worst_dw|f3}}, but the shortfall remained large and its sets grew by a factor of {{T:T9_dw_vs_lgb|method=SCP|size_ratio_dw_vs_lgb|f2}}. Stratified calibration remained valid ({{T:T9_dw_summary|method=SDLC|worst_stratum|f3}} for SDLC), and SDLC again gave the smallest stratified sets (Holm $p$ {{dw.BCCP.p}}, {{dw.S-NCP.p}}, {{dw.S-CQR.p}} and {{dw.S-HPD.p}} against BCCP, S-NCP, S-CQR and S-HPD). Imbalance-aware learning and stratified calibration are complementary; the former does not deliver the guarantee.

*Grouped splits.* On three datasets with repeated units (sarcos, superconductivity, video_transcoding) we repeated the main comparison with splits that keep each unit in one part (5 seeds; Supplementary Table S15). On sarcos and superconductivity the results were close to those of random splits (SDLC/S-NCP size ratios {{J:metrics_v3.json|grouped/sarcos/sdlc_vs_sncp_grouped|f3}} and {{J:metrics_v3.json|grouped/superconductivity/sdlc_vs_sncp_grouped|f3}}). On video_transcoding, splitting by video increased every method's sets several-fold (SDLC: {{T:T16_grouped_splits|dataset=video_transcoding&method=SDLC|size_random|f3}} to {{T:T16_grouped_splits|dataset=video_transcoding&method=SDLC|size_grouped|f3}}), so random splits of this dataset measure interpolation between runs of known videos. Stratified methods still covered their worst stratum at {{J:metrics_v3.json|grouped/video_transcoding/strat_worst_grouped_min|f3}}–{{T:T16_grouped_splits|dataset=video_transcoding&method=S-NCP|worst_grouped|f3}}, against at most {{J:metrics_v3.json|grouped/video_transcoding/marg_worst_grouped_max|f3}} for marginal methods, but SDLC's sets were larger than BCCP's (ratio {{J:metrics_v3.json|grouped/video_transcoding/sdlc_vs_bccp_grouped|f3}}), and BCCP and S-CQR covered less than nominal marginally ({{T:T16_grouped_splits|dataset=video_transcoding&method=BCCP|coverage_grouped|f3}} and {{T:T16_grouped_splits|dataset=video_transcoding&method=S-CQR|coverage_grouped|f3}}), as expected when exchangeability holds between groups rather than rows. SDLC's size advantage thus depends on the density model transferring to unseen units, and grouped validation is advisable for such data.

*Computational cost.* Computing a prediction set took a median of {{J:metrics_v3.json|timing_us_per_point/SDLC|f1}} µs per test point for SDLC, against {{J:metrics_v3.json|timing_us_per_point/S-NCP|f2}} µs for S-NCP and {{J:metrics_v3.json|timing_us_per_point/BCCP|f2}} µs for BCCP (Supplementary Tables S13 and S14). SDLC is roughly two orders of magnitude slower than interval methods at prediction time, but the cost is negligible next to model fitting.

## Error analysis {#sec:error}

The price of stratum-wise validity (SDLC relative to DLS) rose with the skewness of the target (Spearman $\rho$ = {{ea.spearman_price_abs_skew.rho}}, $p$ {{ea.spearman_price_abs_skew.p}}) and fell with the calibration-set size ($\rho$ = {{ea.spearman_price_log_ncal.rho}}). It was largest for forest fires ({{T:T10_error_analysis|dataset=forest_fires|price|f2}}), whose atom at zero merges the two lower strata. Its upper-stratum coverage also includes automatic misses: whether the largest fire falls into the training split decides the domain $\mathcal{Y}_D$, and in three of ten seeds one to three test targets lay above it. Across all datasets, out-of-domain test targets were rare (at most {{J:statistics_review05.json|ctr23.ood_max|pct}} of a run's test points on CTR23 and {{J:statistics_review05.json|heldout.ood_max|pct}} on the held-out data; Supplementary Tables S1–S2). SDLC was larger than S-NCP on {{h3.S-NCP.losses}} datasets (socmob, cars, forest fires and QSAR fish toxicity; ratios up to {{T:T10_error_analysis|dataset=socmob|vs_sncp|f2}}), all small or with many ties, so that the scale and noise models rest on few distinct values. The density score helps most when the location model is accurate and the noise is heteroscedastic ($\rho$ = {{ea.spearman_vs_sncp_rmse.rho}} with the relative RMSE).
