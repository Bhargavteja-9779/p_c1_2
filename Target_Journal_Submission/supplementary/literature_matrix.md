**Bottom line for the authors.**
- **Do not claim** to introduce label-conditional or Y-stratified conformal calibration for regression. BCCP (2024/2026) and EOC/BFQR (2023) already do this, and Mondrian theory covers its validity.
- **The novelty can rest on:**
  - the optimal level-set score under per-stratum coverage, with a proof that generalises Sadinle et al. (2019);
  - finite-sample analysis, including small-stratum effects and data-driven stratum construction;
  - the precise label-shift statement;
  - systematic evidence on 31 datasets that even oracle X-conditional intervals under-cover rare targets, consistent with Barber et al. (2021) and Boruah (2026).
- **Required baselines:** BCCP and, where possible, BFQR.

**Content confirmations for existing keys.** Each was read on 2026-11-18 from the arXiv abstract or full text.
- podkopaev2021distribution: shows that label shift degrades the coverage of conformal prediction sets and the calibration of binned predictors in classification, and restores the guarantees by reweighting with label-shift weights estimated from unlabeled target data.
- gibbs2025conformal: recasts conditional coverage as coverage over a class of covariate shifts. For finite-dimensional classes it obtains exact finite-sample coverage over every shift in the class, including coverage within each group of a given collection of subgroups. For infinite-dimensional classes it quantifies the coverage error.
- hore2025local: RLCP randomises the localisation of weighted or localised CP. Its intervals are marginally valid and also satisfy a relaxed local-coverage guarantee and validity under covariate shift. The arXiv v5 title ends "…enables local guarantees"; the JRSSB title ends "…enables robust guarantees".
- sesia2021conformal: is indeed "Conformal prediction using conditional histograms" (NeurIPS 34:6304–6315, arXiv:2105.08747). CHR turns black-box conditional histograms into the shortest intervals with finite-sample marginal coverage, and gives asymptotic conditional coverage and optimal length when the model is consistent.
- izbicki2020flexible: introduces Dist-split and CD-split, conformal methods built on conditional density estimators. They give asymptotic conditional coverage without strong assumptions on the dependence of Y on X. CD-split gives optimal-size regions and local coverage through a data-driven partition of the feature space.
- izbicki2022cdsplit: proves that CD-split converges to the oracle highest-predictive-density set with local and asymptotic conditional validity. It introduces HPD-split, which needs less tuning and has the same guarantees.
- lei2013distribution: concerns prediction sets for a new observation Y ∈ R^d without covariates. The optimal (smallest) region is a density level set. A conformalised kernel-density region has distribution-free finite-sample coverage and near-optimal efficiency, with explicit rates (arXiv:1111.1418, Sec. 1.1–1.2).
- sadinle2019least: derives minimum-ambiguity (smallest expected size) set-valued classifiers under total or class-specific coverage. The oracle classifiers are level sets of the class-conditional probabilities.
- randahl2026bin: BCCP calibrates split conformal prediction within user-defined bins of the outcome, giving consistent coverage across those bins. It is applied to simulated data and to conflict-fatality forecasts, with slightly wider intervals than standard CP.
- ding2023class: clustered conformal prediction clusters classes with similar conformal-score distributions and calibrates per cluster, to improve class-conditional coverage when there are few labelled examples per class. The author list was checked against arXiv and NeurIPS.
- angelopoulos2024theoretical: arXiv v5 (2026). The arXiv comment says the book will be published by Cambridge University Press, but no Crossref record exists yet, so it stays cited as arXiv.

**Content checks for the new entries (C1-13, C1-15, C1-21, C1-42).** Details are in the rows above. Statements of note:
- Kiyani et al.: the length-optimal sets are density level sets by **Proposition 3.1**, but the constraints there are covariate-shift and covariate-group constraints, not label-defined strata.
- Barber et al. (2023): the total-variation bound is **Theorem 2** together with **Lemma 1**. For an i.i.d. calibration sample within a stratum and a shifted test point, Theorem 2 with Lemma 1 gives a gap of at most about 2·d_TV. The sharper d_TV(Q_k, P_k) follows from the remark in Sec. 4.5 that the gap is at most d_TV(Z, Z̃) for exchangeable Z̃, or directly from the definition of TV.
- Jin & Candès: conformal p-values with Benjamini–Hochberg control the FDR for selecting units with Y > c (Theorems 2.3 and 2.6). The clipped score is eq. (9).
- Wang et al.: Theorem 4.1 is the bin- and group-conditional finite-sample bound for fixed β_m.
- Kandinsky CP: the coverage is approximate and holds with high probability (Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.3).
- pintervals: `pinterval_ccp` clusters user-supplied Mondrian classes, and its coverage is approximate per cluster. It is the clustered conformal device of Ding et al. applied to regression classes, not to Y-bins.
